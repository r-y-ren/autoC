# Round 10 mechanics audit: causal upgrades and failure gates

Scope: static inspection of the official `kaggle-environments` 1.32.7 Kaggriculture engine, the frozen V9 source (`experiments/round9_market_slack.py`), and the isolated crop-slack V10 candidate (`experiments/round10_crop_slack.py`). This note proposes mechanisms, not measured wins. The V9 release is unchanged.

## Facts the policy must respect

- The engine executes physical actions before market orders, then town purchases, then crop decay and midnight refresh (`kaggriculture.py:894-959`). A `HARVEST` puts goods in a worker inventory; `SELL` reads **only the shed** (`:414-448`, `:652-666`). An overnight worker drop makes them saleable on the next day; a midday `DROP`/`PLACE` can make them saleable in the same turn. Goods harvested in the last action with no deposit and subsequent market order have zero terminal cash value.
- One-time crop yield can decay every second turn after `max_lifespan_step`; a plant with two consecutive unwatered nights becomes a weed. Ongoing crops have at most four scheduled productions, even if their currently held yield is harvested early (`:752-805`). This separates *saving expiring goods* from merely moving a harvest earlier.
- Animal midnight production is processed **before** today's CARE becomes a pending bonus (`:805-834`). CARE on day `d` cannot affect production at the refresh to day `d+1`; its earliest possible payout is a scheduled production at some day `n>d+1`. It also needs a fed production day and room under `max_held` (goose 4; cow/sheep 6). For example, CARE on day 28 cannot yield saleable extra product in a 30-day game.
- The physical action does not cost another hire when a genuinely idle hand performs it. An animal still needs wheat feed (its shadow cost is the purchase/foregone sale price); a new production project would incur seed/animal/land cost, daily Fibonacci hiring, feeding, transport, and warehouse capacity. V9's spare-hand layer does not hire anyone. The final score is **bank coins**, not goods on the tile or in inventory.
- Market orders run in ordered slots with simultaneous one-unit quotes against the rival (`:544-630`). At most ten slots execute; `SELL` requires shed stock. Selling a premium good may depress the rival's subsequent quote; at the $1 floor, the sale does not increase market inventory (`:652-666`). Town drain is deterministic for **currently unlocked** shops (`:728-750`); future shops are unknown and may repeat. Forecasts must use public current state and ranges for unknown rival stock, rather than treating a replayed rival action sequence as an adaptive opponent.
- An early harvest of a one-time crop changes the set of empty tiles. `_end_of_day` draws weeds on those tiles and then draws a new shop from the same RNG (`:836-889`). Equal initial seeds can therefore lead to different later shops. A paired win delta is still a real-game result, but its decomposition is not solely harvest economics.

## Proposal A — value spare-hand tasks at the first **saleable** unit

**Why:** V9's `_r9s_apply` at roughly lines 7760–7834 selects CARE with `price/(distance+1)`, harvest with `0.3*units*price/(distance+1)`, and fertilizer collection at headline price. Its CARE gate uses `yield_units+pending<5` for all animals, even though the caps and production dates differ. This can prefer an action that cannot become money by the terminal deadline over a nearby harvest that can. It can also refuse profitable care when an intervening guaranteed harvest would clear the held cap. These are engine-timing questions, not per-opponent thresholds.

**Insertion:** keep V9's `round9_slack_agent` and `_r9s_reservations`; replace only the candidate scoring and eligibility inside `_r9s_apply` in a new isolated source. Preserve its full-day no-native-work check, current-command `PASS` check, live tile checks, and the 85-unit combined stock guard. Give each task a conservative `cashable_units` and a first possible `sale_step`; reject if no executable deposit and sale before the last market action. For CARE, simulate the one-tile daily refresh with and without the extra care under visible tile state and parent planned FEED/HARVEST actions. Require a scheduled production `n>d+1`, fed production, free held capacity or a demonstrably earlier harvest, and time to harvest, deposit and sell. For HARVEST, count only goods that would decay, be capped out, or otherwise remain unavailable to a parent harvest, and count their actual shed arrival. For fertilizer, compare extra collection against the existing `_r85_reserve` input obligation and a realizable sale or avoided purchase. Score the **bank-margin** gain: own market revenue plus the rival revenue reduction caused by our added supply, minus genuine extra input expense. Bracket rival sale quantities using `0` and a public-observation bound; do not assume a private shed count. A simpler first ablation can use own cashable revenue only and test the incremental externality model separately.

**Hard constraints:** no extra hire; no change to farmer or a non-PASS worker; no override of any actual parent command; do not consume wheat or fertilizer reserved for parent work; no harvest before `first_yield_day`; no pre-maturity one-time harvest except one actually lost at tonight's unwatered/decay transition; no credit for goods that cannot reach a shed and be sold; no hypothetical shop draw. The returned action must remain within ten market slots and preserve all funded purchases. If future reactive parent actions cannot be certified from a static tape, abstain.

**What could fail:** the parent controller is reactive, so its native tape does not prove future feeding, collection or sales; a conservative certificate may fire rarely. Marginal price estimates may err when an opponent sells simultaneously. Prevent movement oscillation by retaining a target only while it remains best and still passes all live checks. Per-game telemetry should report attempted actions, executed harvests, deposited units, sold units and final unsold units, not just gross harvested units.

### Immediate audit of the existing crop-slack candidate

The candidate adds a mature/at-risk crop HARVEST scan to the V9 spare-hand loop and extends product crediting. Its use of `age>=max_yield_day` for one-time crops and `yield_units>=4`/near-lifespan for ongoing crops is a reasonable *screen*, and its threat-of-weeds exception matches the midnight rule. It still needs the following acceptance checks before a win claim:

1. **Credit discontinuity:** `_r9s_apply` resets `state['previous']` on day change before reconciling an hour-23 harvest. The hired hands disappear at midnight, so a hand's final-hour inventory delta also cannot be read from the next day's `private['inventories']`. Day-28 harvests are further excluded because the whole layer returns on day 29. Count goods that the parent sells anyway, then account for any remainder as unsold; do not call each new HARVEST a revenue gain.
2. **Sale liveness:** the layer checks `if not free: return action` before applying accumulated `state['credit']` to the market. A parent action using every worker can delay the extra sale even after goods reach the shed. Newly appended extra sales appear *after* V9's `round9_final_market_agent` reorders the queue, and an existing ten-slot list can contain an empty slot yet block `append`. Reconcile and sell independently of whether a worker is free; insert only into a demonstrably executable slot, respecting fixed purchase timing.
3. **Input reservation:** `WHEAT` is added to credit tracking but deliberately omitted from the extra SELL list. That avoids accidentally selling feed; it also means new wheat harvests need an explicit test that they reduce future `BUY_PRODUCT WHEAT`, avoid lost feed, or are sold by the parent. Other crop credits may be consumed by ordinary parent sales, so do not double count them.
4. **Production timing and worker conflicts:** for one-time crops, harvesting at nominal maximum day can be beneficial, but a **later WATER on that same day can still add one or two units**. `_r9s_reservations` currently reserves only CARE/HARVEST/COLLECT_FERTILIZER and does not reserve a crop tile with a current or later parent WATER/FERTILIZE. An earlier spare worker can remove that crop and turn the parent's maintenance into a no-op. Reserve future WATER/FERTILIZE and inspect current actions in actor execution order before harvesting. Harvesting before max day only to avoid a predicted weed is safe if the crop is already at `first_yield_day` and will truly die tonight. For ongoing crops, `yield_units==4` is a cap on held units, not proof another production remains. Inspect `max_lifespan_step` and the production count before claiming cleared capacity creates new output. Distinguish saved current yield from created future yield.
5. **Market and RNG:** added crop sales can drive down own later quotes and the opponent's quotes; early one-time harvest can change weed/shop RNG. Audit final shop sequence per paired game, and record physical harvest, delivery, executable sale and terminal bank separately. If shops diverge, report that fact and retain the pair in the actual-game result while avoiding a pure-production causal claim.

## Proposal B — close the market queue after spare-hand sales, with a public-state sale-timing gate

**Why:** V9 calls `round9_final_market_agent` first, then `_r9s_apply` appends or enlarges a credited `SELL`. Thus the last market transformation does **not** see that new sale. The existing `_ADV_LOOK=4` can move a planned sale forward even when visible shops are draining that good quickly enough that waiting would raise the price. Both effects depend on current inventory and legal order execution, not opponent identity.

**Insertion:** in a new wrapper immediately outside `round9_slack_agent`, first simulate the final queue on own current projected shed and bank under the official ten-slot order. Fill a genuine no-op slot for a credited sale only if it leaves every earlier funded `HIRE`, `BUY_LAND`, `BUY_SEED`, `BUY_ANIMAL`, and `BUY_PRODUCT` executable and does not interfere with a same-item wheat/fertilizer round trip. Then rerun only the bounded contiguous SELL permutation evaluator (`_v44y_reorder`) on the resulting *final* queue. For timing, introduce a separate ablation around `_adv_apply`: compare selling available unreserved cash goods now against the parent's next planned sale within four turns. Compute current per-unit quote and future quotes from known town consumption plus a bounded rival-flow scenario derived from public market inventory changes, visible ripe tiles and animal structures. Advance only when the lower bound on **bank-margin** gain is positive; otherwise leave the parent plan. Future unknown shops contribute no guaranteed drain. Avoid exact account, name, seed or replay identifiers.

**Hard constraints:** use goods actually in the shed or deposited by this turn's physical commands; never increase a SELL beyond executable stock after prior sales; do not sell wheat needed for feeding or fertilizer reserved for future work; at most ten orders; preserve protected BUY_PRODUCT slots and the opening's cash-dependent purchases; use engine price parameters from the observation when overridden. The two subchanges should be evaluated separately, because a queue fix and timing model can interact.

**What could fail:** a clone best-response objective may help against a mirror while harming diverse opponents. V9 also inherits `CT_TABLE` at roughly lines 4070–4100, which maps two exact early `(rival money, wheat inventory)` signatures to counters, and clone-gated sale pre-emption around lines 5767–5875. Those are lineage-dependent. Ablate `CT_TABLE` separately on a mixed pool; a victory over fixed or reconstructed top-player tapes is not evidence against private adaptive agents. For sale timing, a brief public-flow window can be noisy and cannot reveal the opponent's shed, so only robust positive interventions should fire.

## Evaluation gate for either proposal

Run a small diagnostic first to verify action legality, delivered-versus-sold goods and whether the branch fires. Then use paired seeds and both seats versus multiple **independent** policy families, with unchanged frozen V9 as baseline. Include a strong recent public opponent and a self-play/mirror case but do not let the mirror dominate selection. Report per-family wins, bank margin, invalid actions, ending unsold stock, execution timing, and fraction of paired worlds whose shop paths diverge. Freeze a single winning candidate before using previously untouched worlds; retain any losing ablation and do not retune on confirmation worlds. A temporary lead in the leaderboard or a small replay sample is insufficient proof of a 3000+ agent.
