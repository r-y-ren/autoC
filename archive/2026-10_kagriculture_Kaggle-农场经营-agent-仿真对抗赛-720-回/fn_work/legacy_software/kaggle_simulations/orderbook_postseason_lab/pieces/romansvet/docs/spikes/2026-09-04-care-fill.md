# CARE_FILL: fill the evening PASS turns with CARE

`plan.CARE_FILL_ON`, default **False**. OFF the plan is byte-identical to the
tree this was cut into (`tests/test_care_fill.py`'s
`test_off_plan_is_byte_identical_to_the_pre_switch_planner`, pinned on
`test_route_early.PIN`).

## 1. The engine's care rule, exactly

Ground truth is `kaggle_environments==1.32.7`,
`kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py`
(sha256 `bc8a548…`, `vendor/engine.lock.json`).

*The op* — `_apply_unit_op`, `kaggriculture.py:524-530`:

```python
if op == "CARE":
    if not (isinstance(tile, dict) and "animal" in tile):   # :525
        return
    if tile["cared_today"]:                                 # :527
        return
    tile["cared_today"] = True                              # :529
```

So `OP_CARE` is a **day flag on an animal tile**, idempotent inside the day.
The engine's cap is one effective CARE per animal per day; a second is a turn
spent on a no-op. It costs no input, needs no PICKUP and moves nothing into
the acting unit's inventory.

*The payout* — `_daily_refresh_animals`, `kaggriculture.py:805-833`, run once
per end of day with `next_day = day + 1`:

```python
if tile["fed_today"]: tile["consecutive_unfed"] = 0         # :814
else:                 tile["consecutive_unfed"] += 1        # :816
if tile["consecutive_unfed"] >= 2: <animal escapes>         # :817-819
days_since_first = next_day - tile["placed_day"] - a["first_yield_day"]
if days_since_first >= 0 and days_since_first % a["interval"] == 0:
    bonus = tile.pop("pending_care_bonus", 0) if tile["fed_today"] else 0  # :826
    tile["yield_units"] = min(a["max_held"], tile["yield_units"] + 1 + bonus)  # :827
    tile["pending_care_bonus"] = 0                          # :828
if tile["cared_today"] and tile["fed_today"]:               # :829
    tile["pending_care_bonus"] += 1                         # :830
```

Read out as four facts:

1. A CARE banks a bonus **only on a day the animal is also fed** (`:829`). A
   CARE on an animal nothing feeds is not a cheap care, it is a wasted turn.
2. The bank pays at the **next production night**, and only if the animal is
   fed *that* night too (`:826`); an unfed fire wipes it without paying
   (`:828` runs either way).
3. The payout is capped by `max_held` (`:827`) — GOOSE 4, COW 6, SHEEP 6
   (`kaggriculture.py:20-22`, `spec.py:82-86`).
4. Production fires whether or not the animal ate. **A feed buys survival,
   not units; a care buys units, and only on a fed animal.** Neither half
   pays alone.

Worked, for a cow (`interval` 2, base 1 unit a fire): cared and fed every day,
the bank runs 0 → 1 (fire night, consumed then re-banked) → 2 → cashed as
`1 + 2 = 3` milk on the next fire. **3 milk per 2 days against the bare 1** —
the loss autopsy's "a fed+cared cow yields 3 milk per 2 days", and the tail
block's `(1 + interval)/interval` in units-a-day terms.

### The sim already agrees

`src/kagg3/sim/eod.py:94-127` (`refresh_animals`) is the same program, term
for term: `bonus = xp.where(fed, st.t_bank, 0)` (`:112`, the engine's `:826`),
`new_yield = where(fires, minimum(held, t_yield + 1 + bonus), t_yield)`
(`:113`, `:827`), `bank = where(fires, 0, t_bank)` then
`where(alive & (t_cared == 1) & fed, bank + 1, bank)` (`:114-115`, `:828-830`),
and `fires` carries `alive` so an escaped animal produces nothing (`:817-819`).
`src/kagg3/sim/units.py:172` gates `do_care` on `t_cared[p][tile] == 0`, which
is the engine's `:527`. **No change was needed in `src/kagg3/sim/`.**

## 2. Where the 604 evening PASSes come from

Evidence: autopsy of Kaggle loss ep 105393487 (our sub 56006782 vs Jithendra
Maddala's 55920227, −16,583). On the same 8 COW + 4 SHEEP herd the opponent
issued **967 CARE ops to our 230** and out-earned us by 11.1k of milk and 6.4k
of wool. 1,309 of our unit-turns are PASS (20.3 % of our unit actions against
their 4.8 %); **604 of them sit in hours 18-23**.

Those 604 are structural, and `_routes` is where they are made:

* `route_turns(h) = TPD - ROUTE_BASE` (`plan.py:2370-2390`), and the caller
  lays each unit's window down as `start_u = route_base + lead`,
  `walk_u = turn_budget - lead` (`plan.py:5362-5364`) — so a unit's window
  ends exactly at turn 23. There is no "after the route" region; the evening
  is *inside* the window.
* `_routes` cuts each block at the largest rank whose whole chain the budget
  reaches (`_cut`, `plan.py:5946-5960`), and the decode then writes
  `working = active & (rr < block_end) & (rr < bud - l_u)`
  (`plan.py:6140`). Every turn from `block_end` to the end of the window is
  `O.OP_PASS`: **the turns the next tile did not fit into.**

That is the tail. `TAIL_FILL_ON`'s block already prices it at 555 idle
unit-turns a game, 41 % of all our idle, a mean 2.5 turns long — and the mean
is the wrong statistic here. Day 5 of that episode has five of ten units
PASSing *every hour of the day* (131 of 231 ops), and days 7-9 run 42-48 %
PASS. The long tails are where the 604 live.

`TAIL_CARE_ON` (on since 2026-09-03) already walks **one** hop of that tail
(`TAIL_CARE_HOPS = 1`), sized against a 2.5-turn tail and the census's 1.5
fed-but-uncared animal-days a day. The gap this switch attacks is the hop
count, not the mechanism: the planner does emit `OP_CARE`, 230 times a game.

## 3. What ON changes

One block in `_routes`, after `TAIL_CARE_ON`'s hop and `TAIL_FILL_ON`'s, on
the same tail cursor (`f_t0`, `f_left`, `fx0`, `fy0`): up to `CARE_FILL_HOPS`
(4) further hops, each a walk to the nearest eligible animal plus **one CARE
and nothing else**.

Eligibility is the engine's rule and nothing else
(`_plan_and_stats`, `plan.py:5165-5192` and `:5328-5335`; the hops themselves at `:6267-6304`):

| gate | expression | engine |
| --- | --- | --- |
| uncared | `t_cared == 0` and not `cf_plan_care & admitted` | `:527` |
| fed tonight | `t_water == 1` or `want_feed & admitted` | `:829` |
| headroom | `where(fires_tonight, 0, t_bank) + 2 <= max_held` | `:827` |
| horizon | `next_fire_after(day) <= VAL.pay_day()` | the unit must still sell |

and the one-a-day cap is `cf_free`, struck for this unit's later hops and for
every later unit — and struck by `TAIL_CARE_ON`'s hop too, so no two tails
land on one tile's single daily CARE.

**No displacement, structurally.** Every hop writes from `f_t0`, which starts
at `block_end + ins` (`ins` being whatever a `MELON_OPEN` / `BANK_BEFORE_LOT`
excursion already charged), and fires only where `active` — a unit `_routes`
gave a block. So it writes into turns that were `O.OP_PASS` and into no
others. It changes no admission, no block cut, no PICKUP row, and **no market
row**: `HIRE_ROW_ON`'s `n_hire_row` counts units that act, and `active`
already means the unit acts. Off on a DROP day, whose tail is the return leg.
`tests/test_care_fill.py::test_on_leaves_every_turn_the_route_owns_alone`
holds all of that on fourteen seeded boards plus the four firing boards.

**Why a CARE filler and not a second `TAIL_FILL_ON`.** That switch's own
post-mortem is the reason it is off: an idle-turn filler that puts *new tiles*
to work plants a marginal tile that sells into our own curve, and it costs
+70 % of planner wall clock. A CARE is the opposite trade — the animal is
already owned, already fed, already routed, and the turn was going to PASS, so
the marginal unit displaces nothing. The one thing it *does* add to the market
is the milk and wool themselves, into a stream `CARE_HOLD_ON`'s report
(−238 coins, t −1.86) shows we are already glutting. That is why the cap and
the headroom test are engine-exact rather than approximated, and why the fed
gate is a hard requirement and not a preference.

## 4. The in-sim check

`scripts/care_fill_scenario.py` — a seeded four-day scenario (`SEED = 4041`,
days 10-13) on 8 COW + 4 SHEEP placed on day 1, one night hungry, 300 wheat in
the shed, six hands, run through `sim/rollout.run_day` with `do_eod=False` and
`sim/eod.end_of_day` split out so per-day animal production is read straight
off the `t_yield` delta the eod writes.

The market is set to the census's own crossover: `mkt_inv` WHEAT 9000
(quote 57), MILK 10060 (34), WOOL 10040 (107). `care_pays`
(`price[product] > price[WHEAT]`) therefore **refuses every cow and admits
every sheep** — the eight cows are fed for survival and the day will not care
them, which is the fed-but-uncared animal-day the autopsy counts, reproduced.

Measured, ON against OFF on the identical scenario:

| window | | CARE ops | milk+wool banked | coins |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| days 10-13 | OFF | 13 | 23 | 15,872 |
| days 10-13 | ON | 18 | 23 | 15,872 |
| days 10-15 | OFF | 33 | 41 | 13,261 |
| days 10-15 | ON | 39 | 42 | 13,261 |

**Over the four days asked for: N = 5 extra CARE ops, M = 0 extra milk/wool
units.** M is zero for a reason worth writing down rather than tuning away:
the extra cares land on day 12, the last day in the window on which the herd
is fed, and `:830` banks them at that night's eod — *after* `:826-827` has
already paid day 12's fire. A cow's next fire is day 14, outside the window.
Six days is the shortest window in which a care taken on day 12 can cash, and
the second pair of rows is that window.

Over six days it is **N = 6 extra CARE ops, M = 1 extra milk unit**, and the
coins do not move: at a milk quote of 34 the unit sits in the shed at the end
of the window rather than being sold. That is a small number and it is
supposed to be read as one -- a single scenario at one seed is a mechanism
check, not a measurement. What it establishes is that the switch fires inside
the real rollout, that the ops it adds are ops the engine accepts (they are
not no-ops on an already-cared tile), and that the unit they bank arrives
through `refresh_animals`' own `:826-827` and not through anything the planner
tells itself. Section 6 is what would actually price it.

The hunger/fire cadence is what makes the scenario work at all, and it is the
engine's: `consecutive_unfed >= 2` (`:817`) means survival only forces a feed
every second day, so the herd is fed on days 10, 12, 14 — and a cow placed on
day 1 fires at the eod of days 10, 12, 14 too (`days_since_first = next_day -
1 - 8`, even). Fed night and fire night coincide, which is the only way
`:826`'s `bonus` is ever read as non-zero.

## 5. What still PASSes after the fill, and why

The switch is deliberately not a general idle-turn filler, so most of the
1,309 stay:

1. **The morning wait.** 119 of the census's day-0-to-12 PASSes are in hours
   0-3 — the turn-1 BUY-row wait and the per-unit `lead` (pickup turns, or
   the land lead). Those turns are before `start_u`; the tail cursor never
   reaches them, and a unit that steps before the hire row resolves
   re-scatters every hand `_spawn_hand` places [LAW]. `ROUTE_SPLIT_ON` owns
   that region and already buys back what it can.
2. **Units `_routes` gave no block at all.** `f_can = active & (bud > 0)`, so
   an inactive unit stays all-PASS. That is not an oversight: making one act
   would move `HIRE_ROW_ON`'s `n_hire_row`, i.e. the market row, which this
   switch may not touch. The autopsy's day 5 — five of ten units idle all
   day, on a crew the hire enumeration sized against no work — is a *hiring*
   defect and belongs to the crew-ramp branch.
3. **Unfed animals.** The dominant residue. `want_feed` needs `must_feed`,
   `bank_feed` or `care_ok`, and `care_ok` carries `care_pays` — so on the
   days the product quote is under the wheat quote the day feeds only what is
   starving, and a CARE on the rest banks nothing (`:829`). `TAIL_CARE_ON`'s
   own report already found this: "what the day leaves undone is mostly
   *unfed* animals, and a tail FEED needs wheat the unit is carrying". This
   switch does not add a FEED and so cannot reach them. The lever there is
   `CARE_HOLD_ON` (the forward quote) or the feed rule, not the tail.
4. **Capped and out-of-horizon animals.** A bank with no room under
   `max_held` and a fire past `VAL.pay_day()` both pay zero, so their turns
   are correctly left as PASS.
5. **Tails shorter than the walk.** A hop costs `dist + 1`; a two-turn tail
   three tiles from the nearest eligible animal buys nothing and is refused.
6. **Everything on a day the quote is kind.** This is the finding the
   scenario forced out and it bounds the whole switch: `care_pays` is
   `price[product] > price[WHEAT]`, and on the price table wheat tops out at
   125 (`spec.PRICE_BASE` 25, `T` 400, `sqrt` below) against milk 160 and
   wool 200 at `MARKET_I0`. So the day already cares every cow and sheep it
   feeds *unless we have crashed our own milk or wool quote by selling into
   it* -- milk goes 160 → 34 within sixty units of surplus. The
   fed-but-uncared animal-day is therefore a **mid-and-late-season**
   population, exactly where the census puts it (43.1 a game, starting day
   15, peaking day 26) and exactly where `CARE_HOLD_ON`'s trough argument
   lives. Geese are the exception at any time of season: egg base 50 sits
   under a scarce wheat quote.

kagg2 + the class-A tapes, both seed bases) is the owner's to run, and the
