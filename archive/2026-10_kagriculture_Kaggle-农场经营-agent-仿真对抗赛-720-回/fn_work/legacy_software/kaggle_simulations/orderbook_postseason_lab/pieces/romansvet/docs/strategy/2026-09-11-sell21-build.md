## Why turn 21 and not 19, 20, 22 or 23

Engine `/root/.cache/uv/archive-v0/tWqbmruwqm--ie1i/kaggle_environments/envs/kaggriculture/kaggriculture.py`:
`interpreter` (894) runs unit ops → `_process_market` (941) → `_town_consume` (942), so a
turn's market is quoted **before** that turn's town tick. `_town_consume` (728) fires on
`step % townShopSellInterval == 0` with the interval 4 (733, 736) and `turnsPerDay = 24`
(906), so `step % 4 == turn % 4`: shops tick on turns **0, 4, 8, 12, 16, 20**. Consumption
removes inventory and `market_price` (192) rises as inventory falls, so the last free lift of
the day is turn 20's tick and the earliest market phase that sees it is **turn 21's**.

Our own `core/projector.py:174-184` `ticks_before(t) = ceil(t/4)` agrees, measured:
`ticks_before(18) = (5,1)`, `(20) = (5,1)`, `(21) = (6,1)`, `(22) = (6,1)`.
21 is the **first** turn in the six-tick window; 22 and 23 buy no extra tick.

Still inside the day: `_end_of_day` fires at `(step+1) % 24 == 0` (945-946), after turn 23.
On day 29 turn 21 is step `29·24 + 21 = 717`, inside the last executed step 718
(`step >= episodeSteps − 2`, 960-963, `episodeSteps = 720`).

The 24-hour sweep prices the alternatives: h21 **+911**/game, h22 +250, h23 +190, h19 +34,
h20 −525, h0 −4,777 (timing-prize Result 2).

**Convention check.** `S/timing/price_timing.py:72,124` takes `h` from the *pre-turn*
observation (`prev_obs0['hour']`), so the timing doc's `h` **is** our turn index — its `h18`
is `SELL_TURNS[-1] == 18`. (Comments inside `core/ops.py` and `core/plan.py` sometimes use
the *recorded* hour `t+1`; that is a different convention and not the one used here.)

## Why an environment variable

`sim/rollout.py` freezes `_LOT_TURNS`, `_SELL_ONLY_TURNS` and `MARKET_TURNS` at **its own
import** (54-66). A `plan.*` attribute flipped after import — which is exactly what
`S/drainpin/on2b.py:25-33` and `S/simscreen/screen.py:70-92` do with the switch string —
would move the planner's row and **not** the simulator's market resolution: the ES would
train against a lot that never resolves. `ops.py` reads the env var before any of it.

Operational consequence: **`KAGG3_SELL21` must never be put in the switch string.**
`on2b.py` asserts `hasattr(plan, name)` and would raise. It is an environment prefix.

### Why — the missing term is the opponent's purse

Splitting the paired delta into the two seats:

| set | Δ **our** money | Δ **their** money | Δ margin |
|---|---|---|---|
| LIVE-C hold-out (n 120) | **+249** (sd 1,014, t +2.69, 61/120 positive) | **+1,383** (sd 906, **t +16.73**, 116/120 positive) | −1,134 |
| TOPB2 (n 40) | **+560** (sd 1,376, t +2.58, 30/40) | **+1,011** (sd 1,101, t +5.81, 38/40) | −451 |

**Our own revenue goes up, in the predicted direction and roughly the predicted size.** The
first-order sweep was not wrong about our purse. What it left out, by its own admission
("the *opponent's* revenue change is ignored entirely", timing-prize §Method), is that our
531 units/game at turn 18 were **depressing the price the opponent sells into at turn 18**.
Vacate that turn and they get an undepressed market: they gain **5.5×** what we gain.

This is the two-purse signature the campaign has seen six times before
(`counterfactuals-overstate`): a lever that prices the marginal unit and not the
displacement. Sell-lot 3 at turn 18 is **denial**, and the denial is worth more than the
price window.

## Open risks

1. **The switch is not one change, it is two.** `plan.DROP_ON = True` ships, so the DROP-day
   turn budget is live and day 29's crew gains **three route turns** (narrow 17→20, wide
   16→19) because the DROP that feeds lot 3 may now land as late as turn 21. That is the
   honest consequence of a later deadline, but the engine legs measure the price window *and*
   the longer day-29 route together. If the legs come back positive, a follow-up arm that
   pins `drop_turns` at 19 would separate the two.
2. **The +911 is a level uplift, not a loss-killer.** The timing doc's own judgement:
   h18→h21 is +1,096 on the 48 losses and +726 on the 48 wins, a ~370-coin loss/win
   difference against a 5,186 mean loss margin. At the 298 pts/logit calibration it is worth
   roughly 0.2 pt of win rate. Nothing here closes the 2200-band clone gap.
3. **First-order pricing.** The sweep does not interleave our units with the opponent's
   inside the alternative turn, and the opponent is closed-loop and would react. The engine
   legs are the only arbiter; the sim screen below is the pre-screen, not the verdict.
4. **Product mix.** The sweep says WOOL (+523) and MILK (+373) carry the whole prize and that
   WHEAT, CARROT and FERTILIZER are *negative* at h21. This switch moves **all nine
   products**' lot 3, so it is strictly coarser than the recommendation
   ("take h18 → h21 for WOOL/MILK/TOMATO in d15-29"). A per-product or day-banded variant is
   the obvious next cut if the flat move screens level.
5. **Trained-in turn.** Candidate B's theta was trained with lot 3 at turn 18; its `press`
   and `hold` genes price a five-tick market. Running B under SELL21 is an *off-policy* read.
   A real verdict needs an ES arm trained with `KAGG3_SELL21=1` in the environment
   (`es/train.py` propagates it: the pinned-evaluator subprocess uses
   `dict(os.environ, ...)` at train.py:2443, and a plain launch inherits the parent env).
6. **Switch-string trap.** `KAGG3_SELL21` cannot go in the `"..."` switch string — it is an
   env prefix. A leg that puts it there dies on `assert hasattr(plan, name)`; a leg that
   forgets it silently runs the OFF build and looks perfectly level.
7. `S/simscreen/screen.py` hard-codes the `arms-next` worktree. This build did **not** edit
   it — `S/sell21/screen_tree.py` is a copy with `WORKTREE` read from `$KAGG3_SCREEN_TREE`.
   The judge's own screen is untouched.

## Verdict from this build (sim only; engine legs are a later dispatch)

The switch is **built, inert OFF, live ON, and free in throughput** — the deliverable is
sound. But at candidate B's theta the sim screen refutes the ledger's sign: **−1,134/game on
120 LIVE-C hold-out boards (t −8.87) and −451 on 40 TOPB2 boards**, because vacating turn 18
hands the opponent more than the later window pays us.

Two readings stay open, and the engine legs decide between them:

1. **The lever is dead** (most likely). The denial term dominates the price term, the same
   way `FERT_VOLUME` and `OPP_FRONTRUN` died: screen win rates do not transfer, denial does.
   In that case SELL21 joins the sell-timing family's closed list and the ledger's "+911 free
   move" is retracted as an ignored-opponent artefact.
2. **It is off-policy**. B's `press`/`hold` genes were trained against a five-tick market at
   turn 18 and are now quoting into a six-tick one, so the reservation is mispriced and lot 3
   under-sells. An ES arm trained with `KAGG3_SELL21=1` would be the fair test — but note
   that the ES cannot learn its way out of the denial term, only out of the mispricing, and
   the denial term is 5.5× the gain.

A cheap discriminator before spending a GPU: the same screen at a theta that never saw turn
18 — or a variant that keeps a *token* lot at turn 18 (splitting lot 3 rather than moving it,
timing-prize variant (c), +247/+332 first order) so the denial is preserved and only the
marginal units move late. Variant (c) is the only member of the family this measurement has
not refuted.

Recommendation: **run the two engine legs as dispatched** (they are cheap and the sim is the
pre-screen, not the verdict), but do not stage an ES arm on SELL21 unless they contradict the
screen.
