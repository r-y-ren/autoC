# PER-BLOCK SNR of the E3 gradient at candidate B — where the 4 % lives, and whether it is a slope

Agent box, 2026-09-11, 45 min, CPU only, read-only on `S/onestep`, `S/onestep_b`, `S/ties`.
Code `S/snr/blocksnr.py`, results `S/snr/blocksnr.json` + `S/snr/blocksnr.log` + `S/snr/budget.txt`.

## 0. The instrument — per-pair recovery, which `sweep_b` did not save but did not destroy

`sweep_b.py` saves only `g`. But `popcurve.one_draw` computes
`g = (adv[:P] - adv[P:]) @ eps / (pop*sigma)`, so **g lies exactly in the row space of the P×n
perturbation matrix**, and `eps = jax.random.normal(PRNGKey(9001), (P, n))` is a pure function of
the seed. With P (256 / 1024) ≪ d_live (5,997) the per-pair weights are recovered by least squares:
measured reconstruction residual `‖E^T ŵ − g‖/‖g‖ = 8.9e-08` (P512) and `1.2e-07` (P2048) — float32
exact. Every pair-level statistic §0 wanted is therefore available.

**Prefix relation verified byte-exact**: `jax.random.normal(PRNGKey(9001),(1024,6789))[:256]` is
`array_equal` to `...(256,6789)` (max abs diff 0.0). Threefry fills a flat counter-indexed stream and
reshapes, and both sizes are even, so the p2048 draw really does contain the p512 draw's 256 pairs.
Live mask `PO.live_mask()` = **5,997** of 6,789; off-mask `|g|` max is exactly `0.0` in all six files.

## 1. §0 reproduced, and two corrections to it

| σ | ‖g‖ P512 | ‖g‖ P2048 | ratio | cos(512, **alg**-rem) = §0 | cos(512, **PURE** disjoint) | boot sd | sign-flip null sd |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.01 | 105.900 | 52.994 | 1.998 | **+0.0386** | **+0.0411** | 0.0120 | 0.01329 |
| 0.02 | 34.239 | 16.891 | 2.027 | **+0.0236** | **+0.0276** | 0.0108 | 0.01288 |
| 0.04 | 23.184 | 11.631 | 1.993 | **+0.0159** | **+0.0325** | 0.0100 | 0.01362 |

§0's column reproduces **to four digits**. The *pure* column is the truly disjoint remainder — pairs
256..1023 of the big draw only, with the shared-256 residual removed exactly rather than bounded —
and it is higher at every σ, confirming §0's "lower bound, not an upward bias" caveat.

The **error bar is real, not analytic**: 400 pair-level sign flips give a null of mean ≈ 0 and sd
0.0129-0.0136, against the analytic `1/√5997 = 0.01291` — the analytic null is validated, so
σ 0.01 is **3.1 σ** and σ 0.02/0.04 are 2.1 σ / 2.4 σ. A 400-draw pair bootstrap (resample with
replacement) gives sd ≈ 0.010-0.012; its percentile CI is biased low (duplicate pairs inflate the
noise part of ‖g‖) so it is quoted as a scale check only.

**Correction 1 — the ρ that §0 matched is the wrong ρ.** `P/(P+d) = 256/6253 = 0.0409` is the
noiseless cosine between two draws of **equal** P. The §0 comparison is 256 pairs against 768, whose
noiseless value is `√(P₁/(P₁+d+1))·√(P₂/(P₂+d+1)) = 0.0682`. Measured / predicted is
**0.60 / 0.41 / 0.48**, not 1.0. The headline "at its theoretical dimension-limited SNR with
essentially no evaluation noise left after CRN" is therefore **too strong**: solving for the
inflation `1+ν` that makes the prediction match gives an *effective* dimension
**D = 10,285 / 15,562 / 13,123** against d = 5,997, i.e. evaluation noise still contributes
0.7-1.6 × the dimensional noise. The estimator is dimension-limited *and* noise-limited, roughly
half and half.

**Correction 2 — "the signal fraction falls with σ" does not survive.** That reading came from the
algebraic remainder, whose shared-pair residual grows with σ. The pure values are
0.0411 / 0.0276 / 0.0325 with boot sd ≈ 0.011: **three values inside one error bar**. The §67 tie
story may still be true; this statistic does not evidence it.

## 2. Per-block disjoint cosine (full tables in `S/snr/blocksnr.log`)

`cos_b` = cosine of the two disjoint draws restricted to block b's live coords. Two references:
`ρ_b(dis)` = the same noiseless 256×768 formula at `d_b` — the value reached **only if the block
held all the signal** — and the sign-flip null sd measured per block. Blocks with `d_b ≤ 4` are
listed but carry almost no information (`d_b = 1` forces `cos = ±1`); read `z` only for `d_b ≥ 16`.

**Headline: no block survives multiplicity.** 30 blocks × 3 σ = 90 tests; the largest |z| anywhere is
2.81 (`g1`, σ 0.01), whose Bonferroni p is 0.45. Every block is individually consistent with the null.
What the table *can* say is which blocks are positive in both σ cells — and even that is weak evidence,
because **the σ cells are not independent replicates**: `sweep_b` passes the same `PRNGKey(9001)` at
every σ, so all three share one `eps` matrix and hence most of their directional-sampling noise.

**Energy is not concentrated anywhere.** The concentration ratio (block's share of ‖g‖² ÷ its share of
coordinates) is 1.00 ± 0.05 for every block with `d_b ≥ 64` at all three σ — exactly what an
isotropic 96 %-noise draw looks like. Any concentration claim rests on the *cosine*, not on energy.

## 3. Signal budget (`S/snr/budget.txt`)

Restricting training to a set T changes the estimator's SNR to `ρ = P/(P + d_T·(1 + ν/f_T))`, where
`f_T` is T's share of the signal power (solved per block from `cos_b`, then renormalised to sum to 1)
and `ν` is the measured evaluation-noise inflation. P = 512-draw ⇒ 256 pairs.

| T (k by signal) | d_train | f_T | ρ @ σ0.01 shares | × vs 0.041 | ρ @ σ0.02 shares | × |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| k1 `gp` | 864 | 0.13 | 0.042 | 1.03 | 0.039 | 0.96 |
| k2 `gp,dh` | 992 | 0.17 | 0.048 | 1.17 | 0.044 | 1.08 |
| k3 `+g5,gb5` | 1,058 | 0.24 | 0.058 | 1.41 | 0.043 | 1.05 |
| k4 `+w3,b3` | 1,123 | 0.26 | 0.057 | 1.40 | 0.043 | 1.05 |
| **k5 `+ds,b1`** | **1,191** | 0.39 | **0.070** | **1.71** | **0.042** | **1.03** |
| (`g1,gb1,g2,gb2` — the cliff) | 965 | 0.46 | 0.094 | 2.29 | 0.023 | 0.56 |
| all live | 5,997 | 1.00 | 0.024 | 0.59 | 0.016 | 0.40 |

(The "× vs 0.041" baseline is §0's number; the honest all-live row is 0.024 because it uses the same
noise-inflated model.) **The gain is 1.0-1.7×, and the top of that range exists only under the σ 0.01
shares.** Concentration ratio for every T above: **1.01-1.08** — the blocks do not hold more gradient
*energy* than their coordinate count, so the whole budget rests on the 1.4-2.8 σ cosines.

## 4. Cross-check against the tie census and SWITCH0 — the σ 0.01 leaders ARE the cliff

`S/ties/census.json` splits live coordinates into fine (coin-scaled, continuously decoded) and
coarse-only (reached only through a `floor`/`argsort`). Coarse-only carriers: `g2` (128/160),
`g8` (96/128), `g10` (96/128), `g11` (32/32), `g5` (32/64), `g6` (32/64), `gb2` (4/5), `gb8` (3/4),
`gb10` (3/4), `gb5`/`gb6` (1 each). Everything in `w1, b1, w2, g1, gb1, w3, dh, ds, g7, g9, gp` is fine.

* **σ 0.01's top of table is `g1` + `gb1` + `g2` + `gb2` + `g5`.** `docs/strategy/2026-09-11-day0-switch.md`
  §2.3 measured that `w1`, `g1` and `g2` — and only those — flip the day-0 switch on **6/6** TOPB2
  boards, while `gp` (a *longer* α-eq step, 0.0381 vs `g1`'s 0.0352), `fh`, `fv`, `w2`, `w3` move
  **0/6**; `gb2` is on the same path and read 0/6 only because its α-eq 0.0060 sits under the
  0.010-0.015 switch radius. So the σ 0.01 signal sits **exactly on the SWITCH0 subspace**, and `g2`,
  `gb2` and `g5` are coarse-integer carriers on top of that. **That cosine is most likely a cliff
  readout** — two draws agreeing about which side of one wall they landed on — not a slope.
* **σ 0.02 / 0.04's top of table is `gp` and `dh`, which are outside the switch subspace** (`gp` 0/6
  boards at more than `g1`'s step length; `dh` 2/6, gain −121 vs −1,269) and are fine-decode blocks.
  Their cosine cannot be the day-0 switch.
* **The σ-profile agrees.** Gaussian smoothing of a step function gives a mean gradient that decays as
  `1/σ` while rank-normalised noise stays O(1), so a *cliff*'s cosine falls with σ and a *slope*'s is
  flat. Measured: `g1` 0.097 → 0.040 → 0.007 (collapses — cliff), `g2` 0.146 → 0.020 → 0.016
  (collapses — cliff), `dh` 0.116 → 0.141 → 0.131 (flat — slope), `gp` 0.049 → 0.064 → 0.094
  (rises — slope, or noise). The two readings agree on the same split.

**How to settle it, cheaply, with what is already saved.** The per-pair recovery of §0 above makes the
decisive test a five-minute job: `S/switch0/census.json` names the 14 crossing and 42 non-crossing
thetas, and `S/onestep_b/csv` holds their per-board margins. Rebuild both draws **from the
non-crossing pairs only** (drop every pair whose fitness is on the far side of the wall) and recompute
`cos_b`. If `g1`/`g2`'s cosine dies and `dh`/`gp`'s survives, the σ 0.01 headline was the wall. The
weaker, one-line version is already in the table: the cosine restricted to the complement of
`S/switch0/freeze_mask.npy` (the `gp,dh,…` rows above) is positive at all three σ, and that complement
provably cannot reach the switch.

## 5. Recommendation for the next arm

```
--train-only gp,dh,ds,g5,gb5,w3,b3,b1     # d_train = 1,191 of 5,997 live (19.9 %)
--sigma 0.01 --optimizer sgd --lr 1.8e-4 --wd 4e-7
```

* **d_train 1,191, expected ρ 0.070** (σ 0.01 shares) **to 0.042** (σ 0.02 shares), against 0.041
  measured on the full live mask — a **1.0-1.7× SNR gain**, quoted as a range because that is what the
  measurement supports.
* **Why this set and not the bigger cosine.** `g1,gb1,g2,gb2` scores ρ 0.094 (×2.29) and is the single
  largest number in the study — and it is the SWITCH0 cliff subspace, where §4 of the switch doc
  already showed the objective is a step function with radius 0.0023-0.0035 and B is on the good side.
  Training there is discrete side-selection, not descent; it belongs in the engine-judged enumeration,
  not in an ES arm. The recommended set is the complement of the freeze mask, intersected with the
  blocks positive at both σ.
* **lr.** `‖δ‖ = lr·‖m‖`, `‖m‖ ≈ 0.229‖g‖` (the EMA of β 0.9 near-independent gradients,
  `√((1−β)/(1+β)) = 0.229`). Restricted to this set the σ 0.01 P512 draw has `‖g‖ = 47.53`, so
  `‖m‖ ≈ 10.89` and **lr = 0.002 / 10.89 = 1.8e-4** (2.1e-4 if the 0.0023 cell radius is the target).
  The lr is σ-dependent because ‖g‖ is: the same set reads ‖g‖ 15.62 at σ 0.02 (lr 5.6e-4) and 9.92 at
  σ 0.04 (lr 8.8e-4). Quote 1.8e-4 **with σ 0.01**.
* **wd.** Under this trainer's convention `upd = (1−wd)(θ+δ)` the equilibrium norm scales as lr, so
  holding it fixed needs `wd ∝ lr²`: from the (lr 3e-3, wd 1e-4) anchor, **wd = 1e-4·(1.8e-4/3e-3)² =
  3.8e-7 ≈ 4e-7**. Note the masked coordinates are not decayed either (`--train-only` gates the update),
  so 4,806 live coordinates are held byte-exact — which is the point.
* **Risk, stated plainly.** (i) No block is individually significant after multiplicity; the set is
  chosen on 1.4-2.8 σ cosines. (ii) The σ cells share one `eps`, so "positive at both σ" is weaker than
  two independent confirmations. (iii) Energy concentration is 1.0 — there is no second, independent
  line of evidence for these blocks. (iv) One seed, one centre. (v) If §4's cliff reading is right for
  `g5`/`ds` (coarse-only carriers that scored on σ 0.01 alone), the honest core of the set is
  `gp,dh,w3,b3,b1` (d_train 1,121, ρ ≈ 0.048-0.057) and the gain is ~1.2×, not 1.7×.
  **A 1.2-1.7× ρ is not a fix for a 4 % estimator.** The same arithmetic says raising P from 512 to
  4,096 buys 8× — more than any block choice in this table — and the two compose.
