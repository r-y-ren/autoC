The town signal arrived in stages. One `PIZZA_SHOP` was visible from day 3, `FARMERS_MARKET` made two tomato-buying shops from day 12, and a second `PIZZA_SHOP` made three from day 21. Tomato market inventory fell from 10,000 at day 0 to 9,895 at day 15 and 9,681 at day 29 even though neither opponent nor the early farm supplied it. This is the observable cause of the rising quote.

The early omission was a crop-mix decision rather than a failed plant action. On days 12--14 the reconstructed macro requested new strawberry and melon while requesting zero tomato, and the replay successfully planted multiple crops. A tomato seed cost only 50 while cash was 5,532--8,751, so seed cash was not the binding constraint. Capacity was heavily committed: by day 14 only one of 75 unlocked tiles was empty, 42 were strawberry or melon, and 17 were pasture/coop; the day's route used 11 hands. More tomato would have meant allocating naturally freed planting slots away from the intended crops and adding repeated watering/harvest travel. The dawn counts alone do not establish that labor was binding.

Tomato's eight-day first yield explains the apparent lag. Day-15 and day-16 plantings could not produce until the late price regime; day 21 is the last planting day admitted by the shipped sale horizon and has very little time to pay. By the time tomato clearly exceeded strawberry at dawn on day 24, new tomato planting could no longer mature before liquidation. The policy sees today's price, market inventory, town demand, current and opposing production, cash, and free space at each dawn. It has no explicit price-history feature or direct future-price output, although the network can learn an implicit anticipatory response from demand, stock, day, and committed production. The episode establishes only that its realized allocation was late and small. `Runtime` builds and caches a complete 24-hour plan at dawn, apart from the unrelated opening wheat tell, so it cannot re-plan for an intraday tomato move either.

## Judgment

There is a plausible missed *sizing/timing* opportunity on days 12--16: the agent saw two tomato buyers yet allocated almost every replacement slot to the then much more expensive strawberry and melon, and only began tomato at day 15. The replay cannot establish that additional tomato would have improved the final score. Extra supply lowers the shared tomato price, and tomatoes occupy a tile for twelve days while adding recurring route work. A source comment records that one prior forced endgame-tomato paired seed increased tomato revenue but lost heavily through strawberry and animal-output displacement. That single seed is a useful mechanism warning, not a general result, and it does not answer whether a smaller learned allocation would help this episode.

Nor is tomato the demonstrated cause of this loss. The opponent also grew zero tomato and already led 31,101 to 11,660 at day 15, before the sharp late rise; the final gap was 13,167. The current agent can condition its dawn decision on observed price, demand, stock, and production, and the exact outputs here eventually included tomato. Its representation supplies no explicit price history or future quote, and its realized response came late enough that only four tiles participated. Establishing a missed win would require a predeclared paired counterfactual, which this review did not run.

### Evidence interpretation

Prices, farms, actions, shops, cash, and opponent tomato absence are direct replay observations. `planned T` is an exact offline reconstruction from each observed dawn with the packaged-source-equal B and is not a counterfactual simulation. Maturation and runtime behavior are source facts from `spec.py`, `brain.py`, `parse.py`, and `runtime.py`. Requested sales are action quantities; this compact review does not reconstruct every market settlement or assign terminal coin value to a hypothetical crop mix.
