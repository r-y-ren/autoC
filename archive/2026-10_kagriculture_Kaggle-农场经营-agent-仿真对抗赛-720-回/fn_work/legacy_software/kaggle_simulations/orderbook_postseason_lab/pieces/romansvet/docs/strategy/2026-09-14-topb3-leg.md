## 3. The fidelity problem this leg found: 11 tapes desync against B

A tape is an action replay indexed by the engine step counter, and the generated package says so: a
HIRE it made can be refused here, after which the engine rejects every later action whose `hands`
list is longer than the roster. `tape_opponent.verify()` only proves the tape reproduces **its own**
game. So `S/topb3/retention.py` adds the live check:

    TAPE RETENTION = coins the tape scores in our leg / coins it scored in its source episode

| file | boards | mean retention | mean margin to B | B W-L |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Majkel1337 | 6 | **0.25** (0.00-0.49) | +99,602 | 12-0 |
| DSM | 6 | **0.65** (0.27-1.00) | +43,320 | 10-2 |
| ymg_aq | 6 | **1.02** (0.95-1.06) | −13,751 | 0-12 |
| control: NEXTHIGH (same pipeline, B leg) | 30 | **0.99** (median 1.01) | — | 2/30 below 0.85 |

The pipeline is fine — NEXTHIGH's 30 tapes retain 0.99. Majkel1337 (11 hands by d10) and DSM (8
hires on d1) are hire-heavy builds whose tapes lose hand alignment against B and then earn 0-49 %
of what they earned live: on those boards B beats a crippled tape, not the file.
