Agent, 2026-09-11, 55-minute box, CPU only. Inputs (read-only): `S/onestep_b/` (56 step thetas,
4,290 paired board rows), `S/onestep/thetas/`, `docs/strategy/2026-09-11-e3b-small-step.md` §2.7,
`docs/strategy/2026-09-11-e3-step-audit.md` §2. New files: `S/switch0/` only.

Question from consensus §60: E3b found that **14 of 56** small step thetas around
`B = artifacts/kagg2_games/thetas/flow193_g100_hr.npy` change the outcome on **100 % of boards in
two disjoint tape families**, and that every large antisymmetric read in E3/E3b is one of those
crossings. Which day-0 decision is it, which genes carry it, and which side is B on?

### 2.3 The block-only screen — three blocks flip it, twenty-seven do nothing at all

`S/switch0/build.py only 0.1` builds 34 thetas, each `B` with **exactly one `PO.SHAPES` block**
moved onto the σ 0.01 / P 512 minus ray at α 0.1 and every other coordinate left at B, screened on
6 TOPB2 boards (`S/simscreen/switch0_only_topb2.csv`). `α-eq` is that block's share of the ray's
length × 0.1, i.e. the α of a full-ray step of the same Euclidean size — compare it with the
switch radius α_crit ∈ (0.010, 0.015) measured in §3.

| block | live coords | α-eq at α 0.1 | boards moved | gain |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `w1` (encoder in→hidden) | 2,304 | 0.0622 | **6/6 (100 %)** | **−1,269** |
| `gp` (product residual→head hid) | 864 | 0.0381 | **0/6** | 0 |
| `g1` (global feat→head hidden) | 768 | 0.0352 | **6/6 (100 %)** | **−1,269** |
| `fh` (fwd feat→encoder hidden) | 512 | 0.0261 | 0/6 | 0 |
| `fv` (fwd value→head hidden) | 384 | 0.0250 | 0/6 | 0 |
| `g2` (head hidden→**head out**) | 160 | 0.0174 | **6/6 (100 %)** | **−1,269** |
| `w2` (enc hidden→grow/sell) | 128 | 0.0161 | 0/6 | 0 |
| `dh` (drain→encoder hidden) | 128 | 0.0146 | 2/6 (33 %) | −121 |
| every other block (`g5`…`gb11`, `w3`, `b1`, `b2`, `b3`, `gb2`, …) | — | ≤ 0.0157 | **0/6** | 0 |

Two things in that table settle it.

1. **It is not ordered by step size.** `gp` moves 864 coordinates a *longer* Euclidean distance
   (α-eq 0.0381) than `g1` (0.0352) and changes **not one coin in six complete games**; `fh`
   (0.0261) and `fv` (0.0250) likewise read exactly zero while `g2` at 0.0174 flips every board.
   The switch lives in a **subspace**, and `w1`/`g1`/`g2` span it: the shared encoder's input
   weights, and the global head's own path `global features → head hidden → head outputs`.
2. **All three give the identical result** — `−1,269` mean gain and mean |Δ| `1,269` on all six
   boards, i.e. the same board-by-board margins. Three disjoint parameter blocks producing
   byte-identical damage is one binary state changing, not three effects.

`gp`, `fh`, `fv`, `w2`, `w3` reading exactly zero is itself informative: a **per-product** decision
would move with `w2` (the grow/sell scores) or `gp`, and none of them does. The only quantity
`w1`, `g1` and `g2` share, and that `gp`/`fh`/`fv`/`w2` do not reach, is a **global head output**
— of which five are decoded (`head[1]` land bias in coins, `head[2]` animal-mix sharpness,
`head[5]` dev_frac, `head[6]` animal_share, `head[7]` plant-mix sharpness). `gb2`, the five biases
sitting directly on those outputs, reads 0/6 only because its α-eq is **0.0060**, below the 0.010
switch radius — it is on the path, just too short at this α.

> **Corrected verdict on gene space: the switch is a threshold on one GLOBAL HEAD OUTPUT.** It is
> carried by `w1` + `g1` + `g2` (3,232 live coordinates, 54 % of the live mask) and is unreachable
> from `gp`/`fh`/`fv`/`w2`/`w3` at more than twice the step length. No *single* coordinate carries
> it (§2.1), but the *block* that does is small and named.

## 3. The λ ladder — the objective at B really is a step function, and the switch radius is TINY

`S/switch0/build.py alpha` + `S/simscreen/screen.py` on the frozen 40-board TOPB2 list, the
σ 0.01 / P 512 **minus** ray (the one E3b's whole positive-`A` headline rests on),
θ(α) = B + α·(θ_sweep − B), `shopdiff 0.0 %`, `S/simscreen/switch0_alpha_topb2.csv`:

| α | ‖θ − B‖ | boards moved | % | paired gain vs B | mean \|Δ\| among moved |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0025 | 0.00058 | 1 | 2.5 | +7 | 295 |
| 0.0050 | 0.00116 | 1 | 2.5 | +7 | 295 |
| 0.0075 | 0.00174 | 7 | 17.5 | −28 | 829 |
| 0.0100 | 0.00232 | 7 | 17.5 | −28 | 829 |
| **0.0150** | **0.00348** | **40** | **100.0** | **−2,089** | 2,510 |
| 0.0200 | 0.00465 | 40 | 100.0 | −2,087 | 2,505 |
| 0.0250 | 0.00581 | 40 | 100.0 | −2,077 | 2,530 |
| 0.0500 | 0.01162 | 40 | 100.0 | −2,015 | 2,483 |
| 0.0750 | 0.01742 | 40 | 100.0 | −1,661 | 2,132 |

This is E3b §2.7's prediction confirmed **to the coin**. `gain(α)` is literally piecewise constant:
α 0.0025 and α 0.005 return **identical margins on all 40 boards**, so do α 0.0075 and α 0.01, and
the whole range α 0.015 → 0.05 sits at −2,015 … −2,089 (a 3.3× change in step length moves the
number by 3 %). There is no linear term to measure anywhere on this ray: the damage is fixed the
instant the switch flips and does not scale with α.

> **The switch radius is ‖θ − B‖ between 0.00232 and 0.00348** — i.e. between α 0.010 and α 0.015
> of the sweep's own step. The trainer's own generation step is `R = lr·√n_live = 0.2323`, which is
> **67-100× the switch radius**. And `lr = 0.003` is the *per-coordinate* Adam step, so **one
> coordinate moving by one lr is already the full switch radius**. Every ES generation at B crosses
> this discontinuity (and, by the same argument, an unknown number of others) on its first step.
> That is why 5 of 8 signed random controls cross it, why κ and the antisymmetric statistic `A` are
> Bernoulli readouts, and why the E3 rig cannot see a gradient at B — there is no neighbourhood of B
> small enough to be smooth that the trainer ever visits.

## 4. Which side is B on, and what the actionable output is

**B is on the good side.** Crossing costs −1,774 coins a board on TOPB2 (28 of 40 boards lost, net
−81.6 k against +10.6 k) and −74 on LIVE-C (a coin flip: 58 of 120 boards gain, +63.8 k against
−72.8 k). Nothing in the 14 gains on TOPB2. **No crossing is a lever** as a board-independent
choice; the only thing the LIVE-C split says is that the *far* side of this switch is right on
about half the hold-out boards, so a **board-conditional** version of the decision would be worth
up to ≈ +530/board on LIVE-C (+63.8 k over 120 cells) — a planner question, not an ES question,
and outside this box.

### 4.1 The freeze mask: 3,648 coordinates (w1+g1+g2), and why it is NOT recommended

`S/switch0/freeze_mask.npy` — `bool[6789]`, **3,648 coordinates set** = the whole of `w1` (2,304)
+ `g1` (768) + `g2` (576), of which **3,232 are live** after `PO.live_mask` drops `g2`'s 13
`DEAD_HEAD` columns — **53.9 % of the 5,997 live mask**. Those are the three blocks the block-only
screen showed can flip the switch on their own; every other block left `B` playing the identical
game at more than twice the step length. `S/switch0/freeze_mask_complement.npy` is the float32
`--train-only` form (1 where the ES may move a coordinate).

**It is the honest answer to the question asked, and it is still the wrong tool**, for three
reasons that the measurement itself supplies:

* **It freezes the network.** `w1`, `g1` and `g2` are the shared encoder's input layer and the
  entire global head. An ES that may not move them is not training a policy; the remaining 2,765
  live coordinates are the per-product and appended feature paths, which the same screen showed
  cannot change the outcome of a single one of six games at α 0.1.
* **It would not make the objective smooth.** The switch it removes is *one* discontinuity that
  happened to be sampled by 14 of 56 thetas. §3 shows the switch radius is 0.0023-0.0035 while the
  trainer's step is 0.2323; at that ratio a step crosses every tie of this class in the layout, and
  nothing here counted how many there are.
* **B is already on the good side of this one** (§4), so freezing it buys no score — it only makes
  the *measurement* (κ, `A`) stop being a Bernoulli readout. That is a benefit to the E3 rig, not
  to the agent.

**What the measurement actually recommends** is a trust region, and it needs no trainer change:
`--lr`. A step inside ‖Δθ‖ < 0.0023 stays on one plateau, but that is lr = 3e-5, at which a
generation moves nothing measurable. The real content of SWITCH0 is therefore:
**at B the smooth region is smaller than one useful ES step, so ES-with-a-gradient is the wrong
instrument at B regardless of σ, population or lr.** What B's neighbourhood supports is *discrete
engine-judged search over which side of these switches to sit on* — enumerate the ties, screen both
sides on LIVE-C and TOPB2, keep the better side. SWITCH0 just did that for one of them and B won.

### 4.2 Does the trainer have a coordinate mask today? Yes — `--train-only`, and it suffices

`scripts/train.py:1244` `--train-only SPEC`, validated at `scripts/train.py:2126-2130` and applied
at `src/kagg3/es/train.py:2784` as `self.mask = jnp.asarray(live * self.train_only)`. It gates the
**perturbation as well as the update** (`generation` draws `eps * self.mask`), which is exactly the
freeze semantics wanted: a masked coordinate is neither jittered in the population nor moved by the
step. Its spec language is `all` / `biases` / `all-biases` / **a comma-separated list of
`PO.SHAPES` block names** — and the mask SWITCH0 measured is *exactly three block names*.

> **The trainer can use this result today, with no code change**: the freeze is
> `--train-only <every block except w1,g1,g2>`, and the complementary experiment — search *only*
> in the subspace that carries the day-0 decisions — is `--train-only w1,g1,g2`. No patch needed.

`S/switch0/trainer.patch` is retained (written, **NOT applied**; `git diff --stat src/` empty) as
the ~14-line clause that would make `--train-only @<mask>.npy` legal, with a length check against
`PO.N_PARAMS` so a mask saved under an older layout cannot be silently zero-extended over an
appended block. It is only needed the day a *sub-block* mask is measured; today the answer is
block-aligned and the existing flag expresses it.

### 5.1 The `gb2` single-gene screen LANDED — and it was too coarse to name the output

20 thetas (`S/switch0/build_gb2.py`): each of the five live head biases `gb2[k]` shifted by
±0.3 and ±1.0 from B, 6 TOPB2 boards, `S/simscreen/switch0_gb2_topb2.csv`.

| gene (head output) | −1.0 | −0.3 | +0.3 | +1.0 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `gb2[1]` land_bias (coins) | 6/6, −23,855 | **0/6, 0** | 2/6, +1,168 | 6/6, −7,582 |
| `gb2[2]` animal_mix_sharp | 4/6, +775 | 4/6, +775 | 4/6, −818 | 6/6, −25 |
| `gb2[5]` dev_frac | 6/6, −17,170 | 6/6, −3,490 | 6/6, +203 | 6/6, −22,739 |
| `gb2[6]` animal_share | 6/6, −20,359 | 6/6, −2,966 | 6/6, −461 | 6/6, −7,203 |
| `gb2[7]` plant_mix_sharp | 6/6, −529 | 4/6, −217 | 6/6, −182 | 6/6, +271 |

**The test does not identify the carrier, and the reason is a design error worth recording:** a
±0.3 bias shift is ≈ 50× the ray's own `gb2` displacement and ~90× the switch radius, so it crosses
*many* ties at once. "Moves 6/6 boards" is therefore not evidence of *this* switch — 14 of the 20
shifts do it, with damage from −25 to −23,855, and none reproduces the switch's signature
(mean |Δ| = 1,269 with gain −1,269). The probe needed to be a **fine ladder**, not a coarse one.

Three things it does establish:

1. **B sits on a boundary in `gb2[1]` (land_bias), asymmetrically.** Shifting the land bias *down*
   by 0.3 changes **literally nothing** — 0 of 6 boards, 0 coins — while +0.3 moves 2/6 and ±1.0
   is catastrophic in both directions. B has slack below and a wall above on that gene. This is
   mild evidence *against* land_bias being the wheat-tile carrier, since the ray moves `gb2` by
   ~0.006 and the gene tolerates 50× that in one direction.
2. **Plateaus again, on single genes**: `gb2[2]` at −0.3 and at −1.0 return the **identical**
   result (4/6, +775, mean |Δ| 1,162) — a 3.3× larger shift landing on the same side of the same
   tie, exactly the §3 step-function shape.
3. **§5's "many more ties" premise is now measured, not assumed.** Every one of the five live head
   biases is within 0.3 of a discontinuity, and all five are within 1.0 of a ≥ 500-coin one. And
   **B beats almost all of them**: of the 20 single-gene shifts only four gain at all (+203, +271,
   +775, +1,168) and sixteen lose, up to −23,855.

**How to finish the identification** (not attempted here): a fine ladder on each `gb2[k]` —
δ ∈ ±{0.002, 0.005, 0.01, 0.02, 0.05} — looking for the *first* plateau boundary, then confirm the
winner with `S/topledger` + `S/switch0/day0.py` against the §3b signature (`planted` 19→18,
`tile_WHEAT` 11→10, `idle` 0→1, `money` +10). The margin number alone is not enough to attribute a
crossing; **the day-0 row is the only unambiguous fingerprint**, and it is cheap (6 episodes).
`S/switch0/tie.py` (written by another agent into this directory during the box, left untouched)
is an `xp`-shim that records every `floor` call inside `brain.decide` and would name the decoded
quantity directly, without the ladder.

* The 34 **block-ablation** thetas* The 34 **block-ablation** thetas (`S/switch0/thetas/abl_*.npy`, α 0.03: every block on the ray
  *except* one) are built but unscreened. They would confirm §2.3 from the other side — restoring
  `w1`, `g1` or `g2` alone to B should un-cross the switch.

**Not attempted, and the real follow-up.** SWITCH0 found and characterised **one** tie. §3 shows
the trainer's step is 67-100× its radius, so the layout almost certainly contains many more, and
nothing here counted them. The cheap census: take B, draw 32 random directions, and for each one
bisect α to find *every* radius at which the 40-board margin vector changes state. The number of
distinct plateaus crossed between ‖Δθ‖ = 0 and 0.2323 is the number of discrete decisions one ES
generation re-rolls, and it is the number that says whether "discrete engine-judged search over
switch sides" is a 5-lever programme or a 500-lever one.
