Static comparison only. Neither the notebook nor a game was run for this report. Arlene's public `main.py` is the frozen external research opponent; V9 remains untouched. The compressed route payloads were decoded as JSON data, never imported as Python policy code.

Data-only route audit: V9's two-egg pairs choose `BAKERY,BAKERY -> 101 -> GOOSE 2`, `BAKERY,BRUNCH_SPOT -> 103 -> GOOSE 2`, `BRUNCH_SPOT,BAKERY -> 105 -> COW 2`, and `BRUNCH_SPOT,BRUNCH_SPOT -> 103 -> GOOSE 2` at step 150. Across 49 non-yarn ordered pairs, the first day-6 animal purchase is `COW 2` for 37 pairs, `GOOSE 2` for 11, and `COW 1` for one. Thus the economic question extends beyond one named shop pair, but the policy must derive its answer from observed market conditions rather than a lookup keyed by pair or seed.

An important constraint comes from the *rest* of the route. V9 routes 101 and 105 have the same actions before day 6 and differ on just 17 of the 719 turns (10 physical turns, 8 market turns, counting overlap). Route 103 and 105 differ on 306 turns, including 176 physical turns. Route 103 has early egg sale slots on days 11, 13 and 14; routes 101/105 and Arlene's base have no scheduled egg sale until day 15. Arlene's goose conversion does not itself rewrite that sale schedule. Its first geese can produce at placement day +4 and hold at most four eggs each, so earlier output can saturate before it reaches the shed or a paying order. This is why gross animal yield is not a valid decision score.

## One implementable missing mechanism: economic recourse among compatible *complete* routes

At the day-6 route commitment (`experiments/round9_market_slack.py:961`), consider a small graph of existing route tapes that (1) have the identical executed prefix, (2) retain the same hire/land feasibility before the first changed purchase, and (3) differ in a bounded animal investment plus its associated pickup, building, placement and sale plan. A data-only scan finds 13 route pairs with an identical pre-day-6 prefix, at most 30 changed actions through step 647, and different first animal buys. For example, 101 versus 105 is a complete goose-versus-cow alternative. This uses the *whole* route rather than applying Arlene's species patch onto a cow route. It is not an exact shop-combination or seed rule: current shop demand, price/inventory state, cash, visible competing supply and capacity decide whether the alternate route is worth using.

Evaluate each compatible route from the current observation with the same physical/market semantics as the official engine. Compare **cashable bank margin**, not nominal production: subtract the actual 300-per-goose or 400-per-cow purchase; include any extra feed purchases and land/hire requirements; simulate maturation (goose +4 days, daily thereafter, held cap 4; cow +8 days, every other day, held cap 6), native FEED/CARE/HARVEST, worker-inventory-to-shed transfer, and the existing as well as newly feasible SELL slots. The engine executes field actions before market orders, quotes SELL orders in lockstep with the rival, and town shops drain inventory every four turns. Use the actual `market.params` override if present. For unknown later shops and rival private stock, compare a small pessimistic-to-optimistic supply range from public observations; switch only for a clear robust margin over the incumbent, otherwise keep V9's route. Report the interval and branch frequency, not just a chosen route ID.

The safest insertion is a **derived router in a new isolated source**, at `experiments/round9_market_slack.py:961`, before `_IMPL=make_agent(...)` freezes that router at line 990. An outer wrapper acting after `_IMPL(...)` returns would be too late for the commitment. Do not rewrite `_R108_SHOP_ROUTES` or frozen V9 in place. The selection must happen once per game and store the chosen route in the native state so `_v219_*` physical adaptation, `_y_controller` purchase confirmations, `_r85_*` reservations, `_r9s_reservations`, and terminal route 2 all see the same route. Exclude yarn-specialized `_V92_TABLE` cases and any rival-signature `_V93_ROUTE_BY_RIVAL` override in the first ablation; those layers have separate contracts. At commitment, verify actual cash after earlier market orders can fund the candidate purchase, and verify the route's required seeds, depot stock, worker positions, unlocked tiles and warehouse room against the observation. If these do not match, retain the original route.

**Sale closure is a hard gate, not an assumed benefit.** A native HARVEST creates worker inventory, not immediately saleable shed stock. A route candidate only earns the product when a feasible DROP/PLACE and SELL occur by game end, within ten executable market order slots and without displacing funded input purchases. If a candidate generates extra eggs but its tape has no timely egg SELL, its modeled value is reduced accordingly. Do not automatically append a sale after V9's final `_v44y_reorder`; if a later isolated sale-closeout is tested, project actual shed stock and re-evaluate the *final* market queue with the rival-price externality. Existing V9 `round9_slack_agent` can add sales after reorder, so ordering and capacity must be checked on the returned action.

Likely failure modes: a route selected for today's two shops may lose when later shops unlock; a goose route may run out of feed or held-cap room despite attractive egg prices; extra own egg sales depress own later quotes and the opponent's quotes; a full route swap can change weeds and therefore future shop RNG; and the local cash-margin model may be optimistic about the rival's unseen shed. The older `round9_market_herd.py` cow/sheep forecast is not precedent for success: it prices prospective output but does not certify each unit's physical delivery and sale. First measure branch coverage and actual purchased/placed/harvested/deposited/sold units, then paired V9-versus-candidate bank margins across independent opponents and both seats. This report makes no performance claim.
