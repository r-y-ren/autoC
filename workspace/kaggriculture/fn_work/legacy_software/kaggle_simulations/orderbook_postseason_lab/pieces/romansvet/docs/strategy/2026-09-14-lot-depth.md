# 2026-09-14 — LOT-DEPTH: is our own sell lot walking its own price down?

**VERDICT: the lot-depth lever is real, universal and TINY — an order of
magnitude smaller than the ~500 coins/board WHEAT-REBUY §7 bounded it at —
and every arm that forces the spread either does nothing or loses.** Capping
the day-29 liquidation at 40 units a lot is the best arm anywhere: **+69
coins (t +1.43) on the 68 band boards, −2 (t −1.67) on the 12 ymg_aq boards**.
Capping all season at 20 loses **−427 (t −3.57)** on the band and **−241
(t −2.48)** on ymg. **No setting comes near the §115 promotion rule (+450
pooled, t ≥ 2) on any board set or subset.** The reason is arithmetic, not
tuning: a split can only harvest the town's consumption *between two lot
turns*, which for wheat on a real day-29 shelf is **+46 to +163 coins on the
whole 65-unit liquidation** (§2). The −3.82/unit day-29 gap `S/rebuy/
depth_cost.log` measured is therefore mostly the OPPONENT's 101.8-unit dump
moving the shelf under us, not our own walk.

Sim, descriptive, paired, CRN, action-replay opponent seat, theta B =
`flow193_g100_hr`, shipped `hr` switches. No engine game, no training arm, no
ladder claim.

**Why day 29 is one lot.** Not a turn budget, not a liquidation rule and not
an allocator cap: the terminal day offers the same three lots as every other
day (`plan.py:288-291` says so explicitly), and `rounds = PJ.K - 1 = 100`
covers a full shed. It is **`press`**. `S/lot_depth/depth_probe.py` §3 runs
the real allocator over the real mean dawn-d29 shelf of the 12 ymg boards
(wheat inventory 9,783, quote 40) with 65 units to liquidate:

| press (wheat) | lot 1 / lot 2 / lot 3 | revenue | vs a cap of 20 | vs a cap of 40 |
|---:|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 0 | 0 / 8 / 57 | 2,621 | −32 | −25 |
| **1** | **65 / 0 / 0** | 2,534 | **+55** | +19 |
| 2 … 32 | 65 / 0 / 0 | 2,534 | +55 | +19 |

At `press = 0` the greedy already puts 57 of the 65 units in the LAST lot and
a cap only damages it. **Any `press >= 1` collapses the whole day into lot
1**, because `press * lot_index` is charged per lot of delay while the whole
curve between lot 1 and lot 3 is worth under two coins a unit here. `press` is
`_qfloor(base * relu(tanh(gate)))` (`core/brain.py:1161`), one-sided and
price-scaled, so it is ≥ 1 on essentially every product B has an opinion
about — and that, not any liquidation rule, is what builds the deep lot.

**And the recoverable amount is ~55 coins, not ~500.**

## 2. THE CEILING — a split can only buy the town tick

Splitting *within a turn* is worth exactly zero: the engine resolves a seat's
slots in order down one inventory path, so three 20-unit orders in one turn
pay the same 60 table entries as one 60-unit order (the same identity
`S/rebuy/wash_check.py` proved for the round trip). The **only** thing a
split buys is the town consumption that fires between lot turns
(`projector.ticks_before`: turn 3 → 1 shop tick, turn 10 → 3, turn 18 → 5).

65 wheat units from inventory 9,850, one lot at turn 3 against 22/22/21 over
turns 3/10/18 (`S/lot_depth/depth_probe.log` §2):

| town size (shop instances) | wheat drained per shop tick | one lot | split 22/22/21 | delta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 8 | 5 | 2,328 | 2,374 | **+46** |
| 16 | 10 | 2,328 | 2,414 | **+86** |
| 24 | 15 | 2,328 | 2,454 | **+126** |
| 32 | 20 | 2,328 | 2,491 | **+163** |

**Every product has the walking structure**, so the switch is written for all
nine: `spec.py:111-121` gives each a monotone `below_func` of `I0 - inv`
(`sqrt`, `hinge`, `log`, `linear`), and `market_price` (`spec.py:154-169`)
therefore falls on every unit sold. What one deep lot costs itself, from
inventory 9,850 (`depth_probe.log` §1):

| product | below-I0 shape | P(9850) | 20 in one lot | 40 | 65 | self-cost of 65 |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| WHEAT | sqrt 0.80 | 37 | 36.90 | 36.45 | 35.82 | 77 (1.18/u) |
| CARROT | hinge 1.00 | 47 | 45.95 | 45.17 | 44.18 | 183 (2.82/u) |
| TOMATO | hinge 0.40 | 78 | 76.90 | 75.67 | 74.17 | 249 (3.83/u) |
| STRAWBERRY | sqrt 0.70 | 223 | 219.55 | 215.85 | 210.94 | 784 (12.06/u) |
| MELON | log 0.20 | 294 | 293.40 | 292.73 | 291.77 | 145 (2.23/u) |
| EGG | hinge 0.40 | 59 | 58.45 | 57.88 | 57.11 | 123 (1.89/u) |
| MILK | sqrt 0.60 | 266 | 262.95 | 259.18 | 254.09 | 774 (11.91/u) |
| WOOL | log 0.20 | 243 | 242.50 | 241.85 | 240.91 | 136 (2.09/u) |
| FERTILIZER | linear 0.40 | 130 | 128.10 | 126.10 | 123.60 | 416 (6.40/u) |

That self-cost is **not** what a split recovers — it is what the *seller*
pays whoever sells after him, and a split only defers it across turns. The
recoverable part is the town-drain column above.

## 3. BUILD — `LOT_SPLIT_ON`, default OFF

`plan.LOT_SPLIT_ON = False`, `plan.LOT_SPLIT_MAX = 0`,
`plan.LOT_SPLIT_DAYS = ()` (`core/plan.py`, next to `REBUY_*`), the helper
`plan._lot_split` (next to `_sell_hold`) and one site inside `_plan_and_stats`
immediately before `s_qty = xp.sum(lots, axis=0)`:

* the cap is applied to the **voluntary** allocation alone and **conserves
  `s_qty` exactly** — so the shed-overflow deficit, the forced sale's ranking
  and quantity, the prestock projection and every bulk add listed in §1 read
  the numbers they always read and place their own units where they always
  did (which is also why those adds can still stand a lot above the cap);
* spare capacity is filled earliest-lot-first, so a product the greedy already
  spread keeps its shape; a liquidation of more than `N_LOTS * LOT_SPLIT_MAX`
  units puts the residue on the LAST lot (the one the town drained furthest).
  **No unit is ever dropped.**
* `LOT_SPLIT_MAX <= 0` and `LOT_SPLIT_ON = False` are both decided at Python
  time, so the shipped program never traces any of it.

**Identity with the switch OFF.** `S/lot_depth/run.py` re-run over the 12

### a tight cap DESTROYS units, and that is a shed fact

`cap 10, d29` sells **10.66 fewer units a board** on the band (t −7.86), every
one of them on day 29 (`S/lot_depth/unit_loss.log`: the seasons are unit-exact
through dawn d29 and diverge only over the last day; wheat alone loses 7.97).
The plan cannot lose a unit — `tests/test_lot_split.py` pins total volume over
six caps and three stock levels — so the loss is the ENGINE clipping a
deposit. Day 29 sells **171.9 units against a dawn shed of 90.8** (band, our
seat): more than half of the terminal sale is stock `BANK_BEFORE_LOT_ON`'s
excursion DROPs into the shed *during* the day, offered in bulk on its own lot
(`plan.py:7434-7451`). `SHED_CAPACITY` is 100. A cap that defers voluntary
volume out of the early lot leaves the shed fuller when that DROP lands, and
the deposit is clipped — the units are never banked and never sold. The effect
is monotone in how much the cap defers: **+0.59 units at cap 40, −0.59 at cap
20, −10.66 at cap 10.**

## 5. WHY THE ARMS DO NOT PAY

1. **The ceiling is ~50-160 coins a board on wheat** (§2), and the whole
   nine-product day-29 liquidation is at most a few hundred — against a
   38,000-coin gap to ymg_aq. WHEAT-REBUY's "~500 coins on wheat" was the
   *unit-weighted gap to the dawn quote* (`S/rebuy/depth_cost.log`, −3.82/u on
   day 29), and that gap is dominated by the opponent: they put **101.8 units**
   into the same day-29 pot to our 64.6, and both seats' realised prices fall
   together. Our own walk is 1.18/unit of it.
2. **`press` is not free to override.** Where the greedy concentrates, it does
   so because a learned per-lot pressure says the later lot is worse; forcing
   the spread pays the pressure term's whole bill to collect the town drain.
   On the terminal day that trade is roughly even (±100 coins). On an ordinary
   day it is not: the day's stock is not a liquidation, the early lot competes
   with the SAME day's forced-overflow ranking and tomorrow's `avail`, and
   `cap 20, all season` loses on both sets.
3. **An early lot is shed room, not just a price.** The terminal day banks
   more than it wakes up holding, so deferring a lot costs deposits (above).
   Any "sell later" arm has to price that; a per-lot cap cannot see it.
4. **The cap is the wrong shape for the right knob.** The measured optimum at
   `press = 0` is not an even split at all — it is **0 / 8 / 57**, i.e. *sell
   late*, not *sell in thirds*. A flat per-lot cap can only approach that from
   the wrong side.

## 7. VERDICT AND NEXT

* **Is our lot walking its own price down?** Yes, on all nine products, and
  the day-29 liquidation does put its whole volume into lot 1 whenever
  `press >= 1`.
* **Is it worth anything?** ~50-160 coins a board on wheat, and paired, no
  cap setting beats B by +450 with t ≥ 2 on any set. The family is **closed as
  a lever**; the switch stays in the tree, default off, as a measured bound.
* **What the numbers actually point at.** The `press = 0` allocation is
  `0 / 8 / 57` — **the gradient is "sell later", not "sell thinner"**, and it
  is a `press` gradient, i.e. one that lives in theta and not in a constant.
  The next experiment is therefore a *pressure* arm, not a *cap* arm: scale
  `macro.press` by a module constant (`PRESS_SCALE`, default 1) and sweep it
  down to 0 on the terminal day and then all season, paired on the same two
  sets. It is the same three lines of code as this switch, it reaches the
  allocation the probe says is optimal instead of approximating it, and it
  tests whether the learned pressure term is carrying real information about
  the opponent's dump or is simply a stale gene.
* Second, unmeasured and larger: the 101.8 units the opponent puts into the
  day-29 pot are **forecastable** (`projector.py:40-62`, `OPP_SUPPLY_ON` is
  built and off). Lot 3 on day 29 is quoted against a shelf they have already
  flooded; `lot_inventories(..., day)` already plumbs the forecast through.
  That is a bigger number than anything in this report.
