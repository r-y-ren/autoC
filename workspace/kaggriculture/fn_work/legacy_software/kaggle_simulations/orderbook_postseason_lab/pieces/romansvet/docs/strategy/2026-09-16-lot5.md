## 4. Why

The switch was built on the reading that LOT4@17 won because it was a free front
turn. It is not. `allocate` gives each unit to the row with the best adjusted
marginal and breaks ties on the EARLIEST row, and that marginal rises with town
ticks — so a front turn carries units only if the lot it fronts is the day's
**best-quoting** row. There is exactly one, lot 3, and LOT4 already stands in
front of it. Turn 9 fronts lot 2, three ticks below: on all five pinned boards it
takes **zero** units, and in the engine it is −204; same for 6, 7, 14. The 17
spike was the property of being tied with the one row that matters, not of the
shop grid — and that slot is taken.

That leaves rows LATER than lot 3, and 21's +386 is a 22-board fluke of exactly
the size ENG22 makes (se 175 → 2.2 σ; 10 of its 22 boards are TOPB2 boards). On
169 boards the same arm is −1,179 at t −8.76 — the mechanism `lot4sweep` priced at
−831 for LOT4@21: at 21 the fifth row becomes the day's LAST lot, so every "day's
LAST lot" bulk add (`DROP_ON`, `MELON_OPEN`, `MIDDAY_PLACE_V2`, `SAME_DAY_FERT`,
`ANIMAL_SAME_DAY`) moves three turns later with it, and the extra town ticks are
bought with `press`. Both purses say so: +245 to us, +1,131 to them.

**The market-row family is CLOSED.** 6/7/9/14/21/22 here and 13-19/21 in
`lot4sweep` read every turn that matters; 8, 12, 16, 19 and 23 are interior to
measured flats or worse neighbours of measured losses. LOT4@17 is the whole lever.
