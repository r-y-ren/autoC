Branch `boey1` (from selfplay1 cc94afdb). Scripts are in `S/boey1/`. Replays are read in place from the main repo:
- Boey vs top-50: `S/q4teams1/games`, 23 games.

**Verdict: NO SHIP. None of the three screened Boey rules comes near the bar; all lose, from −23 to −58 net flips.**
- Against the same opponent family (V), Boey's edge is +19.4k of its OWN purse per game. The V rival's purse is the same in both samples, so none of it is denial.
- About 80 % of that edge sits on axes already closed (see §4).
- The three Boey rules nobody had tested are all worse for our body:
  - no tomato: −23
  - no d0-9 carrot: −58
  - Q2 on d6: −36
- Boey's rules only work as one package: a d0 melon plate, zero cash, and an early herd with crew. Every piece of that package has already failed on our body.

## 0. Method
- **`census.py`** records, per seat and per day:
  - dusk plantings by crop, noon crop-tile and animal census, noon hands
  - ops by type for the farmer and hands
  - seed / animal / product ORDERS
  - the exact hire bill and the land day
- **`netled.py`** builds the exact net market ledger from the shed identity. For each step:
  - sold − bought = shed_prev + deposits − pickups − shed_next.
  - Deposits are DROP / PLACE at a shed-access tile plus the end-of-day dump; pickups are PICKUP.
  - Units are priced at the pre-step quote.
  - Actual seed spend comes from Δseeds + new plantings. Actual animal spend comes from Δhead count.
  - **Closure check** (implied spend = 3000 + net$ − final, against actual spend):

    | seat | implied | actual |
    |---|---|---|
    | Boey vs V | 22.9k | 23.5k |
    | V rival | 24.4k | 23.1k |
    | ours | 24.3k | 19.4k |

  - Ours is the only one that misses, by 4.9k. That gap is our sale **slippage**: pricing our fills on the exact engine walk (`churn.py`) closes our books to 0.0k, so we lose 5.0k/game to price walk inside our own lots, while Boey loses ≤ 0.6k.
- **Why orders cannot be used as sales:** Boey fires ~10 market orders every hour — interleaved SELL ≤ 7 u and BUY_PRODUCT wheat/fertilizer — and most of them fail for cash or shed. Its order ledger overstates wheat sales by 10×, so every Boey product number here comes from `netled.py`, not from orders.
- **Family** is the opponent's d2 h0 board (the LIVEWATCH16 tell, `fam.py`): V = 11-13 melon and 6-9 wheat.
  - Boey's 33 low-rated games are 24 V, 8 MELON, 1 ZERO.
  - Our 73 games are 63 V, 10 MELON.
- **Opponent control:** the V rival plays the same game in both samples (`vs_v.txt` rival columns):
  - plantings d10-19: 87.4 vs 87.6
  - hands: 10.5 vs 10.3
  - animals: equal
  - final purse: 98.4k vs 97.6k

## 3. How Boey beats the V family, day by day, against our run
- **d0-9:** both sides are level with V (G −0.8k vs +0.1k).
  - Boey spends to zero on crew, geese and sheep, plus a plate identical to V's.
  - We bank 5.8k by d10 for Q3 and the late melon.
- **d10-14: the whole difference is decided here.**
  - Boey sells its 65 plate melons into the same window as V's 71: the board melon quote is 137 vs 223 in our games.
  - It still holds the gap at −1.2k, reaches +8.1k by d15, and its purse compounds from 13.8k at d12 to 31.3k at d15.
  - We fall to −17.2k at d15 against V's plate. Our first melon is sold on d20.
- **d15-29:** Boey keeps widening, +8 → +26k.
  - Eggs +3.3k and wheat +3.7k are volume.
  - Strawberry +9.5k is a board-level price.
  - We recover +23.5k from d15 to d29, and the recovery is our late melon (85 u) sold into a melon-free d20-29 market.
- **Boey loses 1 of 24 to V; we lose 7 of 63.**
  - Our losses are the d12-15 hole not closing by d29 (LOSSMAP17 S2).
  - Boey never digs that hole, because its own plate pays it in d10-14.

Why each rule fails:
- **Tomato is one of our real earners.** Boey's zero tomato comes with a plate-funded wheat engine. The wheat and carrot that replace it on our tiles return about a third of the tomato's coin: +2.4k against −6.4k.
- **Our d0-2 carrot is the one early product V does not grow.** Replacing it with wheat changes our cash path (Q2 moves a day earlier), and V's purse rises +6.0k (t 16.2), mostly in milk. The early carrot is load-bearing on this leg.
- **Q2 on d6 is Boey's way of feeding the herd from the d5 purse.** On our body the d5 cash has no herd use (HERD_FIRST and WOOLFIRST are closed), so the day's delay only costs strawberry and gifts wool and milk.

## 6. Verdict
- **Biggest Boey-vs-us gaps, against the same V opponent, per game:**
  1. The melon plate's d10-14 cash timing: +12.8k in d10-19, G15 +8.1k vs our −17.2k.
  2. Strawberry d20-29: +9.5k, of which 7.5k is price. It is the board-level quote and not play.
  3. Eggs from 7 geese: +6.8k.
- Sale slippage adds +4.4k and the wool opening +3.3k.
- Every expressible item is on a CLOSED axis. The rival's purse is identical, so Boey wins on its own production, not on denial.
- **Screen:** all three untested Boey rules fail — tomato −23, early wheat −58, Q2 d6 −36. Δtheirs t for the three is 3.2 / 16.2 / 11.0. None goes to ship legs.
- **Insight that should change strategy:** Boey's rules only pay as ONE open-loop package, and every piece of it has been rejected on our body separately:
  - the d0 plate (gift against a reacting V)
  - zero cash
  - early herd and crew
  - Q2 d6 / Q3 d8
  - no tomato
- The only remaining way to test the Boey hypothesis is the whole package against the reacting V. The SCRIPTOPEN / MELONENG precedent says that loses (−15k, gift).
- So the Boey axis is CLOSED. Our loss class stays the d12-15 hole against V's plate (LOSSMAP17 S2), which Boey avoids only by owning a plate of its own.
