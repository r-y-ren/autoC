# 2026-09-14 — SHED-CLIP: what the engine destroys at the shed door

**VERDICT: the clip is REAL AT B's DEFAULTS, MATERIAL, and it is mostly NOT
the day-29 DROP that LOT-DEPTH found.** Under theta B `flow193_g100_hr` with
the shipped `hr` switches and nothing else set, our seat destroys **21.6
units / 1,718 coins a game-seat on the 68 band boards (t +7.33)** and **24.6
units / 1,816 coins on the 12 ymg_aq boards (t +4.43)** — ~1.7-2.0 % of our
final purse — against a §115 promotion bar of +450 pooled. It is **two
separate leaks**: the nightly free dump loses **15.4 (band) / 21.9 (ymg)**
units across **d14-d28**, and the day-29 return-leg `OP_DROP` loses **6.3
(band) / 2.7 (ymg)** more in the last two hours of the season. The
action-replay opponents lose **4.4 / 2.6** units doing the same thing, so
this is our leak, not the game's. The one built repair in the tree,
`SHED_OVERFLOW_ON`, was measured here for the first time and recovers **0.10
units a board** (§4a); the live term is the forced sale's projection, which
excludes the watering bonus (§4a').

Sim, descriptive, paired-CRN board sets, action-replay opponent seat, frozen
HEAD `src` (5f9cac7). No engine game, no training arm, no ladder claim. No
`src/` was edited: every counter is a wrapper around the shipped functions.

## 1. MECHANISM — where the units die (exact lines)

| what | file:line |

**There is no harvest-side guard and there does not need to be one.** A
harvest lands in the unit's *hand* inventory (`units.py:164-172`, `inv = inv +
gain`), which has no cap; nothing in `plan.py` skips or defers a harvest
because the shed is full. The shed door is the only place goods can die, and
`OP_DROP` and the nightly dump are the only two doors.

**Why the day-29 DROP clips (the hour profile is decisive).** Mean shed level
at the start of each hour of day 29, our seat, 68 band boards:

```
h0  h1  h2  h3 … h12 h13 h14 h15 h16 h17 h18 | h19 …
 91  91   4   4      5   6   9  11  22  38  60 |   7
```

Lot 1 (turn 3, engine hour 1-2) clears the whole 91-unit dawn shed — the
terminal `hold` is `SELL.LIQUIDATE` (`plan.py:7322`), so `avail` is the
entire shed and `press >= 1` puts it in lot 1 (LOT-DEPTH §1). The crew then
walks, and **ten return-leg DROPs pile 99.6 units back into that shed between
h13 and h18 with no market row between turn 10 and turn 18 to drain it**. Lot 2
(turn 10) offers nothing, because at turn 10 the shed holds 5 units. The shed
passes 100 at h17/h18 and the last DROPs are destroyed: **0.74 units at hour
17, 5.51 at hour 18** (band). `4 + 99.6 = 103.6 > 100` — the day's own harvest
simply does not fit, and `deficit` is gated off on the terminal day
(`plan.py:7452`) so the forced sale never looks.

**LOT-DEPTH's attribution was wrong in one detail.** `BANK_BEFORE_LOT_ON` is
**off** on day 29 (`plan.py:7097` `bank_day = ~terminal`). The mid-day deposits
that make day 29 sell 171.9 units against a 90.8 dawn shed are `DROP_ON`'s
terminal return legs, not the bank excursion. The rest of §4.5 stands: a cap
that defers voluntary volume out of lot 1 leaves the dawn shed sitting there
when those DROPs land, which is why `cap 10` destroyed 10.66 units.

## 3. IS IT A LEAK IN THE SHIPPED AGENT? YES — AND IT IS BIGGER THAN THE ARMS

The thread that produced the question (LOT-DEPTH, WHEAT-REBUY) saw the clip
only under its own arms, and concluded it was an artefact of deferring volume.
It is not: **at B's shipped defaults, with nothing set, 21.6-24.6 units a
game-seat die** — 4-8x what the opponents lose, and both criteria of the brief
(≥ 5 units, ≥ 200 coins) are cleared by an order of magnitude on both sets.

Two honest discounts before anyone spends it:

1. **The coin figure is a dawn-quote upper bound.** Selling 21 extra units a
   game walks the quote down (LOT-DEPTH §2's per-product self-cost: 1.18/u on
   wheat from a deep lot, 12/u on strawberry), and the units that die are
   disproportionately the expensive thin products whose curve is steepest.
   A realistic recovery is well under 1,718.
2. **Part of the loss is not recoverable at all.** On day 29 the shed simply
   cannot hold the day's harvest (4 + 99.6 > 100) with only one market row
   left; on the nightly side the forced sale (`plan.py:7493-7530`) already
   fires and the residue is what its conservative-LOW projection and its
   reservations hid from it.

## 4. NEXT — the built repair is DEAD; the projection term is the live one

**(a) `SHED_OVERFLOW_ON` — MEASURED HERE, AND IT IS INERT.** The obvious
candidate was the built, default-off, never-measured switch (`plan.py:565`,
body `:7666-7695`) that offers back the stock a reservation hid from the sale.
Two paired CRN arms against the stored OFF raws, frozen HEAD `src`
(`S/shed_clip/launch_overflow.sh`, raws `S/macro_exec/raw_shedovf*.npz`,
tables `S/shed_clip/shedovf_report.md`):

| set | our coins Δ (t) | margin Δ (t) | units sold OFF → ON | flips |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 12 ymg_aq | **+0** (identical on every board) | +0 | 1389.5 → 1389.5 | 0 |
| 68 band | **+4** (+2.67) | +4 (+2.70) | 1404.16 → 1404.26 | 0 |

It recovers **0.10 units a board of the 15-21 that die.** Its `spare`
(`shed - used - s_qty - forced`) is empty in practice: the first pass has
already drained every product it was allowed to touch, so the hidden
reservation is not where the loss lives. **Close that switch as a lever** —
keep it in the tree as a measured zero.

**(a') The live term: the overflow projection excludes the watering bonus.**
`plan.py:7438-7441` sizes the forced sale on

```
inflow = sum(where(covered & has_harv, view.t_yield, 0)) + sum(covered & has_coll)
```

— the *pre-watering* yield, explicitly ("today's watering bonuses excluded",
`:7431`). But a chain that waters before it harvests collects the bonus, and
`plan.py` already spells the corrected quantity two blocks down, in
`BANK_BEFORE_LOT_ON`'s own add: `bl_units = where(bank_mask & has_harv,
view.t_yield + 2 * bl_w, 0)` (`:7621`, with `bl_w = any(chain_op == OP_WATER)`).
So `proj_eod` under-counts tonight's shed by **2 units per reached tile that
waters and harvests on the same chain**, `deficit` comes out too small, and the
difference is exactly what the night destroys. **The change is one term** —
`view.t_yield` → `view.t_yield + 2 * has_water` inside `inflow`, behind a
default-off `OVERFLOW_WATER_ON`, paired on the same two sets against
`raw_headOFF.npz` / `raw_melOFF.npz`.

**Bound, and the reason it is a real experiment and not a free win:** the
recoverable side is the nightfall clip, **≤ 1,272 coins (band) / 1,686 (ymg)**
at dawn quotes, minus the walk a bigger forced sale costs itself. The cost side
is that the projection stops being conservative: every over-estimated day
force-sells stock that did not need selling, at the day's cheapest marginal
quote. The arm settles which is bigger, and it is the only candidate in this
file whose upper bound clears +450 pooled.

**(b) A terminal-day sell row between the DROP burst and turn 18.** The engine
resolves a market every turn; the simulator resolves only the turns the planner
uses, and the infrastructure for extra sell-only rows already exists
(`ops.MELON_LOT_TURNS = (11, 13, 15)`, `rollout.py:56-69`). One extra row at
turn ~15, emitted on day 29 alone and offering the shed, drains the pile before
it reaches 100. **Bound: 446 coins (band) / 130 (ymg) of destroyed units, plus
LOT-DEPTH §2's town-tick gain of +46…+163 on the volume it moves earlier —
call it 500-600 on the band, at the promotion bar and not over it on its own.**
It also costs ~6 % of simulator throughput on every day of training, which is
why it should be bundled behind (a') rather than run first.

Not recommended: ordering the day-29 return legs so the big loads drop first.
The binding constraint is total capacity (103.6 units offered into 100), not
order, so no permutation saves more than a unit or two.
