## 2. THE AGENT — what it actually is

One file, no training, no data. Structurally it is a **frozen action tape plus 30-odd
reactive layers stacked as decorators**: each block does `_X_PARENT = agent` and then
redefines `agent(observation, configuration)` to call its parent and patch the result.
Every layer is wrapped in `try/except` and falls back to the parent's action; the
chassis falls back to the raw tape; the entry point falls back to `PASS`. Nothing it
does can crash the seat. Telemetry dicts are threaded through every layer.

### 2.1 The tape and how a game selects one (`v45_main.py:944-968`)

The tape carries **everything physical**: which tile is planted with what on which turn,
hires (`['HIRE']` market orders), `BUY_LAND`, animals, `FEED`, `FERTILIZE`, `PICKUP`,

(a) repair the tape when the world does not match it, (b) move *when* things are sold,
and (c) fund purchases.

### 2.2 The chassis repair layers (`406-908`)

Shipped config is `_SETTINGS` at `948`: `hand_align`, `weed_repair`, `sell_lead` **on**;
`budget_guard`, `room_guard`, `clamp_sells`, `dead_stock`, `terminal_liquidation`,
`front_run` **off** (each was superseded by a later, better-guarded wrapper).

* **`_hand_align`** (507) pads/truncates the tape's hand list to the real headcount.
* **`_weed_repair`** (517) if a `PLANT`/`BUILD_*` target is a WEED, `DIG` now and **queue
  the intended action**, replaying it on a later step when that unit's tape action would
  be a no-op; a `PLANT` is only replayed when the unit's next tape op is not a move, so
  the mandatory same-day `WATER` can still follow. A no-op turn standing on a weed also
  becomes `DIG`. This is their **idle-turn recovery**.
* **`_projected_shed`** (565) shed contents after this turn's unit ops but *before* the
  market runs — the quantity every sell layer is clamped against.
* **`_sell_lead`** (631) the base sale-pre-emption: sell next step's planned lots **now**
  and suppress them next step, `"when step % 4 != 0 (no town consumption between the two
  steps)"`, never across a shop-unlock boundary (`nxt % 72 == 0`), never WHEAT/FERTILIZER,
  never if that product is already being sold this turn.
* **`_apply_suppression`** (601) removes the pre-sold quantity from the later turn and
  **keeps the zero-quantity order**: `"A zero-quantity order keeps later market race
  slots intact."` (see §2.5 — order index is a resource).
* `_clamp_sells` (855) documents the same thing for buys: `"Removing empty orders would
  change the later lockstep market races."`
* `_front_run` (662) is a dormant hook: given the opponent's expected tape, sell
  MILK/WOOL/STRAWBERRY/MELON one step before they do. **Off** — they have no opponent tape
  at runtime; their equivalent is inferred instead (§2.4).

### 2.3 The sale clock — a five-rung horizon ladder

`_r36_reserve` (1660) is the real sale engine. For steps 216-696 it walks its **own future
tape** from `step+1` to `min(695, step+H, end-of-72-block)`, sums each product's planned
`SELL` quantities, sells that much **now** out of projected stock, and books a `debt` so
`_r36_suppress` (1648) subtracts it from the tape's later orders. It breaks the scan at a
future `PICKUP` or `BUY_PRODUCT` of the same item, skips WHEAT/FERTILIZER, skips price<2,
skips items already traded this turn, and stops at 10 orders.

The horizon `H` escalates:

| rung | condition | H | code |
|---|---|---|---|
| base | always | 2 | `1878` |
| similarity | `_r37_similarity ≥ .90` for 6 straight turns, steps 336-648 | 3 | `1881-1885` |
| cash probe | rival's cash change matches ours after an all-sale probe | 4 | `1888-1890`, `_r44_*` 1835-1857 |
| mid-game | steps 288-696, unconditional | 4 | `1892` |
| **V44 clone gate** | steps 216-696, hands+farmer identical to rival 4 of last 6 turns **and** similarity ≥ .95 | **8** | `3391-3397`, `3441-3460` |
| **V44 race lost** | plus: rival sold a race product at the turn our shed rose and we held | **24** | `3408-3425` |

### 2.4 Order-slot priority — sells first, and the most price-sensitive sell first

* `_v224_sales_first` (1478, active from step 144) bubbles every `SELL` toward index 0,
  stopping at another `SELL` or at a `BUY` of the same item. Earlier index = quoted
  against a lower inventory = a better price.
* `_r37_quote_priority` / `_r37_reorder_sales` (1781-1827, active from step 288) then ranks
  a contiguous block of distinct-item sells by **how much revenue the rival could take
  away**, not by headline value:

```python
batch = min(24, max(8, standing))          # standing = rival's VISIBLE ripe yield of that item
now   = sum(_r37_market_price(item, inventory+j)       for j in range(quantity))
later = sum(_r37_market_price(item, inventory+batch+j) for j in range(quantity))
return now-later
```

  The price curve `_r37_market_price` (1750) is a **verbatim copy of the engine's**
  (kaggle-environments 1.32.7, params for all nine goods inlined at `1730`), so every
  layer can price marginal units exactly.

### 2.5 The engine quirks they exploit (all of them stated in the source)

1. **Lockstep per-unit quoting across the two seats.** From the Evaluation cell:
   *"The engine quotes each unit of both players' same-index orders at the same inventory
   and commits both; a large buy at index 0 followed by a large sell at index 1 therefore
   leaves a rival's index-1 purchase quoted at a scarcer, flat inventory."* The same model
   appears twice in the code as a cost bound:

```python
def _r127_prefix_bound(obs,quantity):        # 2989
    inventory=int(obs['market']['inventory']['WHEAT'])
    # Both players quote one unit before either commits. Before own unit j,
    # at most j-1 own and j-1 opponent wheat purchases have depleted inventory.
    return sum(_r37_market_price('WHEAT',inventory-(2*j-1)) for j in range(1,quantity+1))
```

   and for the sell side in `_r128_sale_credit` (3057): `sum(price(item, inventory+2*j) …)`.
2. **A SELL's proceeds fund a later BUY in the same turn.** `_r128_credit_supply` (3158)
   adds that lower-bound credit to `money` and re-runs the funding check, so a buy that
   looks unaffordable at the start of the turn is issued anyway.
3. **Atomic same-crop PLANT cancellation.** `"Removing rejected requests can unblock the
   engine's atomic crop batch"` (2972) — if more PLANTs of one crop are requested than
   there are seeds, the engine drops the *whole* batch. `_r148_atomic` (3244) PASSes exactly
   the invalid requests so the fundable ones survive. **Shipped OFF** (`_R148_SEEDS=False`).
4. **Dawn deposit destroys the ordered suffix of carried cargo.** At hour 23 every unit's
   inventory is pushed into the shed and overflow is lost *in deposit order*.
   `_r148_overflow` (3206) sells exactly the shed items that the doomed suffix would
   replace, so the post-dawn warehouse vector is **bit-identical** to the unmodified plan
   while the destroyed units are converted to cash. **Shipped ON** (`_R148_OVERFLOW=True`).
5. **Last-hour plants that cannot be watered are wasted seed.** `_r127_last_hour` (2967)
   re-simulates and drops any hour-23 `PLANT` that does not end the turn `watered_today`.
6. **Weed randomness is drawn per empty tile of *both* farms before the shop draw**, so any
   first-turn change re-rolls the shop lottery (Evaluation cell) — the same finding as our
   `shop-lottery` memory, arrived at independently.

### 2.7 Everything else, one line each

* **Terminal (712-718)** `_shadow_terminal` (1025) rolls the frozen tape forward 7 steps in a
  shadow chassis; if the shadow reproduces the live action exactly, a bounded search
  (`plan_terminal`, `max_simulations=64`, `proposals_per_actor=4`, 1 pass) re-plans the last
  seven callbacks for physical delivery, with abort/recover guards; step 718 is always the
  full liquidation `_parent_liquidate` (1013) = `DROP` everything shed-adjacent and `SELL`
  the whole projected shed, ordered by `price*qty` descending.
* **Room guard** (1110) at hour 23: if shed + carried > 99, sell the excess highest-price-first.
* **Idle avoidance** `_r128_service` (3108): a unit whose tape op is `PASS` while standing on
  the shed ring picks up exactly the WHEAT its next four turns of `FEED` need
  (`_r128_food_need` 3096); a `FEED` with an empty bag and a `PICKUP` next turn is turned into
  `PICKUP q+1` now and the FEED deferred by one turn (`service_swaps`).
* **Grain supply** `_r97_supply` (2786): guarantees the wheat a future `PICKUP` needs exists,
  by first *shrinking an existing wheat SELL* and only then enlarging/adding a BUY, under a
  deliberately pessimistic price bound (`_r37_market_price(p, inventory-2000)`, "at most ten
  100-unit purchases per opponent turn").
* **Feed economics** `_r85_feed` (2525, days 10-28): skip a `FEED` when
  `bonus*(price(product)+5)*1.25 < price(WHEAT)` and the animal is fed again in time
  (`_r86_next_feed`) — i.e. feed only when the marginal animal product beats the grain.
* **Fertilizer** `_r85_fertilizer` (2577) sells fertilizer above a reserve computed by backward
  recurrence over the remaining tape; `_r79` (2498) gates tomato fertilizer on
  `bonus*price ≥ 2*(10*fert + extra_labour) + 100`; `_r51_input_control` (2198) hires **extra**
  workers on days 12-28 for a fertilizer tour only when `value ≥ 1.5*cost + 50` and ≥3,000 cash
  remains.
* **Late labour** `_r53_labor_assignment` (2347) on days 26-28 hires 2-3 harvest workers and
  solves the spawn-to-row assignment by permutation under the remaining-turns bound.
* Investments: `_v219` finite late tomato (1224+), `_v233/_v234` financed six-sheep SE expansion
  (1943+), `_v231` bounded cattle substitution (1530+) — each with its own qualification test,

## 4. VERDICT

### The three worth testing, ranked against a ladder that is 90 %+ this notebook's clones

1. **Sell-slot priority inside our existing lot rows (#3).** The one mechanism we lack
   that costs *nothing* to try: same rows, same turns, same quantities, only the index
   order changes, and the engine's own rule — *"the engine quotes each unit of both
   players' same-index orders at the same inventory and commits both"* — makes index
   order a contested resource whenever the rival sells the same product on the same
   turn. Against a ladder that is 90 % one tape, we know exactly which products they
   sell and roughly when. **Test:** add `SELL_SLOT_PRIORITY_ON=False` next to
   `DEFAULT_ORDER` (`plan.py:3861`), rank the row by our own version of
   `now − later` using the rival's *visible* ripe yield, take the `assert_no_cross`
   exemption `OPEN_PUMP` already has (`plan.py:1796-1803`), screen on
   `S/simscreen/screen.py`, then `WORKERS=8 bash S/judge7065/run_eng22.sh` (44 rows,
   ~3 min) and `run_band180.sh` (158 rows, ~4-8 min) with the switch appended to `SW`.
   Expected size: small but positive-sum-free — it moves price, not volume, so read
   **margin**; anything under +300 at t ≥ 2 in sim kills it before an engine leg.

2. **The hour-23 doomed-suffix overflow row (#4).** The only mechanism in V45 that is a
   genuine self-gain rather than a displacement, and the only one with a measured ceiling
   on *our* boards: **21.6 units a game-seat destroyed, 15.4 at the nightly dump, ≈1,272
   coins** (`SHED-CLIP`). `2026-09-16-lot4.md` closed turns 14 and 21; the dump turn
   itself was never tested, and their instrument is different from LOT4 (exact suffix,
   invariant post-dawn shed, not a price-driven lot). **Test:** `SHED_DUMP_ROW_ON=False`,
   sized from the projected carried suffix, a market row on turn 23 only on days where
   the projection says the deposit overflows; sim-screen the units-destroyed delta first
   (it must recover ≫ 0.07 units or it is LOT4 again), then BAND180 reading margin.
   **Two-purse warning:** LOT4 bought +308 coins and gave the tape seat +1,139 of the
   same shelf.

3. **The public-market rival-sale detector (#5), as a tell, not as a lever.** `_race_town`
   + `_race_lost` recover the opponent's sales from `market.inventory` deltas minus a
   modelled town tick, with no future tape and no private data. Our only turn-time
   opponent read is `OPEN_PUMP_TELL_KEEP0`, whose tell **cannot see a same-turn round
   trip** (`nbintel` §6.1, confirmed to the unit in `v45leg` §3 — the pot reads a one-unit
   draw against a 70-unit racer). Rebuilding the tell on inventory deltas fixes the one
   defect the archive has already proven. **Test:** telemetry-only first — replay 20 band
   boards in sim and check the inferred rival sales match the known tape; no engine leg,
   no coins claimed. Only then does it become a gate for a reactive arm.
