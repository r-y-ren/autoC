## 2. Cost of a generation and its signal

- Plain gen **55.4 s** = 512 members × 160 episodes (147 pinned boards + 13 drawn) = 81,920 episodes → 1,480 ep/s on a 3090. Pinned rungs are 92 % of the episode budget and ≈ 94.6 % of wall clock (71,355 of 75,467 s).
- abs probe every 10 gens +13 s (68.3 s); record-nominee replicate gens 94–98 s (+40 s). Together ≈ 2,660 s = 3.5 %.
- Real gate: CPU workers in parallel with training — 120 games in 217–430 s (median 222 s), verdict 7–9 gens later; **≈ 0 % of GPU time**. Three blocking anomalies (gens 1/10/1200: 552/944/474 s) = 1.9 %.
- Estimator: fitness per member = mean over 160 episodes of sigmoid(margin/3000) (+ own-coin score on tape episodes), rank-normalised to [−0.5, 0.5] with tied ranks averaged, antithetic difference, `@ eps / (pop·sigma)`, then Adam lr 0.003. Adam takes |step| ≈ 0.22·lr per coordinate with or without signal (plateau-review measurement; the docstring's noise-floor random walk), so the centre moves ≈ 0.0007/coordinate/gen = one sigma per ~30 gens: 30 gradients are averaged into every sigma of movement.
- Signal per generation: with `--pinned-once --shop-crn` a member's fitness on the 147 boards is deterministic — the only noise is the perturbation draw. Half-split gradient Spearman: 0.96 at 160 pinned episodes, 0.95 at 384 (drawn boards without shop-crn needed E ≥ 512 for 0.83). In-run: mean_win drift/residual-sd per 100 gens 9.7 (gens 1–100), 7.5, 2.7, 3.6, 2.4 … 0.4–1.2 after g900 (residual sd 0.005–0.009). One update is far above noise for ~500 gens and ≈ 1 sd/100 gens after g900 — the fixed-board objective flattening, after its transfer to the engine had already stopped.
- `--best-margin 0.005` = 0.14 SE of a read (audit): in-sim nominations after g1000 are max-of-noise picks, which is why their gate reads came in below the periodic ones' own noise.
