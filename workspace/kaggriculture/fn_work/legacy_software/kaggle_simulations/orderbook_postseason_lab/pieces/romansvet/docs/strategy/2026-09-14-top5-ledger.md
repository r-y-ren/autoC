## 7. Conclusion — the coins

The −13,722 (the ymg_aq half of the quoted −12,599; the DSM half is undecomposed) is carried by
three lines, all of them in d15-29:

1. **WHEAT −6,619** — *(i) their extra production.* They net-sell 332 units of own wheat to our 174,
   off 16–19 wheat tiles against our 8.5–9.5. The 1,145-unit / 42,453-coin buy leg is price-neutral
   churn (37.1 in, 37.4 out) and is **not** where the coins are; the tiles are. A ~3-coin/unit dent in
   our own realised wheat price (34 here vs 37 against the clone, ≈ −950) is the only denial term.
2. **WOOL −5,659** — *(iii) our production under-sold, plus (ii) price.* We hold **more** sheep at d20
   (6.5 vs 5.8) and still sell 13 fewer units (121.7 vs 134.7) at 177 against their 202. They have
   23.3 units away in d0-9 before our first 5.0. This is the one line with the **same sign against
   both opponent classes** (−1,732 / −4,725 / −5,659): a B-side defect, not a class effect.
3. **STRAWBERRY −3,131, a 9.6k swing off the clone baseline** — *(ii) our price denied.* Against the
   clone strawberry is our single largest surplus (+6,434 / +7,138); against a second strawberry-first
   grower (26.4 straw tiles at d10 vs our 22.8) our line falls from 34,134 (248.9 u @ 137) to 21,464
   (205.2 u @ 105) — 44 fewer units **and** 32 coins less each.

Milk (−3,005) and carrot (−751, from +3,278) are secondary. Melon is **not** a driver: their season
melon total is the same as the clone's (16,584 vs 17,673), only moved out of d10-14 into d15-29.

**Caveat:** TOPB2/LIVE-C and TOPB3 are different board sets, so the cross-set comparisons in §3/§7 are
board-confounded. TOPB2 and LIVE-C agree with each other closely on our own book (strawberry 34.1k/35.1k
@ 137, wheat 315.9/333.7 u @ 37), which is the only evidence offered that the TOPB3 differences are
opponent-driven rather than board-driven — **not a controlled test**.
