## 3. Does λ=2 price the gift? — yes, and that is the finding
λ=1 does not: `dTHEIRS` +676 at t 6.85 is 70 % of GATE260's +963, so a single unit of paired baseline
barely dents the shared-pot channel. λ=2 does: `dTHEIRS` collapses **+676 → +116**, a 5.8× cut, and the t
drops 6.85 → 2.05. This is the first time in the ACTIONRL line that a trained head has come back
essentially gift-free against the incumbent. The price is our own coin: `dOURS` goes +423 → **−7**, i.e. at
λ=2 the policy pays for the whole gift reduction out of our own purse, and margin is still **−123…−138**
(t ≈ −2) below `head_940`. The gift term is therefore a working control — it moves dTHEIRS monotonically
in λ — but at λ=2 it has slid past the optimum into pure suppression rather than differential advantage.

The one column that flips sign is the decisive one for rating: **board wins 175 → 179 (+4 net flips) vs
the incumbent** and 174 → 179 (+5) vs head-off, with a favourable 137/96 better/worse split against
head-off. Under "rating is wins" (JOINTFREE) that is a real signal, but it is 4 boards of 233 with
`signp` 0.29 — not separable from noise, and it sits on a negative margin, so it does not clear the bar as
written and must not be shipped on the win column alone.

**NEXT (do not re-run λ ∈ {1,2}):** the useful λ is between the two — sweep λ ≈ 1.4–1.6 where dOURS is
still positive and dTHEIRS is already flat — or drop the two-term reward for a direct **margin** objective
(ours − theirs), which is what λ→large degenerates toward anyway. Re-gate on BAND2-233 with the same
two banked bases, and require the win flip to hold at ≥ +8 boards before it counts as evidence.
