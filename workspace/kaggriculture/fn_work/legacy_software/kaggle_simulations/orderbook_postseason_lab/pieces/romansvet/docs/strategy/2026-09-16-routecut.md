# ROUTECUT — the block ASSIGNMENT (`_cut`), measured and CLOSED by a theorem

2026-09-16 21:48–22:30Z, branch `routecut` off `d131961` (FT2 + ROUTEEFF +
ROUTEORDER). Follow-up to `2026-09-16-routeorder.md` §6, which named the
remaining 86 % of `mid_move` as the block **assignment** rather than the
block's visiting order. Legs: ENG22 (22 engine tapes, both seats, 1 seed = 44
games), instrument on ENG22 + TOPLEG. No training, no upload, remote untouched.

## 1. VERDICT

**The assignment saving that `_cut` can express is not small — it is
provably ≤ 0.** The gate-SAFE *ceiling* is large (+10,195/board, of which
+1,729 is assignment beyond the reorder), but every coin of it needs tiles to
move **into the middle of another unit's block**, and `_cut` hands out
**contiguous prefixes**: the only thing it can move is the boundary. A boundary
move is bounded by the triangle inequality and can never buy walk:

    gain(c) = [base(e+1) - move[e+1]] - [base(c+1) - move[c+1]] - (cu[e] - cu[c])
            <= - sum_{c < j <= e} n_ops[j]   <  0

because the leg the next unit walks in from its **spawn** to rank `c+1` is
already no longer than the hops it would otherwise have walked to reach it.
The switch built here (`ROUTE_CUT_ON`) is therefore **byte-identical to the
shipped planner on all 44 ENG22 games** at look-back 3 and 12 with the shed
gate, and at look-back 8 **without** it — it is the **price**, not the gate,
that closes this lever. Taking the boundary on the entry term alone (the first
arm, before the stranded turns were priced) reads **ENG22 −14,987/board,
t −11.61, board win 40.9 → 13.6 %, flips +0/−6**: the boundary is not slack,
it is load-bearing. **The route-walk family is CLOSED on the assignment side.**

## 2. How the block start/end and the shed turn are decided today (`plan.py:_routes`)

1. **Start.** `start` is a running cursor: unit `u` takes `s = e_{u-1} + 1`
   (:10357, :10957) — blocks are contiguous prefixes of the day's ranked
   `order` by law, and the unit walks in from its own **spawn** (`base_move`,
   :10365), not from the previous block's last tile.
2. **End.** `_cut` (:10478) takes the **largest** rank whose block — and, on a
   drop day, its return leg `home` — fits the turn budget: `L(e) = base +
   cum[e] − cum[s] + max(D(s,e), land_lead) <= bud`. Nothing looks at the
   walk; the boundary falls wherever the budget runs out. The excursions
   (`MELON_OPEN`, `BANK_BEFORE_LOT`) re-cut against `bud − res`, never at a
   different *kind* of cut.
3. **Shed turn.** PICKUPs are the block's **lead**, charged before rank `s`, so
   they sit at the route base; the DROP is the return leg after rank `e`, at
   `start_u + cu[e] + DIST_SHED[e]`; a mid-day PLACE/DROP is priced at the rank
   it fires on. So the shed turn is a function of **`e` and the block's own
   clock** — which is why the assignment, not the sweep, owns it.

## 3. The gate-SAFE ceiling (`S/routecut/probe.py`, `cut_ceiling.py`, 22 ENG22 boards)

Same probe as ROUTEORDER (ordered op path of every unit-day, real engine, both
seats) plus the **turn index** of every step. Each unit-day is cut at its shed
interactions into SLOTS with a fixed start, a fixed end and a **turn window
read off the real turns**. Pinning the window *is* "the shed visit stays at the
same turn": a unit may always idle (tail_pass 1.37/unit-day, tail_move 0.00),
so a shorter walk never pulls an anchor earlier. A tile whose chain consumes a
carried input (PLANT / FEED / FERTILIZE) may not leave its own unit-day; every
other tile may move. Priced exactly like ROUTEEFF/ROUTEORDER (spot on the
landing day, our impact ON, seed on the PLANT share, our purse, nothing past
d29). The model reproduces the engine's walk to the step: **10.17 / 10.17
moves per unit-day**.

The **assignment's own** gate-safe increment over the reorder is **+1,729/board
adjacent-unit, +3,134/board any-unit** — both far over the +450 bar, which is
why the switch was built. **But the increment is tile-level**: it moves a tile
out of the *middle* of one block into the *middle* of another. That is not a
boundary, and `_cut` has no other handle.

## 4. The switch — `ROUTE_CUT_ON` (default OFF), plan.py:2426 (constants), :10639 (the recut)

Per unit, after the cut and the excursions, pull the block's end back by at
most `ROUTE_CUT_LOOK` (3) ranks when that hands the **next** unit a cheaper
entry. The crew's whole walk for a day is

    sum_{j<=E} move[j]  +  sum_u base_move_u  -  sum_{u>=1} move[s_u]

— every hop of the ranked order is walked once **except** the hop into each
block's first rank, which the unit replaces by the leg from its spawn — so for
fixed coverage the boundary owns exactly `base_move_u - move[s_u]`, and nothing
today looks at it. Gates kept: no excursion on the block (`ins == 0`), a
successor to hand the ranks to (`n_units > u + 1`), the shed gate in its
strongest form (`hf_dist[c] <= hf_dist[e]`, and `c <= e` on a monotone clock,
so every shed interaction lands on the same turn **or sooner**), and the
**price**: the recut is refused unless the entry saving exceeds `cu[e] − cu[c]`,
the turns this block strands.

`tests/test_routecut.py` (11 pass): whole-plan sha256 OFF-identity against
`git archive d131961 src` via `tests/_pin.py`; pickup-before-use on the emitted
route; the shed-visit invariant (no shed op lands later than OFF) on a fixed
state with and without a drop day; ON-inertness; the mutual-exclusion assert;
jit; and **the theorem of §1 on 200 random geometries**.

## 6. Standing

* **`_cut`'s contiguity is the binding law, not its greed.** The gate-safe
  assignment ceiling is real (+1,729 adjacent / +3,134 any-unit over the
  reorder) but it is a **tile-level allocator** — a unit taking a tile out of
  the middle of its neighbour's block — and no boundary move can reach it.
  Anything that would is a new assignment mechanism (a per-tile unit key, not a
  prefix cut), i.e. a rewrite of `_routes`, not a switch.
* **The route-walk family is closed on both halves**: the visiting order
  (ROUTEORDER: 9 % of the term, ENG22 +433 t 2.74 / V45 −175, ships OFF) and
  the assignment (here: ≤ 0 by theorem, −14,987 when taken anyway).
* `ROUTE_CUT_ON` ships **OFF** and is pinned byte-identical to `d131961`. It is
  kept as the record of the measurement, not as a candidate.
