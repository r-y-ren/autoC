# Where the top five's extra 8-15k per game actually sits — price, volume, mix or cadence

2026-09-16. Tools `S/pricevol/` (README-equivalent: §9 below has every command). A
price / volume / product-mix / sell-cadence decomposition of the same 117 live replays
`docs/strategy/2026-09-16-pop-shift.md` profiled, plus the first **paired** per-product
ledger of B against the current top five (TOP5NOW). All CPU, no training, no remote host,
no planner change.

## 0. VERDICT

**The extra coins are not extra units and they are not a better product mix. They are
price per unit, and the largest single reachable piece of that price is the intra-turn
price walk B pays for selling its whole day in two market phases.**

* **The single largest reachable line: +5,382 coins/game, the burst walk (§4.3).**
  The engine prices a SELL order unit by unit and adds +1 to the shared market inventory
  after every unit, so the n-th unit of a burst is quoted off an inventory n−1 higher than
  the first. B sells **9.73 units per burst** and forfeits **7,936 coins/game** between the
  first unit's quote and what it actually takes. The TOP-NEW class sells **3.73 units per
  burst** and forfeits **2,554**. At B's own production, on B's own boards, matching their
  burst size is worth **+5,382 coins/game** — and it is an upper bound, not a promise.
* **The mechanism that would have to change:** B resolves a market row on 6 turns of 24
  (`sim/rollout.py:MARKET_TURNS = (0,1,2,3,10,18)`, `core/ops.py:SELL_TURNS = (3,10,18)`)
  and in practice sells on **four** (t1 64 % of units, t18 34 %, t10 2 %, t3 0.2 %), while
  the engine allows 10 orders on all 24 turns (`maxMarketOrdersPerTurn`, and `action` has
  three independent channels `farmer` / `hands` / `market`), so **selling out of the shed
  costs no unit-op**: the overnight lot is already in the shed and can be split across the
  turns B owns for free. Only pushing the *same day's* harvest out later needs DROP ops.
  It is an action-interface change, exactly where
  `docs/strategy/2026-09-16-es-plateau.md` already pointed.
* **The size of the market that has to absorb those bursts (§4.4):** with the towns these
  117 games actually drew, the town eats **4.3 wheat / 3.5 strawberry / 2.5 milk / 1.8 wool
  units per shop tick** in d15-29. B pushes **18.2 wheat / 15.0 strawberry / 9.6 milk /
  7.6 wool** through a single tick. Melon (town demand **1.0 unit/day**) and fertilizer
  (**0**) have no town bid at all, which is why B realises 0.64× and 0.52× base on them.
* **Volume and mix are not the gap.** B already realises **85.5 coins/unit**, TOP-NEW
  **85.2**, SINGLETON **85.4** — B's average unit is worth *more* than the top five's,
  because they put their extra units into cheap wheat and carrot. Per product, the
  TOP-NEW−B gross gap of **+11,472** splits **volume +4,679 / price +9,245 / interaction
  −2,452**; re-cut as scale/mix/price it is **scale +11,857 / mix −7,179 / price +6,793**.
  In the **paired** frame (§5) the same split is **volume +1,258 / price +15,986 /
  interaction −3,438** — on identical boards the top five sell 262 more units at a *lower*
  average price (83.7 vs our 89.5) and still take +13,805 more gross coins, entirely by
  getting more for each unit of a given product.
* **Not reachable** (§7): TOP-NEW's **+90 planted tiles** and **77 carrot tiles** need the
  third quadrant by d10 and the labour to work it, and `docs/strategy/2026-09-09-board-fill.md`
  already priced that at **−5.6 to −6.9k** of our purse because labour binds;
  SINGLETON's **189 tomato units off 31 tomato tiles + 217 fertilize ops** is a different
  build, not a sell schedule. Neither is a cadence change.
* **Paired reality check (§5):** on the 25 TOP5NOW tapes, retention keeps **14 boards**;
  on those B is **28.6 %** win, **−8,125 coins/board** (t −1.32). In the sim frame (10
  boards where sim == engine) **−8,148** (t −2.09), win 30 %. The per-product paired ledger
  puts the losses on **CARROT −5,131, WHEAT −4,501, TOMATO −3,749, MILK −2,586** against
  **STRAWBERRY +8,068**.

**One more negative result worth as much as the positive one:** the "sell on 421 turns"
headline is *not* itself worth coins. Regressing realised price on the units sold that day,
the day of the season and a class dummy (§4.5), TOP-NEW's price-at-equal-volume advantage
over B is **−7,248 coins/game — negative**. Selling on more turns pays only through the
burst size it permits, not because those hours are richer.

### 4.2 Why turn 1 is nevertheless the right *hour*

Turn 1 is the first market phase after the turn-0 tick, when the town centre has just eaten
one of every product and every unlocked shop has eaten its own. B is already on the best
hour of the grid. **What it gets wrong is how much it puts through it.**

### 4.3 The burst walk — the number in the verdict

+1 inventory per unit sold, the last unit of a burst is quoted lower than the first.
`lost = Σ(n × first price − coins actually taken)`, per game:

mechanism's reach, in that it ignores the compounding of a higher price on the next day's
quote.

**At the same product, the same day of the season and the same daily volume, TOP-NEW does
not out-price B — it under-prices B by 7,248 coins/game.** Its apparent price advantage in
§3.1 is therefore composition, not a per-unit edge on a matched cell: a different board
pool (their ~3,000-rated opponents leave a different shared inventory behind), plus
curvature in the day term the linear control cannot absorb. The two products where it genuinely out-prices
B at equal volume are strawberry (+28.2/unit, t +5.7) and milk (+13.1, t +2.9); the
enormous negative on melon is B's d20 melon meeting their d10 melon with the day controlled
for. SINGLETON's +8,036 is essentially one line, strawberry at +72.2/unit.

Note the positive `units` slopes: at day resolution, a day with *more* units sold shows a
*higher* price for six of nine products. That is reverse causation, not a discount-free
market — a seat sells more on the days the harvest lands, and the harvest lands on days the
shops have been eating. It is exactly why the day-level regression cannot see the
within-turn walk of §4.3, and why the burst is the right unit of analysis.

## 7. Verdict: what is reachable and what is not

**Reachable, largest first**

1. **The burst walk: +5,382 coins/game.** Mechanism to change: B resolves a market row on 6
   turns of 24 and sells on 4, so its whole overnight lot goes through one market phase at
   9.73 units a burst against a town that eats 1.8-4.3 units of a product per tick — the
   fix is more, smaller market rows out of the shed, which cost no unit-op and no labour,
   not more production.
2. **Strawberry, milk and wool inside that number: +4,020 of the +5,382.** Mechanism: these
   three lines are 5,839 of B's 7,936 forfeited coins because B sells 15.0 / 9.6 / 7.6 units
   of them per burst into a 3.5 / 2.5 / 1.8-unit-per-tick bid.
3. **The turn-1 dump: an in-product price gap of 83 → 171 on strawberry, 98 → 163 on
   tomato, 104 → 188 on milk between B's own turns.** Mechanism: the overnight lot is cut
   once and sold once; splitting one lot across t1/t10/t18 is a routing change inside the
   turns B already owns, before any new market turn exists.

**Not reachable with our production**

* **TOP-NEW's +90 planted tiles and its 77 carrot tiles.** They are bought with the third
  quadrant by d10 (3.0 quadrants at d10 vs the clone's 2.0, coins at d10 down to 7.6k), and
  `docs/strategy/2026-09-09-board-fill.md` has already measured that filling to the top's
  plantings costs us **5.6-6.9k** because labour binds; `docs/strategy/2026-09-14-lot-depth.md`
  says the same about our lot walk. The +4,679 of volume in §3.1 is not available at our
  labour.
* **SINGLETON's 189 tomato units / 31 tomato tiles / 217 fertilize ops / 8.4 geese.** A
  different build with 6,351 coins/game more non-market spend; §3.2's +11,594 tomato line
  is a production decision taken on day 0, not a selling decision.
* **Melon and fertilizer price.** Town demand is 1.0 and 0.0 units/day. B's 0.64× and 0.52×
  base on those lines is the market's shape, not B's schedule; the melon *timing* tax
  (d10-14 −9,036 paired) is a separate, already-named family.
* **Bought-wheat volume.** CLONE's +311 units over B are bought wheat re-sold at a 2.2-coin
  margin (321 units bought at 35.7, 533 sold at 37.9) and the class still ends on 97,679 —
  the same purse as B. Volume as such is not the mechanism, which is the cleanest control
  in the whole census: the class that adds the most units gains nothing.

No ES arm is proposed here. Per `docs/strategy/2026-09-16-es-plateau.md` the next step is
the action interface, and §4.3/§4.4 give it a target and a size.
