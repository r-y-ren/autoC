## 6. Why the judge and the live counterfactual disagree: the deviating arm wins on an open-loop tape
- **The judge direction.** The tape is recorded against PFS, and V56 is the arm that deviates. FIREBANK1/LIVEVAL1 found V56 gains there: for example, live faithful 13 seats 3 -> 5.
- **This read, the reverse direction.** The tape is recorded against V56, and PFS is the arm that deviates. PFS gains here: faithful 9 vs 6, with 0 seats going the other way.
- **Decomposition.** Suppose a symmetric bonus b goes to whichever arm deviates from the tape's recorded opponent, and t is the true fire effect. Then:
  - judge = t + b = +3.30 expected on these 13 faithful seats;
  - live read = t - b = -3.
  - This gives t ≈ **+0.15** flips / 13 fired seats (≈ 0) and b ≈ +3.15.
  - Caveat: this is an inference, and the judge rows are on different seats.
- **What it means.** The band's +28 flips (z 6.2) is almost all open-loop deviation bonus: the rival tape keeps the plan it made against the recorded opponent, and a different line of ours exploits it. It is not a live edge.
- **The mechanism in the purses.** Live V56 denies the rival -3.8k (t -2.08) but loses -6.0k of our own purse (t -3.52). The judge's "denial + own gain" pattern does not hold once the rival has actually reacted to V56.
