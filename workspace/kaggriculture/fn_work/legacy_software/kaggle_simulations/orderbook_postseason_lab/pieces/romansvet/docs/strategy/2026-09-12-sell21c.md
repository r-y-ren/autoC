**Code:** `S/sell21c/` (`sell21c.patch`, `README.md`, `test_inert.sh`, `test_split.py`,
`run_screen.sh`, `pair.py`, `run_legs.sh`, `time64.sh`, logs and csvs).
**Tree:** `/root/tree_sell21c` (a copy of `/root/tree_sell21` = the pristine `arms-next`
**branch head** + `sell21.patch`; the patch in this directory is the diff against that tree).
**Priced by:** `docs/strategy/2026-09-11-timing-prize.md` Result 2 / §Judgement.

## Why variant (c)

`KAGG3_SELL21` **moved** lot 3 from turn 18 to turn 21 and screened **−1,134/game**
(t −8.87) on 120 LIVE-C hold-out boards. The seat split named the missing term: **our**
purse rose **+249**/game, exactly the direction and roughly the size the first-order sweep
predicted, while the **opponent's** rose **+1,383** (t +16.7, 116/120 boards). Our ~531
units at turn 18 were depressing the price the other seat sells into; vacate that turn and
they get an undepressed market. Turn 18 is **denial**, and the denial is 5.5× the price
window.

Variant (c) is the only member of the sell-timing family that measurement leaves standing
(`sell21-build.md` 212-220; consensus §103 explicitly re-opens it): **keep** lot 3 on turn
18 with its full denial volume, **add** a fourth lot on turn 21, and let that lot carry only
the marginal late units of the three thin-market goods the h18→h21 sweep priced as the whole
prize — **WOOL +523/game, MILK +373, STRAWBERRY +284**. WHEAT, CARROT and FERTILIZER are
*negative* at h21 and stay on turn 18 whole.

and not a `plan.*` attribute for `SELL21`'s reason: `sim/rollout.py` freezes `_LOT_TURNS`,
`_SELL_ONLY_TURNS` and `MARKET_TURNS` at *its* import, so a runner that flips an attribute
after import would move the planner's row and not the simulator's market resolution).

**Neither name may enter a switch string** — `S/drainpin/on2b.py` asserts every switch name
against `plan` and would raise; a leg that forgets the prefix silently runs the OFF build
and looks perfectly level.

### The two laws in `core/sell.py`

1. **The greedy may never *choose* turn 21.** `adjusted_marginals` subtracts `2**24` from the
   late lot's row, so `argmax` never lands there — inside `allocate`'s body and at
   `plan.py:6689`'s forced-overflow placement alike. A lot with zero units has `drop == 0`
   exactly, so it adds nothing to the earlier lots' externality: **lots 1-3 see precisely the
   three-lot arithmetic.** Letting the allocator bid freely into a fourth lot would be
   `KAGG3_SELL21` with extra steps — it is the allocator *preferring* the late window that
   vacated turn 18 in the first place.
2. **The split is a deterministic post-step.** `_split_late` moves `floor(f · lot3[g])` for
   `g ∈ {WOOL, MILK, STRAWBERRY}` out of lot 3 into lot 4. Integer, floor-divided (int32 end
   to end, identical on numpy and JAX). `sum(lots, axis=0)` is bit-for-bit unchanged — the
   shed-overflow projection downstream reads that sum.

Forced-overflow units and the DROP's banked `gain` both stay on turn 18 by the same two laws,
deliberately: they are denial volume.

### The anchors — and the one difference from SELL21 that matters

`SELL21`'s open risk 1 was that it silently handed the day-29 crew **three extra route turns**
(`drop_turns` 17/16 → 20/19), because `plan.drop_turns` and the DROP-day `turn_budget` read
`O.SELL_TURNS[-1]` as the route's deadline. Its engine legs would have measured the price
window *and* a longer day together. Two new anchors in `core/ops.py` keep variant (c) a
**pure split**:

* `DROP_LOT` — the lot the route feeds: `len(SELL_TURNS) − 2` under SELL21C, `− 1` otherwise,
  i.e. **index 2 either way**;
* `DROP_DEADLINE_TURN = SELL_TURNS[DROP_LOT]` — **turn 18 either way**.

`plan.py`'s three route-deadline reads take `DROP_DEADLINE_TURN` and its four
`SELL.N_LOTS − 1` row selectors (DROP, MELON, MIDDAY_PLACE_V2, SAME_DAY_FERT) take
`SELL.DROP_LOT`. Both are identities when the switch is off, so the edit is inert by
construction. Measured, all four modes: `drop_turns(4) = 17`, `drop_turns(12) = 16`,
`early_lot_turns() = (3, 10, 18, 21)`, `MARKET_TURNS = (0,1,2,3,10,18,21)`.

## Unit tests — 62 PASS OFF; ON needed two anchors fixed *in the tests*

The three `SELL_TURNS`-sensitive suites (62 tests) against `/root/tree_sell21c`:

* **OFF: 62 passed.** This is the pass the inert claim rests on, and it is clean.
* **ON: a full no-`-x` inventory at mode 1 found exactly TWO failures across the 62**, both
  of them tests that hard-code the three-lot layout, neither a defect in the build. Both are
  now fixed in the tree copy and all three suites are green in all four modes.

**Failure 1 — `test_day29_endgame::test_day_29_harvests_walks_home_and_drops.`**
Asserts the DROP's banked tomato sells on `O.SELL_TURNS[-1]`, i.e. it encodes *"the day's
last lot is the lot the route feeds"* — precisely the invariant variant (c) breaks on
purpose. Checked directly on `P.build_day` with the test's own day-29 ripe view rather than
argued:

| | `SELL_TURNS` | turn 3 | turn 10 | **turn 18** | turn 21 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| OFF | (3, 10, 18) | 0 | 0 | **9 tomato** | — |
| mode 1 | (3, 10, 18, 21) | 0 | 0 | **9 tomato** | 0 |

Identical: same turn, same quantity. That is the behaviour the switch is designed to have,
and the reason the day-29 crew keeps its 17/16-turn budget.

**Failure 2 — `test_sell_side::test_forced_overflow_units_join_the_lot_the_allocator_chooses.`**
Asserts `adj[:, I_TOMATO].tolist() == [1, 1, 1]`, a hard-coded three-element list. Under
SELL21C it reads `[1, 1, 1, -16777215]`: **the three choosable lots are `[1, 1, 1]`,
byte-identical to OFF**, followed by the blocked lot's sentinel (`1 − 2**24`). The failure is
therefore positive evidence — it shows the block is in place and that the three real
marginals still tie at the $1 floor, so the tie still falls to the earliest lot and all 15
forced units still land in lot 1, which the rest of the test then asserts and passes.

**The fixes, in the tree copy only** (`.orig` kept beside each; the repo's `tests/` is
untouched): the five route/DROP references to `O.SELL_TURNS[-1]` become
`O.DROP_DEADLINE_TURN`, and the marginal assertion compares `adj[:n_choosable]` with
`n_choosable = S.N_LOTS if S.LATE_LOT < 0 else S.LATE_LOT` (plus a new positive assertion
that the late lot *is* blocked). Both are identities when the switch is off. Two of the five
day-29 anchors are load-bearing under SELL21C — the `range(O.SELL_TURNS[-1])` "nothing sold
before the route's lot" scans, which would otherwise sweep over turn 18 itself and see the
DROP's own sale.

**Result after the fixes:** `test_sell_side` 9/9 in OFF and modes 1, 2 and 3; the mode-1
inventory had already shown `test_day29_endgame` and `test_tape_flow_fidelity` green once
their anchor was corrected. **62/62 both ways.**

Any future arm on a four-lot day should expect the same class of edit: the suites encode
"last lot == the lot the route feeds == `N_LOTS − 1`" in several places, and that identity
is exactly what variant (c) separates.

**The ledger picked exactly the wrong three products.** Compare with `KAGG3_SELL21`, which
moved *all nine*: ours +249, theirs +1,383, Δ −1,134. Variant (c) at f = 1 moves **three**
and already gives up ours +90 / theirs +1,036, Δ −946 — **83 % of the whole SELL21 loss out
of 3 of the 9 products.** WOOL, MILK and STRAWBERRY are not the goods the opponent leaves
alone; they are the goods whose turn-18 volume most depresses the price the other seat
sells into. A thin market is thin **for both seats**: the same thinness that makes the
h18→h21 lift large for us makes our turn-18 units the thing holding their quote down.

And the effect is **dose-responsive in their purse and saturating in ours** — the cleanest
displacement signature the campaign has measured:

| f | Δ ours | Δ theirs | ratio | Δ margin (LIVE-C) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ¼ | +41 | +101 | 2.5× | −60 (t −1.90) |
| ½ | +83 | +384 | 4.6× | −301 (t −5.93) |
| 1 | +90 | +1,036 | **11.5×** | −946 (t −9.60) |

Our own purse is **already saturated at f = ¼**: doubling the moved volume twice buys us
+41 → +83 → +90, i.e. essentially nothing past the first quarter, while the opponent's
take grows 10× and super-linearly. The first few units carry the whole price-window prize;
everything after them is pure denial given away. That is the displacement signature
(`counterfactuals-overstate`), not a mispricing an ES could tune out.

### The one place it pays: the top tier

On TOPB2 the sign flips — our purse +310 / +563 against theirs +58 / +330, Δ **+252**
(t +3.20) and **+233** (t +1.73). Against the 2800+ tier our wool/milk at turn 18 is *not*
what is holding their price down (they are not selling those goods into that window in
volume), so the later window is nearly free and we keep most of it. Read as an observation
about the opponent class, not as a promotion case: TOPB2 is 40 boards and a floor, LIVE-C
hold-out 120 boards is the promotion set, and the §50 bar is |t| 2.5 board-level.

## Open risks

1. **Off-policy read.** Candidate B's `press` and `hold` genes were trained against a
   *three*-lot day whose last lot stands on turn 18. Under SELL21C they quote into a
   four-lot day, and `press * lot_index` now charges lot 3 an index-2 penalty while the
   turn-21 units pay nothing at all (the split bypasses the greedy). Nothing in B's theta
   prices the turn-21 window, so the screen below is a read of *the split's mechanical
   effect at fixed genes*, not of the best play available under the layout.
2. **The block is a modelling choice.** Blocking the greedy out of lot 4 is what makes the
   switch a pure split — but it also means the allocator never *declines* the late window
   when the curve says it should. A per-board fraction, or a learned one, is strictly
   better; `f` is a constant here precisely so the measurement has one degree of freedom.
3. **Three goods, flat over the season.** The sweep says every coin of the prize is in
   **d15-29** (d0-9 is exactly zero, d10-14 slightly negative). This switch splits on every
   day of the season, so a day-banded variant is strictly closer to the ledger.
4. **The fourth market resolution is not free**, unlike SELL21's (which kept the same six
   turns). See the throughput table.
5. **Switch-string trap** (inherited): `KAGG3_SELL21C` is an env prefix. A leg that puts it
   in the `"..."` string dies on `assert hasattr(plan, name)`; one that forgets it runs the
   OFF build and looks perfectly level.

## Would an ES arm on SELL21C be the fair test? — **No**

The off-policy objection (§90's reading 2) is the reason to ask, and it is half right: B's
`press`/`hold` genes were trained on a three-lot day. But the arm as dispatched cannot
settle anything:

* **The genes cannot reach the term that kills it.** `press` and `hold` price *our* curve.
  The loss is the **opponent's** curve rising when our turn-18 wool comes off it — it is
  not in our fitness gradient at fixed `f`, and no reservation the ES can learn makes the
  other seat sell into a lower quote. The ES can only recover the mispricing, and the
  mispricing is the +83…+90 we already get; the denial is +384…+1,036 against us.
* **`f` is not a gene.** The split fraction sits outside theta by construction (that is what
  makes the switch a pure split). An arm would train `press`/`hold` around a constant the
  measurement says should be **0 on LIVE-C and > 0 on TOPB2** — two opposite answers on the
  two opponent classes, which no single constant, and no gene that cannot see the opponent,
  can hold at once.
* **What the data actually asks for** is a per-opponent-class `f` — i.e. "is this seat
  selling wool into turn 18?" — which is an **opponent model**, not a sell-timing gene. That
  belongs with the gate work (`S/opgate/`), not with an ES arm on `SELL_TURNS`.

Recommendation: **do not stage a GPU arm on SELL21C.** Keep `/root/tree_sell21c` and the
patch; if the opponent-class read is ever built, the TOPB2 column (positive at all three
doses, +139 t 2.58 / +252 t 3.20 / +233 t 1.73, with the opponent's purse barely moving)
is the one cell of this table that was positive, and it is an argument for an opponent
gate, not for a sell-timing arm.

Also update the ES arm's premise: the `press * lot_index` term now charges lot 3 an index-2
penalty while the turn-21 units pay none at all, because the split bypasses the greedy. Any
future arm on a four-lot day should make `f` (or a per-product late-share) a **gene with a
measured decodable slope at the training sigma** (`gene-slope-check`) before it is launched,
or it will be a second dead zero-init gene.
