# A Holistic and Hybrid Service Selection Strategy for MEC-Based UAV Last-Mile Delivery Systems

Jia Xu , Member, IEEE, Xiao Liu , Senior Member, IEEE, Azadeh Ghari Neiat , Liju Chu , Xuejun Li , Member, IEEE, and Yun Yang , Senior Member, IEEE

AbstractâWith the widespread use of Internet of Things (IoT) technology, an enormous number of end devices that request various kinds of cloud services have been connected to the Internet. Multi-access edge computing (MEC) can reduce the service response time by selecting the required edge computing resources closer to the end device. However, MEC-based smart systems require heterogeneous and diverse services support. Taking unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) last-mile delivery system as an example, there are two types of services required: delivery and computational services. The edge services in MEC environments are distributed and limited. Inefficient service selection plans will affect the quality of services of such smart systems. Therefore, how to design a suitable service selection strategy is a crucial issue for MEC-based smart systems. To address this issue, we propose a service selection framework and a holistic and hybrid service selection $( H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 } )$ strategy for MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems in real-world UAV last-mile delivery scenarios. This framework considers three important characteristics of UAV delivery systems: diverse service requirements, service availability, and service mobility. The $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy focuses on selecting the optimal delivery and computational services and provides an integrated approach with a static service selection algorithm and a dynamic service re-selection algorithm. The $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy determines the optimal delivery and computational service selection plans with the lowest UAV energy consumption and shortest service response time. We assess the effectiveness and efficiency of the $H ^ { 2 } \hat { S } ^ { 2 }$ strategy through ablation studies and comparative analyses with diverse representative strategies. The experimental results show that the $\hat { H ^ { 2 } } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy improves the effectiveness and efficiency of the UAV delivery system by significantly reducing UAVâs energy consumption and service response time.

## I. INTRODUCTION

N RECENT years, the Internet of Things (IoT) has led to the development of various smart systems and applications, including smart logistics, smart homes, smart health, smart agriculture and smart transportation [1], [2]. As a result, the number of smart end devices connected to the Internet has increased drastically. According to Statista, the number of connected smart end devices worldwide will increase to 45.72 billions by 2028 [3]. This means that nearly 46 billions end devices will need to access the Internet and request various kinds of services. Currently, with the help of cloud computing-based smart systems and applications, end devices can easily access complex and diverse cloud services. However, the rapidly increasing number of service requests generated by end devices could cause challenges, including network congestion and conflicts. Therefore, cloud services could face problems such as high service response times, low system efficiency, and an inability to meet the service quality requirements of users [4], [5].

Multi-access edge computing (MEC) has been developed to address the challenges faced by cloud computing [2], [6]. MEC has revolutionized the cloud computing architecture by moving cloud services and resources to be near end devices in edge servers. Combined with 5G and smart systems, MEC can offer end devices or users a better Quality of Service (QoS) and Quality of Experience (QoE). In particular, in delay-sensitive smart systems, the service response time of the end device can be effectively reduced because the end device is closer to the edge servers than the cloud servers. As a result, the MEC environment has been widely used in various kinds of smart systems, such as smart logistics, smart health and smart transportation. However, edge servers have limited resources, and they may not satisfy the service requirements of users or may even significantly degrade the QoS of the smart system. Therefore, how to design a suitable service selection strategy is a crucial issue for MEC-based smart systems.

Last-mile delivery is a critical component of smart logistics systems due to its significant impact on the overall logistics cost and time. It typically accounts for a substantial portion of the total delivery cost and time. For example, according to the report by CapGemini,1 last-mile delivery constitutes 53% of total shipping costs and 41% of overall supply chain costs. Efficient last-mile delivery solutions are therefore essential for optimizing logistics operations and enhancing customer satisfaction. UAV deliveries significantly enhance accessibility for individuals with limited transportation options and services. They are also more cost-effective than traditional express delivery methods, costing approximately \$1.23 per delivery compared to \$5.33 for last-mile distance by electric van [7]. This cost-efficiency positions UAV to serve a niche market for expedited goods delivery, ushering in a new era of sustainable smart logistics systems.

In the process of UAV delivery, there are many types of applications and services that need to be used, such as delivery services and computational services. The execution of such services requires significant energy consumption of UAVs. However, due to the size and capacity of the batteries, UAVs often cannot complete the entire delivery process independently. Therefore, the UAVs need to request different types of services to complete the delivery process. Last-mile delivery is typically suited for small-scale deliveries within urban areas, where the environment is relatively complex and requires real-time detection and planning adjustments. In contrast, long-distance delivery is more applicable to rural and mountainous regions. This complexity in urban areas makes the joint consideration and optimization of delivery and computational service resources a significant challenge. Additionally, urban environments tend to have higher densities of UAV delivery operations, increasing the likelihood of resource conflicts compared to long-distance deliveries. To address this, it is crucial to dynamically adjust the selection of delivery and computational services based on the actual delivery process. This helps avoid service conflicts and ensures the efficiency of the delivery operations.

As shown in Fig. 1, the typical UAV delivery process can be divided into four main phases. Phase 1 involves user ordering, where users submit logistics and transportation service demands within the MEC environment. Phase 2, i.e., as order assignment, involves assigning the most suitable delivery service resources to fulfill user orders. This phase ensures optimal allocation to efficiently meet user requirements. Phase 3 encompasses UAV flight tasks, requiring real-time computation for various services during UAV flight, such as target detection and obstacle avoidance. The final phase, Phase 4, focuses on image documentation, for photographic record. During this stage, user identity verification is conducted, and the entire delivery process is recorded for documentation purposes.

In the delivery process, the UAVs need to request two kinds of services: delivery services and computational services. The delivery services are responsible for handling the UAVâs delivery requirements, such as delivery task assignments and delivery relays [8]. The computational services are responsible for handling all types of computational tasks during the UAVâs flight process. UAVs often generate a vast amount of data during their operations, such as location information, sensor readings, and images. Computational services can be utilized to process this data in real time, enabling tasks such as object recognition, path optimization, and collision avoidance. [9]. The execution of these functions or tasks necessitates computational capabilities situated at the networkâs edge. For example, when the delivery process reaches Phase 3 (UAV Flight Tasks), the UAV needs to request a relay delivery service and a computational service to finish computational tasks such as pose and face recognition. This study focuses on UAV delivery scenarios with relative independence between services requests. This allows us to simplify the optimization problem and develop initial solutions.

Existing studies have mostly focused on the service selection optimization problem and have proposed different algorithms for determining the optimal service selection plan [10], [11], [12], [13], [14]. Moreover, most works have considered the perspective of service providers, optimizing the service selection plan with less focus on the perspective of service users [15], [16]. From the userâs perspective, there are three important factors that should be considered: the mobility of the userâs end device, the dynamic nature of the network environment and the diversity of the userâs QoS [17], [18], [19]. Most research works have considered only one or two of these factors [14], [18], and very few studies have considered all of these factors. However, many real-world smart systems, such as MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems, must consider these complicated characteristics at the same time. An unsuitable service selection plan may increase the service response time and UAVâs energy consumption, which can impact the efficiency of delivery system and cause delivery task failures. Hence, in this research paper, we explore this matter from the standpoint of service consumers (namely, UAV delivery companies) and propose a holistic and hybrid service selection strategy (H2S2) for MECbased UAV last-mile delivery systems. This paper makes the following primary contributions.

1) We introduce a novel service selection framework for MEC-based UAV delivery systems grounded in a real-world scenario. Traditionally, the delivery service and computational service are optimized independently. In contrast, our proposed framework unifies the two services for collaborative optimization, which enhances the efficiency and effectiveness of the delivery system by concurrently reducing the UAVâs energy consumption simultaneously.

2) We introduce two new models (viz. the UAV service delivery model and the UAV computational service model) for optimizing the UAVâs energy consumption under the orderâs deadline. The two new models take care of three aspects: diverse service requirements, service availability and mobility, which can effectively address the issues of the conflict and availability of services in UAV delivery.

3) We propose a holistic and hybrid service selection strategy for MEC-based smart logistics systems that can optimize the multi-UAV service selection problem. The proposed strategy consists of a set of algorithms which cover the entire UAV delivery process from a holistic optimization perspective. Meanwhile, existing works often deal with the static and dynamic service selection problems independently which cannot guarantee a global optimization. In contrast, our proposed hybrid strategy incorporates both the static and dynamic service selection with the aim to determine the optimal delivery and computational service selection plans.

4) We conduct comprehensive experiments using Antworkâs ADNET scenario to validate the effectiveness and efficiency of $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy. The simulation experiment dataset was abstracted from real UAV air path data collected by the ADNET project. The experimental results indicate that $\dot { H } ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy achieves an average 9.71% improvement in delivery efficiency and 15.42% reduction in delivery energy consumption for improvement of effectiveness when the number of orders is 6.

## III. SYSTEM FRAMEWORK AND PROBLEM FORMULATION

This section introduces the system framework for service selection in MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems. Unlike the system models proposed in the previous section, this system framework focuses on the design of the service management module in MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems and provides the basis for modelling the problem. Subsequently, we formulate the service selection problem within the context of MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems, building upon the proposed service selection framework.

## A. System Framework

Drawing upon the motivating example and the problem analysis outlined in Appendix A, available online,2 we establish a service selection framework tailored for MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems. As shown in Fig. 3, the service selection framework considers two kinds of services requested by the UAV: delivery services and computational services. The delivery service and computational service resources in the MEC environment can be efficiently managed by this system framework.

The service selection framework is divided into five layers: infrastructure layer, virtualization layer, service function layer, service management layer and application layer. The application layer receives the two types of service requests from the UAV: delivery service requests and computational service requests. The service management layer analyzes the service requests received from the application layer. Then, the best service selection plan is generated by the service management controller. When the service function layer receives the service selection plans from the above layer, it invokes the available service functions in this layer to execute the UAVâs service requests. The virtualization layer provides a unified abstraction of the bottom infrastructure layer, which includes the computational service function (CSF), delivery service function (DSF) and network virtualization. The foundational layer is the infrastructure layer, encompassing network infrastructure, computational infrastructure, and delivery infrastructure.

The above analysis shows how a suitable service selection plan can be generated according to the characteristics of the $\mathrm { \bar { U } A V \mathrm { ^ { , } s } }$ service requests which is a crucial issue in ensuring the efficiency of MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems. Therefore, suitable algorithms should be designed for the computational and delivery service management sub-modules in the service management controller (highlighted in a slate blue color). In this paper, the $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy focuses on the delivery and computational service selection optimization problems, providing an integrated approach that considers both the delivery and computational service selection algorithms and the dynamic service re-selection algorithm.

## B. System Models

The system model contains formal definitions of various types of services as well as the definitions and calculation methods for optimization metrics in the MEC environment. As shown in Fig. 4, the system model of the MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems has four components: UAVs, edge service resources, cloud service resources and a service management controller module [25], [26].

The UAVs in MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems are the end devices that deliver the userâs package (represented by $U _ { i } )$ . The delivery process can be divided into two main stages: the static stage and the dynamic stage. In the static stage, the UAVs at each delivery station are in standby states and wait for instructions from the delivery system. After the delivery instructions are issued by the system, the UAV flies to the destination delivery station. In this process, because of battery and computing capacity limitations, a UAV cannot handle the service requests by itself. Therefore, the UAV needs to submit requests to an edge or cloud service. As shown in Fig. 4, UAV $U _ { 1 }$ requests edge service $R _ { 1 }$ from $C S _ { 4 } ^ { E }$ . After the service request is processed, the resulting data $R D _ { 4 }$ is sent back to $U _ { 1 }$ . Note that these service requests are independent of each other.

The edge service resources consist of two types of service resources: delivery services and computational services. The delivery service is mainly composed of various types of UAVs and delivery stations. First, when delivery orders are sent to the starting delivery station, the most suitable UAV should be selected for the packages. Then, the best flight path between the starting and destination points should be planned before the UAV takes off. Because of the long distance between the cloud servers and the UAV, the data transfer time of cloud services is usually higher than that of edge services. Therefore, cloud services are only suitable for computational resource-intensive service requests.

The service management controller module provides global delivery and computational service control and management functions for MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems. When the UAV sends a service request to the system, the service management controller module considers both the $\mathrm { U A V } _ { \mathrm { \Delta } }$ requirements and the current MEC service environment when selecting the best service. Therefore, how to design an effective and efficient method to optimize the service selection plan is a key challenge.

## C. Problem Formulation

Leveraging the proposed service selection framework, we initiate an analysis of the service selection problem within MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems, including both the delivery service management problem and the computational service management problem. Then, a series of problem models are defined to formulate the service selection problem with optimization objectives.

Definition 7: Delivery service optimization problem

1) The total delivery time can be calculated as

$$
D T _ { \mathrm { s u m } } = \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { N _ { O } } \sum _ { j = 1 } ^ { N _ { U } } D T _ { i , j }\tag{11}
$$

$$
D E _ { \mathrm { s u m } } = \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { N _ { O } } \sum _ { j = 1 } ^ { N _ { U } } D E _ { i , j }\tag{12}
$$

The total delivery energy consumption is the aggregate of all delivery energy consumption $D E _ { i , j }$ for UAV $U _ { i , j }$

3) Optimization objective of the delivery service selection problem: $F I U V _ { 1 }$ . The objective is to optimize energy consumption of each UAV during delivery while adhering to the deadline constraints of the delivery orders.

Definition 8: Computational service optimization problem

In this paper, the optimization goal of the computational service optimization problem is to minimize the integrated utility value of the fitness $( \dot { F } I U V _ { 2 } )$ . The goal is to optimize the service response time of the computational service. The service response time must meet or exceed the required deadlines to ensure the availability and reliability of subsequent requests. An integrated utility function $( I U F _ { 2 } )$ that considers the total service response time when evaluating the computational service selection plan was designed, as shown in (14). The optimization goal of the computational service selection problem is to minimize the

$I U F _ { 2 }$ value of the fitness $( F I U V _ { 2 } )$ . Hence, relying on the aforementioned model, the optimization problem for computational service selection can be formulated as follows:

The total computational service response time is the sum of each computational service response time $C T _ { i , j }$ for computational service $C S _ { i , j }$ . This objective is designed to ensure that the response time remains below the computational service request $C \bar { R } _ { j } ^ { \ , } \mathrm { s }$ deadline $D L ^ { C }$ , thus avoiding task execution failures that could affect subsequent tasks.

## IV. PROPOSED SOLUTION

In this section, a two-stage holistic and hybrid service selection $( H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 } )$ strategy for MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems is proposed as an integral approach for optimizing multi-UAV service selection problems in MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems. First, we present an overview of the $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy. Then, the details of two service selection algorithms for solving the delivery and computational service selection optimization problems are discussed.

## A. Overview of the $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ Strategy

Real-world MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems have two main stages: the static stage and the dynamic stage. First, when the delivery system receives the userâs delivery order, the system needs to analyse the characteristics of the userâs order and generate various service selection plans. Second, when the UAV begins to deliver the package, the system needs to provide a dynamic service selection plan in case there are service availability or conflict issues. Therefore, the $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy is also divided into two stages. In the static stage, before the UAV takes off, the cloud servers generate static delivery service and computational service selection plans according to the characteristics of the service requests. After the UAV takes off, the dynamic stage resolves service availability or conflict issues with service re-selection algorithms. It is obvious that the delivery and computational service selection problem focused in this paper is a typical NP-hard problem. Since there is no guarantee to find the global optimal solution for a NP-hard problem, the practical target of the $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy proposed in this paper is to find a near-optimal solution which is the best solution found by the optimization algorithm within a limited amount of running time.

Fig. 5 illustrates the schematic diagram of the $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy in MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems. The strategy is categorized into two layers according to the two types of service requests. Each layer has two optimization stages: the static and dynamic stage. In the static phase, the UAV delivery service is not started. There is no real-time requirement for the delivery system at this time. Therefore, we use the particle swarm optimization-based delivery and computational service selection algorithm that is capable of time consumption and close to the optimal solution. To mitigate the risk of the PSO algorithm converging to local optima, we employed diverse initialization, velocity clamping, adaptive inertia weight adjustment and periodic re-initialization strategies. In this context, due to the collaborative optimization of delivery and computational services facilitated by the proposed $H ^ { 2 } { \breve { S } } ^ { 2 }$ strategy, the generation of computational service selection schemes relies on delivery service plans. Consequently, the initial delivery plans need to be provided as algorithm input to the computational service selection algorithm. In the dynamic phase, we use greedy and BFS-based delivery and computational service re-selection algorithms with low time complexity to solve the service reselection problem due to the real-time requirements in the UAV delivery process. Similar to the static stage, the computational service selection algorithm in the dynamic stage also requires delivery service selection plans as algorithm input.

## VI. RELATED WORK

The research status of two service selection problems are presented in this section. The first is the general computation service selection problem in cloud or MEC environments. The second is the delivery service selection optimization problem in the MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery scenario.

## A. Computation Service Selection

The computational service selection problem has emerged as a prominent research focus in the field of service computing [32], [33]. These research endeavors can be categorized into two main groups based on the application environment type: cloud computing environments and Multi-Access Edge Computing (MEC) environments.

In the context of cloud computing environments, a considerable body of research has concentrated on the optimization problem of service selection. Most studies consider three pivotal factors: the userâs QoS, the mobility of end devices, and the network environment [2], [34], [35]. For instance, Yu et al. [30] introduces FaaSDeliver, an adaptive and efficient function delivery engine for optimizing cost in a heterogeneous computing continuum (HCC) comprising. FaaSDeliver identifies cost-efficient function delivery policies (FDPs) for FaaS platform selection and resource allocation. Real-world evaluations show that FaaSDeliver reduces function execution costs by 38% to 78% compared to state-of-the-art methods. Sun et al. [29] introduces the Enhanced Task Type First Algorithm (ET2FA) for cost-efficient, deadline-constrained workflow scheduling in cloud computing. ET2FA minimizes total cost and idle rates using task type scheduling, delay optimization, and instance hibernation. Simulations show ET2FA outperforms state-of-theart algorithms.

In the MEC environment, several research endeavors have focused on optimization problems related to service selection. For example, Peng et al. [36] investigated the impact of safety factors on service selection and task offloading plans during UAV flight, proposing a multi-objective evolutionary algorithm. Experimental results demonstrated that the proposed algorithm can optimize energy consumption in service selection and task offloading plans while ensuring the safety of UAV flight. Yin et al. [31] proposes an Efficient Convergent Firefly Algorithm (ECFA) for scheduling security-critical tasks in cloud-edge computing. ECFA uses a probability-based mapping operator and low-complexity position update strategy, ensuring efficient solution exploration. Theoretical analysis and evaluations demonstrate ECFAâs superiority and robustness against boundary traps compared to other metaheuristics. Wang et al. [37] introduced a collaborative filtering-based service recommendation method in the MEC environment, which simultaneously considered user mobility, service availability, and user Quality of Service (QoS) requirements. Experimental results affirmed that the proposed method enhances the accuracy of the service selection plan in the MEC environment.

Upon analyzing the existing research on computing service selection problems in cloud or MEC environments, it is evident that the majority of studies concentrate on optimizing the computing service selection problem. However, these efforts often overlook the diverse range of services in MEC environments. To tackle this gap, we propose a holistic solution for addressing the diverse service selection problem in MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems. This solution takes into account both computational service and delivery service selection problems.

## B. UAV Service Selection

Numerous research works have addressed the UAV delivery service selection problem [8], [21], [38]. For instance, Alkouz et al. [38] introduced a swarm-based drone-as-a-service framework for delivery systems. Within this framework, two service selection algorithms were proposed, taking into account various behaviors of drone swarms. Experimental results demonstrated that the proposed algorithms enhanced the delivery efficiency of the drone delivery system. Chu et al. [21] presented a twostage energy-efficient service provision strategy based on the genetic algorithm (ESP-GA) to address the delivery service and computational service selection problems in Multi-Access Edge Computing (MEC)-based UAV last-mile delivery systems. Experimental results showcased the improved performance of the proposed strategy in terms of UAV energy consumption and delivery time. Addressing practical constraints in the UAV delivery process, existing studies have also proposed solutions. Hamdi et al. [8] considered the drone delivery service selection problem with uncertainty factors, aiming to maximize the efficiency and effectiveness of the delivery system. A comprehensive solution, featuring an uncertainty-aware delivery service selection architecture and several service selection algorithms, was proposed. The suggested solution facilitates efficient and effective delivery services within the delivery system.

The literature review presented in Table V highlights that existing research on the UAV service selection problem has predominantly focused on either delivery service selection or computational service selection, with limited attention to both static and dynamic service selection. This fragmented approach fails to address the comprehensive, multi-stage complexity of the problem, potentially leading to prolonged response time and increased energy consumption in UAV delivery service schemes.

To address this gap, this paper proposes a mobility-aware hybrid service selection $( H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 } )$ strategy for MEC-based UAV last-mile delivery systems. This strategy adopts a holistic perspective by considering both static delivery service selection and static computing service selection, a hybrid perspective by incorporating dynamic delivery service re-selection and dynamic computing service re-selection. The primary objective of the $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy is to optimize UAV energy consumption while ensuring efficient service response times.

## VII. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

This paper introduces a comprehensive and hybrid service selection strategy $( H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 } )$ tailored for multi-access edge computing (MEC) in unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) last-mile delivery systems. The $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy is designed to mitigate the UAVâs energy consumption during delivery and enhance computational service response time, taking into account various influencing factors in the given scenario that impact UAV service plan selection. Experimental evaluations were conducted on the ADNET project, a real-world UAV delivery system. The $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy was thoroughly assessed alongside other representative strategies, focusing on optimization objectives related to delivery and computational service selection. The results of the experiments demonstrate that the $H ^ { 2 } S ^ { 2 }$ strategy significantly enhances the efficiency and effectiveness of the UAV delivery system by reducing both energy consumption and service response times.

The service selection problem in multi-access edge computing-based UAV last-mile delivery systems remains a key challenge. This paper focused on optimizing the delivery energy consumption and the computational service response time. However, the complex relationships between different service requests were not considered. Therefore, future research work should consider the complex relationships between service requests by combining different technology workflows.

Additionally, the issue of UAV path conflicts when scheduling multiple UAVs simultaneously in close proximity, such as within a one-mile radius, was not considered in this study. Future work will investigate how to effectively manage and resolve potential path conflicts to enhance the robustness and efficiency of the delivery algorithm. This will involve developing strategies for dynamic path planning and conflict resolution in dense urban environments.
