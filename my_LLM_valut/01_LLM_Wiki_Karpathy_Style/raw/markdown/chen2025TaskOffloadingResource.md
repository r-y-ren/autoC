# Task Offloading and Resource Pricing Based on Game Theory in UAV-Assisted Edge Computing

Zhuoyue Chen , Yaozong Yang , Jiajie Xu , Ying Chen , Senior Member, IEEE, and Jiwei Huang , Senior Member, IEEE

AbstractâDue to the limited battery capacity and computational resources of mobile devices, computation-intensive tasks generated by mobile devices can be offloaded to edge servers for processing. This paper investigates the multi-user task offloading and resource pricing issues in Autonomous aerial vehicle (AAV)-assisted Multi-Access Edge Computing (MEC) systems. The optimization objectives is optimizing the utility of the server and the utility of the Edge Users (EUs), with decision variables encompassing the offloading strategies of EUs and the pricing strategies of the server. We divide the entire optimization problem into two parts. When optimizing the serverâs utility, server energy consumption is a crucial metric; hence, in the first part, we formulate the user allocation problem with the goal of minimizing the serverâs overall energy consumption. Utilizing game theory, we transform the user allocation problem into a multi-user non-cooperative game and prove the existence of a Nash Equilibrium (NE). The Game-based User Allocation (GBUA) algorithm is proposed to obtain the user allocation strategy. After addressing the user allocation problem, we consider the simultaneous optimization of both server and EUs utility. Therefore, in the second part, we model the server and EUsâs engagement using the Stackelberg game model and employ backward induction to verify the presence of a Stackelberg Equilibrium (SE). Additionally, we propose the Resource Pricing and Task Offloading (RPATO) algorithm, based on game theory, to obtain the SE solution. Finally, extensive experiments are conducted to validate the effectiveness of the proposed algorithms, and numerous comparative algorithms are tested to prove the advancement and innovation of our proposed algorithms.

## I. INTRODUCTION

A S INTERNET of Things (IoT) devices become increas-ingly common and technological progress continues, IoT opens up numerous application domains in the field of computationally intensive functions, such as intelligent transportation systems, smart healthcare, and online interactive gaming [1], [2]. Yet, due to restrictions in power usage and processing capabilities, existing IoT devices are fundamentally incapable of accommodating such applications [3]. Mobile Cloud Computing (MCC) has emerged as a promising approach to enhance the computational capabilities of devices. By offloading computation tasks to well-endowed cloud data centers for completion, MCC surpasses the constraints on a deviceâs battery capacity and computational resources [4].

However, for some latency-sensitive IoT services, MCC is not ideal due to the additional transmission costs and delays. Fortunately, The emergence Multi-Access Edge Computing (MEC) provides a feasible solution to these latency-sensitive tasks [5]. By deploying MEC servers, Edge Usersâ (EUs) workloads are able to be shifted to adjacent Edge Servers (ES), thereby enhancing the Quality of Experience (QoE) and greatly decreasing power usage and latency [6]. Nevertheless, traditional MEC deploys the ES on the ground base which may not be applicable to certain communication scenarios, such as urgent assistance, armed forces training, and catastrophe relief [7]. In such situations, many terrestrial facilities are damaged, thereby being unable to furnish connectivity and computational support.

Autonomous aerial vehicles (AAVs) are able to utilized to support the networking and processing of MEC, having emerged as a pivotal technology handling the difficulty [8]. UAVs possess superior maneuverability and flexibility, enabling them to be deployed agilely in such environments, providing computing services to EUs within the affected areas. Moreover, UAVs can offer improved channel conditions while airborne, extending the coverage of wireless networks and simultaneously reducing latency, enhancing the speed and quality of data transmission. Furthermore, UAVs are equipped to actively change their positions according to live demands, balancing network loads and optimizing resource allocation, thereby enhancing the overall efficiency and performance of the multi-access edge computing network.

Although many efforts have been made to solve the problem of task offloading in UAV-MEC system [9], [10], there are still some challenges that need to be addressed. First, the addition of UAVs provides EUs with more offloading options. However, it is challenging to make offloading decisions between Base Station (BS)-ES and UAV-ES to achieve load balancing. Second, from an economic perspective, BS-ES and UAV-ES require profits to drive their service provision, while also consuming their computing and communication resources. We need to make appropriate strategies to balance profits and costs. In addition, the increasing number of IoT devices and network scale, will also bring more challenges, and we need to consider the scalability of algorithms to adapt to different environments.

Stackelberg games can be employed to investigate the competition and cooperation among rational individual participants, wherein the participants are categorized as leaders and followers. However, to the best of our knowledge, the design of Stackelberg games in UAV-assisted MEC systems has not been thoroughly explored. Zhou et al. in [11] utilized Stackelberg games to address the task offloading and resource pricing issues between servers and users, but did not fully consider the utility of both servers and users. In the study by Yuan et al. in [12], a three-level Stackelberg model was proposed to address the optimal resource allocation problem; however, the issue of user allocation was not considered. Xu et al. in [13] focused on resolving anti-interference transmission issues in communication networks through Stackelberg games, but did not address the utility issue between users and servers. Yang et al. in [14] employed Stackelberg games to study the interaction between UAVs and attackers, but did not consider the overall utility of the system. Therefore, the scenario we consider involves EUs being assigned tasks for execution, with both BS-ES and UAV-ES possessing unused computational capacity. EUs may choose to delegate tasks to the BS-ES or UAV-ES to alleviate pressure and costs, while the servers can profit from monetizing their unused computing power [15]. Specifically, we apply game-theoretic approaches to address the user allocation problem and utilize a Stackelberg game to resolve the task offloading process between servers and EUs, with BS-ES and UAV-ES acting as leaders and EUs as followers. Subsequently, we introduce an algorithm to obtain a relatively optimal solution that maximizes the utility for both servers and EUs. The primary contributions can be summarized as follows:

- We consider the scenario of UAV-assisted MEC where there exist several EUs, one BS-ES, and one UAV-ES, and investigate the computation task offloading problem of EUs in this scenario. The offloading strategies of EUs and the pricing strategies of servers are considered as decision variables, aiming to optimize the utilities of both servers and EUs.

We consider the EUsâ allocation problem with the objective of optimizing the serverâs energy consumption. Employing game theory, we transform the user allocation problem into a non-cooperative game among EUs, prove the existence of a Nash Equilibrium (NE), and propose the Game-based User Allocation (GBUA) algorithm to resolve the user allocation problem for EUs.

C We utilize a Stackelberg game model to characterize the server and EUsâs engagement, in which the BS-ES and UAV-ES act as leaders, while the EUs act as followers. The optimization objective is to maximize the utility for both servers and EUs. We employ backward induction to analyze the proposed Stackelberg model and proe that there exists a unique Stackelberg Equilibrium (SE) between the servers and EUs. Furthermore, we have designed the Resource Pricing and Task Offloading (RPATO) algorithm to obtain an approximate solution.

- We evaluate the proposed algorithms through extensive experimentation, and the results prove the feasibility and effectiveness of the algorithms. Additionally, a comprehensive set of comparative experiments has been conducted, pitting our methods against several traditional baseline approaches. The experimental outcomes highlight the advancement and innovation of our proposed algorithms.

The structure of the paper is as follows, followed by a discussion of related work in Section II. In Section III, the system model is considered and the problem is formulated. The offloading problem is analyzed using game theory in Section IV. The experimental evaluation is presented in Section V. Section VI concludes the paper.

## II. RELATED WORK

## A. Computation Offloading in MEC

In recent years, computation offloading in MEC has garnered significant attention from both the academic and industrial sectors, with a multitude of studies focusing on computation offloading in ground-based MEC systems. Guo et al. in [16] considered the communication and computation dynamics between ground BSs and users in MEC systems, aiming to design an online computation task offloading mechanism to optimize task execution latency. Meanwhile, Wang et al. in [17] also considered ground-based MEC systems and utilized the Lyapunov optimization framework to make optimal single-time-slot decisions for user transmission power allocation and MEC server selection, with the goal of minimizing time-averaged expected task execution delay under the constraint of average energy consumption. Dai et al. in [18] considered a new scenario in ground-based MEC systems, treating vehicles as small BSs, and studied the coordination of task uploading among multiple vehicles to construct a collaborative computation offloading problem, aiming to minimize the latency of task completion. Additionally, in [19], the authors proposed a ground vehicular network that supports MEC and introduced a new computation task offloading mechanism designed to minimize energy consumption during task completion. Although task offloading in ground-based MEC systems has been extensively researched, its physical characteristics on the ground make it challenging to conduct studies in more flexible areas.

## B. UAVs-Assisted Edge Computing

As ground-based MEC systems continue to develop, they also face challenges in conducting studies in more flexible regions. However, amidst the rapid development of MEC, drones have also found widespread application across various domains. Zhu et al. in [20] proposed a computation offloading scenario in drone-assisted MEC, outlining a new paradigm for droneassisted computation offloading, and endeavored to design a novel computation task offloading mechanism to optimize task execution latency. Concurrently, Lin et al. in [21] proposed a dynamic resource allocation for drone-assisted MEC based on Lyapunov, aiming to select appropriate servers for users and minimize time-averaged expected task execution delay under the constraint of average energy consumption. Simultaneously, research efforts have been directed towards optimizing energy consumption within the system. Zhao et al. in [22] studied a collaborative multi-access edge computing system with multiple drones and multiple edge clouds, aiming to reduce the total energy consumption of the system by optimizing the droneâs trajectory and the allocation of computation tasks. Lu et al. in [23] explored a drone-assisted MEC scenario and proposed two multi-drone-assisted multi-access edge computing transmission methods based on single-agent and multi-agent reinforcement learning, aiming to minimize the total energy consumption of the system. A plethora of research has been dedicated to optimizing single attributes such as system delay and energy consumption, and the overall performance optimization of the system has gradually become an unavoidable challenge.

## C. Game Theory in Task Offloading

When considering the overall performance in MEC systems, game theory is a favorable approach for addressing the issue. Xu et al. in [24] proposed a scenario in the Internet of Vehicles, where game-theoretic methods are used to determine the optimal task offloading strategies for users and the optimal pricing strategies for servers during the task offloading interaction process, thereby optimizing the overall performance of both servers and users. Similarly, [25] introduced a novel MEC system comprising multiple ground servers and an aerial server carried by a UAV, with the objective of maximizing the systemâs weighted computational efficiency under the constraints of communication and computational resources, minimum computational requirements, and UAV mobility, thereby enhancing the systemâs overall performance. Fan et al. in [26] presented a game-theoretic computation task offloading strategy for IoT mobile devices, which effectively reduces the overall computation delay and energy consumption by distributing tasks among MEC BSs to balance the load, thereby improving the systemâs overall utility. Chen et al. in [27] proposed a two-stage Stackelberg game model for computational resource pricing and task offloading, which effectively reduces costs, latency, and energy usage by attracting idle servers to process computation tasks, thus improving the task completion rate.

Different from existing research, this study investigates the allocation of offloading destinations for EUs and the problem of task offloading and server resource pricing in a drone-assisted MEC network. First, a scenario is constructed comprising a BS edge computing server, a drone edge computing server, and multiple EUs. The interaction between servers and users is studied with the aim of optimizing both partiesâ utility. Furthermore, for the utility of users and servers, reward and punishment functions, as well as factors such as delay and energy consumption, are considered. For users and the initial stage of the research, gametheoretic methods are employed to analyze the user allocation problem, and a game-based GBUA algorithm is proposed to determine the target destination for $\mathrm { E U s } ^ { \prime }$ task offloading. After resolving the user allocation problem, two Stackelberg game models are established, with edge servers acting as leaders to formulate pricing strategies, and users acting as followers to determine their own offloading strategies. Moreover, an iterative RPATO algorithm is proposed to optimize usersâ offloading strategies and server pricing strategies. Finally, the effectiveness of the GBUA algorithm and RPATO algorithm is validated through simulation experiments.

## E. Problem Formulation

In this paper, our optimization objective is to enhance the utility for both EUs and servers. The servers generate revenue through the provision of computational resources to EUs offloading tasks to them, thereby securing a certain profit. ## VI. CONCLUSION

This paper investigates the task offloading and resource pricing problem for EUs in UAV-assisted MEC networks. Initially, a scenario is considered that involves a BS-ES, a UAV-ES, and multiple EUs. The engagement between the servers and EUs is studied with the objective of optimizing the utility for both parties. First, game theory is employed to examine the user allocation problem, and a game-based algorithm named GBUA is proposed to determine the task offloading destinations for EUs. After addressing the user allocation problem, two Stackelberg game models are established, where the edge servers act as leaders to provide pricing strategies, and the EUs as followers to determine their offloading strategies. Additionally, an iterative algorithm named RPATO is proposed to obtain the EUsâ offloading strategies and the serversâ pricing strategies. Finally, the effectiveness of the GBUA and RPATO algorithms is validated through simulation experiments. As future work, our objective is to investigate the problem of UAV positioning and trajectory optimization while researching task offloading and resource allocation in the scenario of UAV-assisted multi-access edge computing.
