# Joint Optimization of Data Acquisition and Trajectory Planning for UAV-Assisted Wireless Powered Internet of Things

Zhaolong Ning , Senior Member, IEEE, Hongjing Ji, Xiaojie Wang , Senior Member, IEEE, Edith C. H. Ngai , Senior Member, IEEE, Lei Guo , and Jiangchuan Liu , Fellow, IEEE

AbstractâThe development of Internet of Things (IoT) technology has led to the emergence of a large number of Intelligent Sensing Devices (ISDs). Since their limited physical sizes constrain the battery capacity, wireless powered IoT networks assisted by Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) for energy transfer and data acquisition have attracted great interest. In this paper, we formulate an optimization problem to maximize system energy efficiency while satisfying the constraints of UAV mobility and safety, ISD quality of service and task completion time. The formulated problem is constructed as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) model, and a Multi-agent Constrained Deep Reinforcement Learning (MCDRL) algorithm is proposed to learn the optimal UAV movement policy. In addition, an ISD-UAV connection assignment algorithm is designed to manage the connection in the UAV sensing range. Finally, performance evaluations and analysis based on real-world data demonstrate the superiority of our solution.

## I. INTRODUCTION

D UE to the large-scale promotion and application of theInternet of Things (IoT) technology, a variety of rich Internet of Things (IoTï¼ technology, a variety of rich IoT devices are springing up [1]. It is predicted that by 2030, Intelligent Sensing Devices (ISDs) will reach 500 billion units more [2]. However, such anytime and anywhere, a large number of different wireless network connections also put tremendous pressure to the traditional terrestrial communication infrastructure, requiring not only high data transmission rates, but also large sensing ranges. In scenarios of data sensing, the effectiveness and timeliness of collected data become very important, such as health data monitoring for patients with chronic diseases in telemedicine, temperature and humidity monitoring of crop growth environment in agricultural production [3], and electrical fault monitoring of power lines and power towers in smart grids [4]. In these scenarios, the data generated in the corresponding ISDs needs to be collected for further processing and analysis, and thus effective data acquisition can increase the accuracy of derived decisions. However, the limited battery capacity of each ISD only supports its operation for a short period, and a low-powered ISD may lead to large transmission delay in data uploading, which has become a key constraint for realizing sustainable communications in the IoT.

The emergence of Wireless Power Transfer (WPT) technology provides a low-maintenance and flexible way to supply energy for ISDs [5]. By integrating WPT with Wireless Information Transfer (WIT) technology, it is possible to carry energy and information at the same time, i.e., transmitting energy to the ISD in the downlink and then collecting the monitoring data generated by the ISD in the uplink. In order to ensure energy-efficient WPT, it is necessary to build Line-of-Sight (LoS) links or improve communication channel conditions (e.g., reducing the distance among energy transfer devices) [6]. With the advantage of flexibility and mobility, Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are regarded as useful tools by carrying specific communication and energy transmitter units to improve transmission efficiency [7], compensating for the limited communication range of traditional IoT infrastructures [8].

The UAV-assisted energy transmission and data acquisition process is usually divided into three phases, i.e., the UAV moves to adjust its position, then connects to ISDs to provide energy within its communication range, and finally collects data from these devices. In general, the following challenges are faced:

- It is quite challenging to meet the data acquisition needs of widely distributed ISDs, which are limited by their physical sizes, limited battery capacities and computational processing capabilities. Thus, stable energy supply needs to be provided for energy-constrained ISDs to supplement their energy consumption for data uploading.

- The sensing range of UAVs is limited by their threedimensional (3D) locations, and it is difficult to regulate the location of UAVs to efficiently sense ISDs while reducing the path loss of communication channels.

- It is also challenging to minimize the distance among UAVs under the condition of safe flight, i.e., avoiding collision among multiple UAVs.

A community-based delay approximation algorithm was designed in [9] to quantify auction bids, and an auction algorithm is proposed to determine trajectories of UAVs. In [10], the authors investigated the beam fugitive vector trajectory planning of UAVs and the Central Processing Unit (CPU) frequency adaptation problem of ISDs, aiming to minimize system energy consumption. In [11], a model for UAV-assisted multi-carrier wireless powered communication is proposed, which is solved by jointly optimising the allocation of UAV trajectory, subcarrier, power and subslot to maximize the minimum achievable rate. In [12], an alternating optimization algorithm was developed aiming at jointly optimizing UAV trajectory planning, computational resource and bandwidth allocation. However, the above studies focusing on UAV position regulations only consider a 2D planar space and do not take into account the height of the UAV, i.e., a 3D space. In addition, only a single UAV is considered in the above schemes, and the position regulation of multiple UAVs is much more complicated. Moreover, the flight safety problem for UAV deployment is rarely considered in existing studies.

Therefore, there is an urgent need to design a stable energy transfer and reliable data acquisition scheme in energyconstrained IoT networks with various ISDs. To solve the above challenges, this paper focuses on multi-UAV assisted energy transfer and data acquisition, and considers their flight safety and 3D coordinate adjustment in a wireless-powered loT network. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

We construct a UAV-assisted wireless powered IoT system with the objective of maximizing system energy efficiency while ensuring UAV safety. A multi-objective optimization problem, with UAV mobility and ISD quality of service constraints, is formulated to make a trade-off between energy consumption and flight safety among UAVs. The formulated problem is decomposed into two sub-problems, i.e., UAV trajectory planning and ISD-UAV connection assignment.

- For the first subproblem, we propose a Multi-agent Constrained Deep Reinforcement Learning (MCDRL) algorithm. Specifically, to guarantee the safety of UAV flight while satisfying the optimization objective, the subproblem is reformulated as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) problem, in which the safety constraint is viewed as a penalty constraint. In addition, it integrates actor-critic networks with personalized attention mechanisms, and adjusts different attention weights and penalty constraints to obtain the suitable UAV movement strategy.

For the second subproblem, we propose a low-complexity ISD-UAV connection assignment algorithm based on matching theory. By maximizing the system energy efficiency of each time slot, the UAV-ISD connection is determined and the quality of service as well as energy consumption constraints of ISDs are satisfied.

- The network distribution of ISDs is simulated based on a real-world dataset of Melbourn (Australia). We first evaluate the convergence of the designed algorithm, and then demonstrate its superiority in several metrics compared to representative schemes.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section II discusses the related work. Section III illustrates the system model. Section IV formulates the optimization problem. Section V specifies our solution. Performance evaluations are presented in Section VI. Finally, we conclude this paper in Section VII.

## II. RELATED WORK

In this section, we review the current studies related to UAVenabled energy transfer and UAV trajectory optimization.

## A. UAV-Enabled Energy Transfer

In recent years, UAV-assisted WPT has become a promising technology for wireless networks, by providing a sustainable energy supply for low-power sensors and IoT devices, to prolong their service time. Compared with traditional fixed-position energy transmitters, UAVs are more flexible.

Authors in [13] and [14] studied the time allocation problem in UAV-assisted WPT networks. Yan et al. [13] considered the UAV as an energy emitter and a data collector, formulated the problem of maximizing the amount of offloaded data in a given time, and derived the closed form expression of the optimal time allocation by analyzing the transmission efficiency. Du et al. [14] designed a time-division multiple access based workflow model, aiming to jointly optimize IoT device connection, computational resource allocation, UAV hovering time, WPT time and service sequence.

Authors in [15] and [16] studied the energy efficiency problem in UAV-assisted WPT networks. Wang et al. [15] considered the joint optimization of UAV location and transmit power allocation, formulated the energy efficiency maximization problem, and constructed the supermodular game model. Yan et al. [16] formulated the optimization problem of maximizing the total energy received by all sensors to obtain the best strategy for UAV deployment.

## B. UAV Trajectory Optimization

In UAV-assisted wireless communication networks, UAVs make real-time decisions by sensing their surroundings, and thus the trajectory optimization of UAVs is particularly important. The studies of [17] and [18] used clustering techniques to determine the hovering position of UAVs, and then leveraged learning-based algorithms to determine their trajectories. Dai et al. [17] designed a hybrid heuristic and learning-based scheduling algorithm to solve the energy-efficient scheduling problem by jointly optimizing UAV trajectories, and computation as well as transmission resource allocations.

However, the approach of approximating the trajectory of a UAV through a limited number of path segments leads to the problem of excessive computational complexity [18]. For this reason, Guo et al. [19] designed a path discretization scheme to ensure high trajectory discretization accuracy while developing a path compression scheme to decompose the 3D path of the UAV into three 1D sub-paths. Pandiyan et al. [20] proposed a dynamic charging scheduling algorithm that combines genetic weighted clustering and nearest neighbor searching to schedule the optimal charging path for UAVs. In [21], the authors considered UAVs with different hover heights for hierarchical deployment and proposed a two-stage joint optimization algorithmic solution.

The studies in [2], [22], [23], [24] used deep reinforcement learning algorithms that allow the UAV to interact with the environment with local observations. In [2], Yu et al. designed an extended multi-objective deep reinforcement learning algorithm to learn online path planning strategies for UAVs based on demand priorities. Yang et al. [22] combine the problems of device selection, UAV placement and resource management to propose an asynchronous advantage actor-critic algorithm to improve the speed and accuracy of joint convergence. Wang et al. [23] proposed a trajectory-based block coordinate descent strategy and a deep deterministic policy-based gradient based control algorithm, and introduced the preferred experience replay technique to improve the convergence of the training process. Zhao et al. [24] formulated the trajectory planning problem as two coupled multi-agent stochastic games, and designed a decentralized reinforcement learning algorithm to decouple the problem and find its equilibrium point regarded as the optimal trajectory.

However, the above studies, involving reinforcement learning approaches, intend to maximize the overall reward by taking actions in the environment, while lacking the consideration of some non-reward factors and explicitly indicating constraints. In contrast, constrained deep reinforcement learning methods consider other important factors in addition to maximizing the reward, depending on the particular application domain or the particular task. In [25], Khairy et al. investigated joint dynamic multi-UAV height control and random channel access management, and proposed an online model-free constrained deep reinforcement learning algorithm to obtain an optimal wireless management control policy and improve the access efficiency of the network. Nevertheless, this study only considered the moving distance decision in one dimension of UAV height, and did not consider the 3D position adjustment decision of UAVs.

Nowadays, most of the articles focus on the position adjustment of single UAV. Yang et al. [26] investigated the minimisation of the total energy consumption of UAVs while achieving the minimum data transfer of the user by using dyadic and successive convex approximations, In order to obtain the optimal access order and trajectory. Yuan et al. [27] presented a UAV trajectory scheme constrained by the maximum flight speed, in order to minimize the energy consumption of the UAV and maximize the minimum harvested energy for the user, simultaneously. However, multi-UAV position adjustment is generally focused on 2 dimensions. Wang et al. [28] considered non-cooperative multi-UAV scenarios, proposed a trajectory optimization problem to minimize the task completion time, and devised a decentralised deep reinforcement learning method to solve it. In practical applications, 3D position adjustment involving multiple UAVs is much complicated. In addition, traditional optimization methods of approximating UAV trajectories by a limited number of path segments lead to the problem of excessive computational complexity. Furthermore, they rarely consider flight safety issues for UAV applications, such as avoiding flight collisions during UAV operation.

## C. Data Acquisition Model

After transmitting energy to the ISD, the UAV starts to collect the uploaded data from ISDs. Note that this paper ignores communication interference among multiple UAVs because orthogonal frequency resource allocation is utilized, which can reduce the possibility of mutual interference, especially when the coverage areas of multiple UAVs overlap [32]. This means that the communication among UAVs can remain relatively independent in the frequency domain, even when the spatial coverage overlaps. In addition, the interference among ISDs based on Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiple Access (OFDMA) technology can be ignored when ISDs upload data [23]. This is because OFDMA technology can divide the entire bandwidth into multiple subcarriers, and assign different subcarriers to devices transmitting data to the same UAV at the same time. ## IV. PROBLEM FORMULATION

In this section, we formulate the energy efficiency problem based on the constraints of UAV-assisted ISD energy transmission and effective data acquisition. Our optimization objective is to maximize energy efficiency of the UAV, which can be calculated by the ratio of the bit number of the transmitted data to the total energy consumption of the UAV.

## A. Optimization Objective

The following four aspects are considered for our optimization objective:

Effective data acquisition: The effective data acquisition ratio of the UAV can be measured by the ratio of the collected data to the size of the original data, i.e.,

## V. MULTI-UAV COOPERATIVE CDRL FRAMEWORK

In this section, we solve the two subproblems in turn. ## B. The MCDRL Algorithm

The proposed MCDRL algorithm uses a centralized training and distributed execution framework. In general, agents upload the observed states to the value network deployed at the central controller, and time difference errors are returned to agents during the training process. After completing the training, agents use the local policy networks to make decisions. Moreover, since obtaining the optimal policy and satisfying the constraints have different objectives, it is necessary to consider different attentions to the reward function and the penalty constraint. Therefore, each agent maintains $m + 1$ critic networks with + 1attention mechanisms, i.e., a reward critic (associated with the main reward function) and m penalty critic (associated with the penalty constraint). Herein, the attention mechanism allows the UAV agent to selectively focus on information about agents that are critical to its policy calculations. An evaluation factor is obtained by embedding information about the agents to measure the likelihood that remaining agents are important to each agent, rather than giving equal attention to all agents.

The ISDs with higher energy efficiency are stored in $\mathcal { C } _ { u } .$ Since UAVs need to collect more data and reduce energy as much as possible, the preferences are ranked to be relative to $\Omega _ { i , u } ( t ) / e _ { u } ( t )$ . It is possible that ISDs with higher energy efficiencies can be matched with higher priorities.

Second, each UAV keeps selecting ISDs based on its preference list and keeps checking whether the UAVâs battery capacity can support its continued service according to constraint (20). Meanwhile, the selected ISD determines whether it matches the UAV. Condition $\boldsymbol { \mathcal { M } } ( i ) = \boldsymbol { u } ^ { \prime }$ is updated if the selected ISD has not been matched before, or if matching with UAV u can achieve higher energy efficiency than the previous one. This procedure lasts until all ISDs in each preference list are checked. Then, the final ISD connection can be obtained from M.

In general, we first propose an MCDRL algorithm with the aim of maximizing the energy efficiency of the system and taking into account the need for real-time decision making. In the MC-DRL algorithm, the agent is trained by combining an actor-critic network with a personalized attention mechanism to obtain a trajectory planning policy for the UAV. Then, we propose a low-complexity UAV-ISD connection assignment algorithm to decide the UAVâs connection management with ISDs within its sensing range.

## VI. EXPERIMENTAL EVALUATIONS

In this section, we conduct simulation experiments based on real-world data to evaluate the effectiveness of our solution, and collect the geographic location of ISDs obtained from the IP addresses provided by APNIC [36] for the Melbourne CBD area in Australia. The online YouTube video service dataset [37] is used to simulate the data size of ISDs. First, we describe the simulation setup. Then, numerical results are given.

## A. Simulation Setup

In our simulation, an Intel(R) Xeon(R) Silver 4210R CPU @ 2.40 GHz, python 3.7, and pytorch 1.1.0 are applied. As shown in Fig. 4, UAVs and ISDs (marked in red) are distributed in a rectangular area of 300 m Ã 700 m. In real scenarios, UAVs are usually restricted to operate at a certain altitude, e.g., in the U.S., UAVs can fly at 400 feet in the controlled airspace by obtaining LAANC or DroneZone authorization [34]. Therefore, we set the altitude range of UAVs between 50 m and 120 m [23]. Based on the physical dimensions of the UAV provided by DJIâs website, the minimum safe distance among UAVs is set to be the length of wings, i.e., 5 m. The setting of simulation parameters can be found in Table II. For the network configurations during the training process, the number of hidden dimensions for both the critic network and the actor network is 128, and the number of training episodes is 1000. The learning rates of actor network, critic network, and Lagrange multiplier are 0.001, 0.001, and 0.0001 [25], respectively.

## VII. CONCLUSION

This paper establishes a wireless-powered IoT system for UAV-assisted energy transfer and data acquisition. Considering the UAV flight safety and energy consumption while ensuring the quality of service of ISDs, the problem of maximizing the system energy efficiency is formulated. It is further transformed into a UAV trajectory planning subproblem and an ISD-UAV connection assignment subproblem to decouple the UAV location adjustment and ISD connection assignment decision variables. Then, an MCDRL algorithm is developed and integrated with the attention mechanism to avoid collisions among UAVs, while UAVs provide services to ISDs to obtain the best trajectory planning strategy. In addition, an ISD-UAV connection assignment algorithm is proposed to determine the connection assignment policy with ISDs. Finally, simulation results show that, compared with representative solutions, our designed algorithm has a shorter training time and is superior in terms of data acquisition, service fairness and energy utilization.
