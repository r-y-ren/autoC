AbstractâRecent developments in unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and mobile edge computing (MEC) have provided users with flexible and resilient computing services. ## I. INTRODUCTION

W ITH the rapid development of 6G and the Internet ofThings (IoT), the number of smart mobile devices has Things (IoT), the number of smart mobile devices has shown unprecedented growth, leading to the increasing proliferation of various innovative mobile applications [1]. Most of these applications, such as face recognition, automatic navigation, and image processing, require intensive computation resources and low latency [2], [3]. However, handling the computation-hungry and real-time data generated by these applications is challenging due to the limited resources of mobile devices [4]. In this context, mobile edge computing (MEC) has been regarded as a promising solution that allows mobile devices to offload the computation-intensive and delay-sensitive tasks to proximate edge servers [5], [6], thereby reducing the computation burden, execution delay, and energy consumption of devices [7], [8], [9]. However, conventional MEC network relies on terrestrial infrastructures which are inflexible to deploy due to the installation cost and environmental limitations.

To overcome the physical restrictions of traditional terrestrial MEC systems, unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs)-assisted MEC is emerging to offer flexible and low-cost offloading services due to the high maneuverability, flexibility, fast deployment, and line-of-sight (LoS) links of UAVs [10], [11], [12], [13]. By offloading the computation tasks to the proximate UAVs, mobile users can flexibly enjoy cloud computing services anywhere and anytime [14], [15], [16], [17].

However, designing an efficient task offloading approach in the multi-UAV-assisted MEC systems still faces several challenges. First, terrestrial MEC servers are typically deployed at fixed locations with limited coverage, causing service interruptions. Moreover, the single UAV-assisted MEC is deployed with limited computing resources and battery capacity, potentially making it difficult to handle a large amount of data or maintain

long-term operations. Second, from the perspective of problem formulation, a significant challenge is that most current research tends to concentrate on optimizing a single performance metric, such as delay or energy consumption, or a combination of these two metrics while overlooking the multi-objective optimization. This could further compromise the overall effectiveness and efficiency of the system. Third, the UAV-assisted MEC system is characterized by several realistic features, including stringent and diverse requirements of users, constrained resources of UAVs, and other dynamics characteristics. These features present challenges in the joint optimization to satisfy the requirements of the users and the resource constraints of the UAVs. Finally, in terms of algorithm design, theoretic methods such as game theory possibly face challenges due to high computational and spatial complexity. Moreover, traditional heuristic methods such as swarm intelligence algorithms may encounter issues with a long iteration process and susceptibility to local optima. Additionally, machine learning approaches such as DRL potentially struggle with difficult convergence and long training periods, especially in large-scale scenarios.

To address the abovementioned challenges, this work proposes a multi-objective optimization problem of task offloading, computation resource allocation, and UAV trajectory control for multi-UAV assisted MEC systems. The main contributions are summarized as follows:

Edge Computing Architecture: We consider a multi-UAVassisted MEC system. Specifically, with the aid of MEC, resources of a UAV network are fully utilized to achieve a more flexible and efficient decision-making process, thereby optimizing the system performance. On the one hand, the MEC capability provided by the edge UAVs effectively compensates for the lack of flexibility of fixed MEC servers, and the computing resources can be dynamically deployed and reconfigured via UAVs to respond to real-time needs of different users in different regions. On the other hand, multi-UAV-assisted MEC effectively alleviates the constraints of a single UAV in terms of computing resources and coverage range, thereby significantly expanding the responsiveness and applicability of system.

- Multi-objective Optimization Problem Formulation: First, considering the delay-sensitive requirement of users, the energy limitation of the UAVs, and the stringent experience quality of the users, we formulate a multi-objective optimization problem. Specifically, this problem aims to minimize the total task completion delay, reduce the total UAV energy consumption, and maximize the total number of offloaded tasks by optimizing the decisions of task offloading, computation resource allocation, and UAV trajectory control. Moreover, to capture the dynamic of the considered scenario, the problem models the channel characteristics (i.e., small-scale fading, path loss, and shadowing). Consequently, the formulated problem is able to capture the realistic characteristics of the delay sensitivity, energy constraints, user experience, and channel dynamic within our scenario.

- Joint Optimization Algorithm Design: Since the optimization problem is a mixed-integer non-linear programming (MINLP) and NP-hard problem, we propose a joint task offloading, computation resource allocation and UAV trajectory control (JTORATC) approach. First, we transform the original multi-objective optimization problem into a single-objective optimization problem to facilitate trade-offs of different objectives by leveraging the linear weighting method. Moreover, to manage the complexity induced by coupled and discrete-continuous mixed optimization variables, we split the problem into three sub-problems. For the sub-problem of task offloading, distributed splitting and threshold rounding methods are constructed to obtain the optimal solution. For the subproblem of computation resource allocation, we apply the Karush-Kuhn-Tucker (KKT) method to achieve the optimal solution. Besides, for the UAV trajectory control sub-problem, the successive convex approximation (SCA) method is employed to dynamically optimize UAVs trajectories based on real-time environmental conditions and task requirements.

Effectiveness and Efficiency Validation: The performance of JTORATC is verified through theoretical analysis and simulation. Specifically, the proposed JTORATC approach is able to converge to a sub-optimal solution in polynomial time. Moreover, simulation results demonstrate that the proposed JTORATC achieves superior performance in terms of the objective function value, total task completion delay, and total UAV energy consumption compared with several benchmark schemes. We also find that the proposed algorithm exhibits better scalability in the considered scenario.

The rest of this study is organized as follows. Section II reviews the related work. Section III introduces the proposed system model and problem formulation. Section IV presents the JTORATC approach. Moreover, the simulation results are given in Section V. Finally, Section VI summarizes the paper.

However, the abovementioned works rely on fixed edge infrastructure MEC severs, which may not be applicable to the scenarios with inadequate infrastructures.

Given the limitations of fixed MEC servers, there has been a growing research for UAV-assisted MEC systems to provide computing services flexibly for end devices and to handle dynamic tasks efficiently. For example, Li et al. Moreover, the authors in [22] focused on deploying a UAV equipped with an MEC server to serve multiple terminal devices (TDs) of IoT in a finite period. However, these studies mainly concentrated on single UAV-assisted MEC systems, which may not be applicable to the real-world scenarios, especially for the large-scale networks with dense users.

To overcome the abovementioned challenges, more research has focused on multi-UAV-assisted MEC environments. For example, Tun et al. [23] investigated a multi-UAVs assisted cooperative MEC system integrated with an MEC-enabled terrestrial base station (BS) to minimize the total delay experienced by mobile users. Guo et al. [24] proposed a software defined network enhanced cooperative multiple UAV-enabled aerial computing system to address the challenges in remote areas. Moreover, Ei et al. In the aforementioned studies, the UAVs are either stationary or follow pre-determined trajectories, which may lead to sub-optimal performances of the system, particularly in environments requiring high adaptability and responsiveness. On the one hand, these UAV-assisted MEC architectures lack flexibility and adaptability in dynamic environments where user locations and communication qualities change rapidly. On the other hand, although some studies focus on energy-efficient mechanisms, the fixed UAV paths can lead to extra cost of energy consumption. To address these limitations, we study a multi-UAV-assisted MEC system, where the trajectories of UAVs are designed by considering the user demands, environment dynamics, and the energy efficiency of the UAVs.

## B. Optimization Objectives

The formulation of a joint optimization problem is crucial to accurately capture the characteristics of the system and define the problem to be solved. For example, Liu et al. [25] proposed an approach for edge-cloud orchestration computing that aims to minimize the average latency of tasks. Moreover, Yu et al. [26] investigated an innovative UAV-assisted MEC system involving the interaction between IoT devices, UAVs and edge cloud (EC). Their optimization objective is to minimize the weighted sum of service latency and UAV energy consumption of all IoT devices. Furthermore, Chen et al. [27] studied the problem of minimizing the energy consumption of UAV equipped with MEC servers as a mobile base station to serve users.

However, the abovementioned works mainly consider the delay, energy consumption, or a combination of these two metrics, while neglecting to jointly take into account delay, energy consumption, and total number of offloaded tasks. In contrast, our work comprehensively addresses the three objectives of delay, energy consumption, and the number of offloaded tasks in the problem formulation. This holistic consideration may ensure the delay-sensitive requirements of tasks, while maintaining efficient energy management of UAVs and the experience quality of users.

## C. Solution Spaces

Considering the strict requirements of users and the limited computation resources MEC system, existing studies explored to optimize specific performance metrics by determining the decisions such as task offloading, computation resource allocation, and UAV trajectory control. For example, Ji et al. [28] focused on multi-UAV-assisted wireless networks, aiming to jointly optimize cache placement, UAV trajectory and transmission power within within a finite period for multi-user content delivery. Moreover, Lee et al. [29] investigated how to control the UAV-MEC trajectory and offloading ratio so that the mobile devices can perform effective data processing when located far from ground-based stations. Besides, Park et al. [30] considered the deployment and optimization of MEC-UAVs for subterahertz communication in remote areas.

Previous studies primarily focused on optimizing one or two aspects of task offloading, resource allocation, and UAV trajectory control. However, these partial focuses may hinder the full exploitation of the advantages of UAV-assisted MEC and result in sub-optimal solutions. To address the limitations of these studies, we jointly optimize the task offloading, resource allocation, and UAV trajectory control for the multi-UAV-assisted MEC system.

## D. Optimization Approaches

To solve the complex optimization problem for task offloading, resource allocation, and UAV trajectory control, many researchers are devoted to adopting efficient approaches. For example, Wu et al. [31] proposed a discrete Stackelberg game approach with multiple leaders and followers, aiming to capture the hierarchical feature and discrete optimization in the multi-coalition UAV-assisted MEC networks. Akter et al. [32] presented a messy genetic algorithm (mGA) approach to obtain task offloading and computation resource allocation. Moreover, Li et al. [33] designed an embedded multi-agent deep reinforcement learning (DRL) algorithm to enable UAVs to autonomously learn trajectory control and computation offloading decisions, adapting to dynamic requirements of users.

However, when the Stackelberg game approach is adopted, the computational complexity of the decision may increase rapidly with the number of players and the size of strategy space. Moreover, swarm intelligence algorithms typically require numerous iterations to approach a near-optimal solution, leading to high execution delays, particularly for high-dimensional solution spaces. Furthermore, while DRL is effective for training agents to make decisions, it demands extensive environmental interaction and a large volume of sample data, making it costly in resource-constrained scenario, especially in large-scale scenarios or complex dynamic environments. Therefore, these approaches may be potentially unsuitable for the context of our work. We aim to design a low-complexity and high-efficiency algorithm to adapt to the delay-sensitive, resource-constraint scenarios while ensuring the performances of system. Different from the existing studies, we propose an approach by employing the combination of distributed splitting, threshold rounding, SCA, and KKT. By integrating these four approaches, our scheme strikes a balance between low-complexity and highefficiency, making it highly suitable for real-world applications that demand strict requirements on response speed and resource utilization efficiency. Furthermore, our approach reduces the complexity associated with the traditional decision frameworks, such as existing algorithms and empirical models, thereby enhancing the adaptability and effectiveness of the system in dynamic environments.

## IV. THE PROPOSED JTORATC

In this section, the JTORATC is proposed to solve the formulated optimization problem. First, we transform the original multi-objective optimization problem into a single-objective optimization problem to simplify complexity, unify evaluation criteria, and facilitate trade-offs of different objectives. Then, we split the single-objective optimization problem into three subproblems of task offloading, computation resource allocation, and UAV trajectory control based on the block alternate descent method, which alternates the solution of each sub-problem iteratively. Specifically, we employ distributed splitting and threshold rounding methods to solve the sub-problem of task offloading. Moreover, the sub-problem of computation resource allocation is solved by adopting KKT method, and the sub-problem of UAV trajectory control is solved by employing SCA method.

## A. Problem Reformulation

Solving problem P directly may presents several challenges. First, a multi-UAV assisted MEC scenario characterized by several realistic features, including the delay-sensitive requirements of users, limited resources of MEC servers, energy constraints of UAVs, and the dynamic of the system. These features complicate the design of a low complex and high efficient algorithm to ensure the stringent offloading requirements of users, meet the constrained computing resources of MEC servers, satisfy the limited energy of UAVs, and accommodate the dynamic characteristics of the system. Moreover, optimizing the metrics of P can often lead to conflicting outcomes. For example, reducing the task completion delay and increasing the number of offloaded tasks typically require higher allocations of computing resources, which in turn increases energy consumption. Conversely, typical approaches to decrease UAV energy consumption often involve reducing the computing resources allocated, resulting in increased delays and decreased number of offloaded tasks. To address these challenges, we leverage one of the multiobjective optimization methods, which is the linear weighting method [43], [44] due to its lower algorithmic complexity and its ability to yield effective results rapidly. Moreover, it also enables us to balance the trade-offs efficiently and achieve a satisfactory solution within the constraints of our problem. ## V. SIMULATION RESULTS

In this section, simulation results are presented to validate the effectiveness of the proposed approach.

## A. Simulation Setup

We perform simulations to verify the validity of our proposed JTORATC method. Specifically, all the simulations are performed in MATLAB R2022b on a desktop computer equipped with an Intel(R) Core(TM) i7-8750H CPU @ 2.20GHz 2.21GHz and 8GB RAM.

Scenarios: We consider a multi-UAV-assisted MEC system where 2 UAVs, 4 UAVs and 6 UAVs are deployed to offer offloading services to 8 users in a $2 5 0 0 \times 3 0 0 0 \mathrm { m ^ { 2 } }$ rectangular area. The timeline is set as $T = 1 0 0 ~ \mathrm { s }$ 0 3000which is divided into 50 time slots.

Parameters: The default values of the simulation parameters are listed in Table III.

Benchmarks: This work evaluates the proposed JTORATC in comparison with the following schemes.

Random offloading and JTORATC-based resource allocation and trajectory control (ROJRATC): the task offloading strategies of users are decided randomly, while the computation resource allocation and the trajectories of UAVs are decided based on the proposed JTORATC.

- Nearby offloading and JTORATC-based resource allocation and trajectory control (NOJRATC): the tasks of each user are offloaded to the nearest UAV, while the computation resource allocation and the trajectory control are decided based on the proposed JTORATC.

- Many-to-many matching-based offloading and JTORATCbased resource allocation and trajectory control (MO-$J R A T C ) .$ : the task offloading strategies are decided by using a matching-to-matching mechanism [58], while the computation resource allocation and the trajectories of UAVs are decided based on the proposed JTORATC.

- Equalizing resources, JTORATC-based offloading and trajectory control (ERJOTC): the computation resource allocation of UAVs are decided averagely while the strategies of task offloading and trajectory control are determined based on the proposed JTORATC.

JTORATC-based offloading and resource allocation, circular trajectory (JORACT): the task offloading strategies and the computation resource allocation are decided based on the proposed JTORATC, while the UAVs fly following the circular trajectories.

- JTORATC-based offloading and resource allocation, predefined trajectory (JORAPT): the task offloading strategies and the computation resource allocation are decided based on the proposed JTORATC, while the UAVs follow predefined trajectories.

## B. Evaluation Results

1) System Performance: Fig. 2(a), (b), (c), and (d) show the comparison results of the objective function value, total task completion delay, total UAV energy consumption, and total number of offloaded tasks of seven approaches with the increasing of the number of UAVs. Furthermore, the total number of offloaded tasks remains constant for all approaches as the number of UAVs increases. That is, regardless of how many UAVs are deployed, the total number of tasks that they can handle remains constant. Therefore, the improvement of the overall task processing capability of UAVs does not increase the number of tasks handled, but only affects the processing rate of tasks due to the increased computing resources.

However, it should be noted that ROJRATC performs relatively poorly in terms of total UAV energy consumption and total number of offloaded tasks. This suggests that we must fully balance energy consumption and task completion efficiency while considering delay. In contrast, our proposed JTORATC approach takes this balance into account. Moreover, the ERJOTC has a good performance in reducing the total UAV energy consumption, mainly because evenly allocating resources avoids some resources being idle or overloaded. At the same time, because of the uniform allocation of resources, there is a certain sacrifice in the task completion delay, which is far less than our proposed JTORATC approach. This may be because task offloading is not flexible enough or resources cannot be optimized for the specific needs of the task.

Consequently, this set of simulation results highlights the importance of our proposed JTORATC approach in terms of scalability and robustness of the system.

## VI. CONCLUSION

In this work, we study the multi-objective optimization of task offloading, computation resource allocation and UAV trajectory control in multi-UAV-assisted MEC systems. We first formulate the multi-objective optimization problem with the aim of minimizing the total task completion delay, reducing the total UAV energy consumption, and maximizing the total number of offloaded tasks. To solve the problem, we transform the multi-objective optimization problem into a single-objective optimization problem and then propose the JTORATC to solve the transformed problem at a lower cost. Simulation results demonstrate that the proposed JTORATC has superior performance in terms of the total task completion delay and total UAV energy consumption. Specifically, JTORATC demonstrates better adaptability to the scenarios with varying computing capacities of UAVs, and it shows superior scalability in both light and heavy workload scenarios.
