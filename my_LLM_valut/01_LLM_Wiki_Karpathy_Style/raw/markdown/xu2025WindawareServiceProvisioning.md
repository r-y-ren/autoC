# Wind-Aware Service Provisioning Strategy for Multi-Package Drone Delivery

Jia Xu , Member, IEEE, Hao Liu, Xiao Liu , Senior Member, IEEE, Azadeh Ghari Neiat Xuejun Li , Member, IEEE, and Yun Yang , Senior Member, IEEE

AbstractâIn recent years, drone delivery has drawn significant attention for its promising potential in solving the last-mile delivery problem. As a novel service paradigm, Drone-as-a-Service (DaaS) is emerging as an effective way to address service provisioning problems within complex delivery networks. However, existing DaaS composition frameworks often fail to consider the sharing of delivery services and are not applicable to multi-package drone delivery tasks. Meanwhile, as one of the most critical real-world environmental factors for drones, the impact of dynamic wind conditions is not adequately considered by existing studies. This may lead to Quality of Service (QoS) degradation of delivery services. To address the above issues, in this paper, we propose a wind-aware service provisioning strategy for multi-package drone delivery. Given the advantages of Edge Computing (EC) in handling such dynamic factors due to its low latency and high reliability, we first establish a spatio-temporal DaaS model based on service sharing according to the edge-based drone delivery system. Then, we propose a novel wind-aware drone delivery service provisioning strategy for multi-package delivery to minimize energy consumption of drones. The proposed strategy consists of two phases: service sharing and service composition. In the service sharing phase, the service sharing plan is generated by an improved genetic algorithm. In the service composition phase, the edge server dynamically generates the service composition plan through our proposed policy iteration based DaaS composition method. Experimental results using real delivery network and wind data demonstrate that our strategy is able to reduce delivery energy consumption of drone by about 10.1%.

## I. INTRODUCTION

L intelligent logistics systems. It is closely related to the cost of service providers and the experience of end users. Service AST-MILE delivery represents a pivotal component of providers want to deliver packages as quickly as possible at lower cost, while end users prefer to use services with lower cost and shorter time. This demand for delivery service has driven the exploration of innovative last-mile delivery solutions, with drone technology emerging as a promising candidate [1]. For example, Amazon Prime Air project can deliver medication to homes by drone in 60 minutes [2]. The Wing Delivery Network logistics platform developed by Alphabet Wing supports large-scale drone delivery tasks [3]. Compared with traditional delivery methods, drone delivery has the advantages of low cost, fast delivery, high flexibility, and low carbon emissions. However, due to the complexity of the delivery environment and the limitations of the drone itself, the development of an efficient delivery solution is still a challenging question.

The service paradigm provides a flexible and scalable approach to modeling complex constraints in delivery networks, enabling efficient and reliable solutions. It abstracts both functional and non-functional attributes of drones as Drone-as-a-Service (DaaS) [4]. Delivering packages along a line segment in delivery networks represents a drone delivery service operating under constraints. Given a delivery network, DaaS composition is the process of selecting the optimal drone services to reduce delivery time and improve other QoS (Quality of Service) dimensions such as energy consumption. These services form an aerial path from a given source to the destination. Typically, a single delivery service request cannot be fulfilled by just one delivery service. Therefore, the significant challenge lies in generating a delivery service provisioning plan that minimizes delivery time and energy consumption, based on the characteristics of the delivery service request and the current state of the resources.

While most existing studies focus on single-package drone delivery, multi-package drone delivery refers to the delivery of multiple packages by a drone in a single task. This delivery approach can fully utilize the payload capacity of drones and use fewer drones to complete the delivery tasks [5]. Compared with single-package delivery, multi-package delivery incurs lower energy consumption in many cases. For example, assigning several packages with the same or close destinations to a single drone for delivery may reduce the overall delivery distance and hence the total energy consumption. As a form of service sharing, this approach allows a service instance to serve multiple user requests at the same time.

However, current DaaS composition frameworks are not applicable to multi-package drone delivery as they often overlook the sharing of drone services among user requests. Furthermore, existing approaches do not adequately consider the impact of dynamic wind conditions (as one of the most critical real-world environmental factors), particularly when selecting delivery stations for recharging or waiting. This may increase energy consumption of drones. With limited battery capacity, the drones may need to recharge to reach their destinations during the delivery process. Also, they may need to wait at a delivery station when encountering strong winds. The decisions regarding recharging and waiting refer to when to recharge or wait and which delivery station to choose. For the same delivery path, choosing different recharging or waiting stations may result in different energy consumption of drones. This is because drones may encounter different wind conditions. Therefore, path selection, recharging decision, and waiting decision should ensure drones avoid flying against the wind as much as possible. Moreover, the dynamic changes in wind conditions can greatly influence the effectiveness of service provisioning strategies. In contrast to these limitations, our work proposes a wind-aware DaaS service provisioning strategy for multi-package delivery. This strategy enables drones to dynamically adapt to varying wind conditions and make intelligent decisions about when and where to recharge or wait, thereby reducing drone energy consumption during delivery.

Meanwhile, to facilitate the services for drone delivery systems in the complex environment, many real-time computational tasks are required. However, it is very difficult for centralized cloud computing to support real-time computational tasks. In contrast, edge computing provides more responsive computing services at the edge of the network, enabling real-time planning under dynamic factors [6]. Edge computing can effectively address the challenges caused by dynamic factors in the delivery process, so as to facilitate the implementation of service provisioning strategies in complex delivery environments. Therefore, edge computing is now widely accepted as a promising computing paradigm for DaaS [7].

There is a body of related work that investigates the drone delivery problem from two perspectives: drone routing and scheduling, and delivery service composition. Some research work models the drone delivery problem as the Travelling Salesman Problem (TSP) or the Vehicle Routing Problem (VRP) [8]. This type of work is applicable to multi-package drone delivery tasks, but does not involve complex delivery networks. It focuses on fixed routing or deterministic optimization and lacks integration with dynamic wind conditions. Some studies focus on service-based approaches for drone or swarm-based drone delivery [9]. The service-based approach models the drone path planning and scheduling problem as a service composition and service selection problem. Orchestrated through a serviceoriented framework, service composition and selection can more effectively deal with the constraints and dynamics of the delivery environment. Existing approaches account for dynamic factors in the delivery networks but normally focuses on single-package drone delivery scenarios. Moreover, without considering optimal recharging and waiting decisions, current approaches often lead to an unnecessary increase in energy consumption. This may result in failure to meet QoS requirements. Aiming to address problems in current research, we investigate the delivery service provisioning problem in the edge-based last-mile drone delivery system. Our approach focuses on multi-package drone delivery in complex delivery networks, as a form of service sharing problem. Meanwhile, we consider the impact of dynamic wind conditions on path selection, recharging, and waiting to reduce the delivery energy consumption. To the best of our knowledge, our work is the first to jointly optimize multipackage delivery under dynamic environmental constraints. The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

- A spatio-temporal DaaS model based on service sharing is established according to an edge-based drone last-mile delivery system.

- A wind-aware DaaS service provisioning strategy for multi-package delivery (named MW-DSP) is proposed to minimize the energy consumption of drones. The MW-DSP strategy consists of two phases: service sharing and service composition.

- An improved genetic algorithm (named ADS-GA) is proposed for service sharing. The ADS-GA algorithm includes an agglomerative clustering based population initialization method and a directed mutation mechanism, and incorporates neighborhood search.

A policy iteration based DaaS composition method (named PIDC) is proposed for dynamically composing near optimal DaaS services.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows: Section II presents related work in the areas of drone delivery, DaaS, service selection and composition. Section III first presents the delivery system model. Then the wind and drone energy consumption models as well as the service model are presented. Finally, the service provisioning problem is defined. Section IV introduces the MW-DSP strategy and details our proposed ADS-GA algorithm and PIDC method. Section V evaluates our strategy through simulation experiments. Section VI concludes this paper.

## II. RELATED WORK

In recent years, the drone delivery problem has attracted extensive attention from researchers. In this section, we review related research in drone routing and scheduling, drone-based delivery service, and service selection and composition.

Several studies have modeled the drone delivery problem as TSP or its variants. Tu et al. [10] studied the truck-drone cooperative delivery problem and modeled it as a multi-drone traveling salesman problem. Cavani et al. [11] developed a compact mixed-integer linear programming model for the TSP with multiple drones. They presented an exact decomposition method based on the branch-and-cut algorithm. Lei et al. [12] proposed an improved variable neighborhood search algorithm for the parallel drone scheduling traveling salesman problem, aiming to reduce the total completion time of delivery tasks.

However, TSP is not suitable for modeling the drone delivery problem due to the limited payload of drones and the impact of dynamic environmental factors. Dorling et al. [13] proposed two multitrip VRPs to model the drone delivery problem. The experimental results demonstrated the importance of reusing drones in drone delivery systems. Chiang et al. [14] developed an uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV) green routing model and designed a genetic algorithm for solving large problem instances. Yuan et al. [15] investigated a scheduling problem with heterogeneous logistics UAVs in urban environments. They proposed a framework based on genetic algorithm for solving the scheduling problem. Sajid et al. [16] presented a joint optimization framework for solving UAV-routing and UAV-route scheduling problems. The proposed hybrid genetic simulated annealing algorithm and the UAV-oriented scheduling algorithm were used to solve these two problems respectively. Sorbelli et al. [17] focused on drone delivery under variable wind conditions and defined the mission-feasibility problem. They modeled the wind through a time-dependent cost graph. Sorbelli et al. [18] investigated the effect of the wind in a truck-drone tandem delivery system and proposed a minimum-energy drone-trajectory problem. Shi et al. [19] considered the recharging constraints of drones and designed an improved adaptive large neighborhood search algorithm. Mahdi et al. [20] analyzed the impact of serving multiple customers in single drone sortie versus several sorties delivering a single package. The experimental results showed that multi-package payload of drones can show benefits under some conditions. However, the proposed method does not consider dynamic environmental factors such as wind.

Many research modeled the drone delivery problem using a service paradigm that abstracts routing into service composition under complex constraints. Shahzaad et al. [4] focused on the spatio-temporal aspects of drone delivery services and the dependencies between QoS attributes. They proposed a composition framework for drone-based package delivery services. A heuristic-based multi-armed bandit method was proposed to compose drone services in [21]. The authors considered drone recharging constraints and the stochastic arrival of drone services. Alkouz et al. [22] studied the swarm-based drone delivery problem and proposed a swarm-based DaaS (SDaaS) composition framework with in-flight energy recharging. They proposed an enhanced A\* (A-star) algorithm for service composition. Shahzaad et al. [23] focused on the recharging pad availability and the variations of wind to design a robust DaaS composition framework to minimize the delivery time. Shahzaad et al. [24] considered dynamic weather conditions and proposed a deterministic lookahead algorithm to build an initial offline composition plan. To adapt to the changes in service behavior, they developed a heuristic-based resilient service composition algorithm. Chu et al. [25] considered drone relay delivery and proposed a mobile edge computing based holistic service provisioning strategy for drone delivery. Hamdi et al. [26] focused on the uncertainty of weather and proposed a DaaS service model based on the dynamic spatio-temporal characteristics of drones. Shahzaad et al. [27] investigated the multi-package drone delivery problem under recharging constraints and proposed a new service-oriented architecture. However, the proposed method is not concerned with how DaaS services are shared among user requests.

Meanwhile, many studies have investigated different service selection and composition problems. Lima et al. [28] proposed a QoS-aware service selection method that considered service sharing. The method included a novel model for the deployment of multiple choreographies and considered the impact of service sharing on QoS. Zhang et al. Most existing research on drone routing and scheduling focuses on point-to-point delivery between warehouses and customers. Service-based approaches are limited to providing solutions for single-package delivery by drones. Meanwhile, both types of research overlook the impact of recharging and waiting under dynamic wind conditions on drone energy consumption. In this paper, we aim to provide an overall solution for multi-package delivery under dynamic wind conditions. Our focus is on finding the near optimal service sharing and service composition plans to minimize the energy consumption.

## B. Wind and Drone Energy Consumption Models

Wind is a major environmental factor affecting drone performance and flight behavior. The headwinds significantly increase the energy consumption of drones. Moreover, wind conditions change dynamically over time. Therefore, the development of service provisioning strategies needs to consider the impact of dynamic wind conditions. In real-world scenarios, wind data is often collected at fixed time intervals. We discretize the wind in the time dimension, assuming that the wind changes at every fixed time slot [17]. The wind conditions across the delivery network in the future t time slots are represented as a sequence $W = \{ W ^ { 0 } , W ^ { 1 } , \ldots , W ^ { t } \}$ $W ^ { i }$ represents the wind conditions in each region of the delivery network in the time slot i. $w _ { i j } ^ { t } =$ $( w _ { s } , w _ { d } ) \in W ^ { t }$ represents the wind conditions at edge $e _ { i j }$ in the time slot $t , w _ { s }$ and $w _ { d }$ are the wind speed and wind direction, respectively. The relative wind direction $w _ { o }$ is defined as the angle between the wind direction and the droneâs flight direction.

In practice, it is feasible to achieve minute-level predictions of short-term wind conditions [33]. We assume that the predicted wind conditions $W _ { p }$ across the delivery network in the future t time slots are known. The wind prediction is performed using historical data from sources like OpenWeatherMap [34]. All the drone delivery tasks can be completed within this timeframe. A drone may require several time slots to fly from the current node to an adjacent node. While this assumption holds for the current study, our future work will explore more robust methods. Due to the uncertainty of wind, the predicted wind conditions $W _ { p }$ may differ from the actual wind conditions W during the delivery process. Therefore, our strategy uses the predicted wind conditions for planning before the drone take-off, i.e., in the static phase. During the dynamic phase, the edge server detects the difference between $W _ { p }$ and W . Then, it executes real-time adjustments according to the impact caused by the changes in wind conditions.

## D. Problem Definition

The service provisioning problem is to use several types of drones to complete the delivery tasks and minimize the delivery energy consumption. According to the service model outlined in the preceding subsections, the optimization problem is formulated as follows:

$$
\operatorname* { m i n } \quad E = \sum _ { i = 1 } ^ { n } \sum _ { j = 1 } ^ { l e n ( P S _ { i } ) } \sum _ { D a a S \in C S _ { i } ( j ) } E _ { l o c _ { s } l o c _ { e } } ^ { t _ { s } }\tag{13}
$$

The decision variables of the optimization problem are SSP and SCP. The problem is difficult to be solved exactly because the actual wind conditions are unknown. Therefore, we solve the service sharing problem and the service composition problem sequentially to obtain a solution for the service provisioning problem. The service sharing problem refers to determining the SSP that minimizes the expected energy consumption for delivery, i.e., minimizing the ESSP (10). The service composition problem refers to determining the SCP based on the SSP to deliver all the packages. The optimization objective is to minimize the energy consumption of the drones, i.e., minimizing the $E _ { S C P }$ (12).

## IV. SERVICE PROVISIONING STRATEGY MW-DSP

This section first describes the MW-DSP strategy and then details the proposed ADS-GA algorithm and PIDC method.

MW-DSP strategy consists of two phases: service sharing and service composition. In the service sharing phase, the cloud server obtains the package information and then generates the

SSP through ADS-GA. The packages from the same station within the current time range will be divided into several subroutes. These packages are then delivered to their destinations by drones via a multi-package delivery plan. In the service composition phase, the edge server composes DaaS for the packages carried by the drone sequentially according to the SSP. The PIDC method is used to compose near optimal DaaS services. Whenever the drone arrives at a new delivery station, the edge server will determine either the path selection, the recharging decision, or the waiting decision.

## A. ADS-GA Algorithm

The overall process of ADS-GA is illustrated in Fig. 2, encompassing four steps: population initialization, selection, reproduction, and mutation. First, the algorithm generates an initial population representing a set of SSPs based on agglomerative clustering. Then, the population evolves within the search space to discover better approximate solutions until the stopping criterion is met. To balance the quality and the diversity of the population, ADS-GA employs truncation selection and linearrank selection. In the selection process, the algorithm retains individuals with superior fitness values and probabilistically eliminates the others. In the reproduction, ADS-GA incorporates neighborhood search to generate new individuals by searching the neighborhoods of good individuals. Where 50% of offspring are generated through crossover operation, while the remaining 50% are generated through neighborhood search. In the mutation, the algorithm performs mutation operation based on a directed mutation mechanism. The poorer gene fragments have a higher probability of mutation. Three types of mutation operators are utilized: exchange, insertion, and refactoring. The following subsections describe the encoding, population initialization method, and genetic operators in detail.

1) Encoding and Population Initialization: As shown in Fig. 2, a chromosome is a structured arrangement of the drone type identifier $D _ { i }$ and the packageâs id. Its fitness value is the expected energy consumption of the corresponding SSP. The drone type identifiers divide the chromosome into several gene fragments, each representing a sub-route. The total weight of the packages in each gene fragment cannot exceed the payload capacity of the drone.

The setting of the initial population has a significant impact on the search process of the genetic algorithm. A specific initial population can enhance the convergence speed of the algorithm [15]. To obtain a high-quality initial population, we formulate a package similarity measure and design a population initialization method based on agglomerative clustering. The similarity of package a and package b is calculated by the following equation:

$$
\displaystyle S i m ( a , b ) = \frac { L c s ( p a t h _ { a } , p a t h _ { b } ) } { D i s t ( d s t _ { a } , d s t _ { b } ) + 1 }\tag{14}
$$

where $p a t h _ { a }$ and $p a t h _ { b }$ are the shortest paths from the source to the destination of two packages, respectively. $L c s ( p a t h _ { a } , p a t h _ { b } )$ is the proportion of the common part of $p a t h _ { a }$ and $p a t h _ { b }$ $D i s t ( d s t _ { a } , d s t _ { b } )$ denotes the length of the shortest path between the destinations of two packages. The similarity of cluster $C _ { 1 }$ and cluster $C _ { 2 }$ is the average similarity of the packages within the clusters:

$$
S i m ( C _ { 1 } , C _ { 2 } ) = \frac { 1 } { \mid C _ { 1 } \mid \cdot \mid C _ { 2 } \mid } \sum _ { P _ { i } \in C _ { 1 } , P _ { j } \in C _ { 2 } } S i m ( P _ { i } , P _ { j } )\tag{15}
$$

The pseudo-code of the population initialization method based on agglomerative clustering is shown in Algorithm 1. It consists of three phases: aggregation (Lines 2-8), division (Lines 9-11), and population generation (Lines 12-22). The initialization operation of the algorithm involves creating an empty tree-like clustering structure tree, a list of clustering solutions cluster, and an initial population population (Line 1). In the aggregation phase, each package in $P$ is considered as an initial cluster, and a node is created in the tree for each cluster (Line 2). Then, the current clustering solution C and the similarity matrix M are initialized (Line 3). Finally, the two clusters with the highest similarity are merged into a cluster, followed by updates to C, M, and tree. The merging process continues until all packages are classified into one cluster (Lines 4-8). In the division phase, the tree obtained from the aggregation is divided at different levels to generate several clustering solutions (Lines 9-11). Function divideT ree performs hierarchical traversal of the tree in a top-down manner. When a node is found for which the number of packages in the cluster is not greater than num. The cluster corresponding to that node is incorporated into the clustering solution, then the node and its descendants are deleted. Function encode first randomly selects a type of drone, then iterates over the packages in cluster c and assigns the current package to that drone if it meets the payload limit. When a drone cannot add a new package, the drone is reselected until all packages have been assigned.

2) Selection and Reproduction: In selection, all individuals are sorted according to fitness values, and a fraction of individuals with high fitness values is retained. The remaining individuals are then determined to have a probability of being selected based on the order of sorting. The higher the fitness value of an individual, the higher the probability of being selected.

In the reproduction, the crossover operation and the neighborhood search each generates half of the offspring. The process of crossover operation is as follows. First, two chromosomes A and B are randomly selected from the population as the parent generation. Then, several gene fragments are randomly selected from chromosome A and directly retained in the offspring. Finally, the existing packages in the offspring are deleted from chromosome B, and the remaining portions are added to the offspring. The neighborhood search algorithm improves the current solution by searching its neighboring solutions. We integrate this method into the reproduction process to improve the ADS-GAâs local search capabilities. The process of generating offspring is as follows. First, it generates three neighborhood solutions by searching the neighborhoods of excellent individuals through three mutation operators. Then, compare the fitness values of the three solutions and add the solution with the highest fitness to the current population.

3) Directed Mutation: Mutation operators include exchange, insertion, and refactoring. After selecting the chromosomes to be mutated, the algorithm will randomly choose an operator. The exchange operator randomly exchanges two genes within a selected gene fragment. The insertion operator inserts a gene from a gene fragment into another gene fragment. If the insertion operation fails to meet the droneâs payload limit, the gene is inserted into an empty gene fragment. The refactoring operator follows these steps. First, it selects several gene fragments from the chromosome. The genes within these fragments are added to a list. Then, it removes the selected gene fragments from the chromosome and randomize the order of the list. Finally, it uses the function encode to generate the new gene fragments and add them to the chromosome.

Upon the mutation of a chromosome, standard mutation operations cannot differentiate between different gene fragments. They will apply the operator to several randomly selected gene fragments. To expedite the elimination of poor-quality gene fragments, we define the fitness value of the gene fragment and design a directed mutation mechanism. The directed mutation is employed solely during the initial phases of algorithmic iteration. During a mutation operation, gene fragments within a chromosome are sorted in descending order according to their fitness values. The probability of being altered is then determined based on the order of sorting. Gene fragments with low fitness value are more likely to be altered. The fitness value of the gene fragment is determined using the equation below:

$$
v a l u e = \frac { E _ { s } } { E _ { m } }\tag{16}
$$

where $E _ { s }$ represents the energy required by delivering the packages in the gene fragment via the single-package delivery approach, and $E _ { m }$ represents the expected energy consumption of the gene fragment.

## B. PIDC Method

PIDC comprehensively considers the impact of dynamic wind conditions, recharging, and waiting on the energy consumption of the drone. It consists of two phases: the static phase and the dynamic phase.

The static phase focuses on the planning before drone take-off, involving deterministic DaaS composition. We model the planning process in the static phase as a Markov Decision Process (MDP), using policy iteration to generate a policy. The policy is based on predicted wind conditions. It is used to make decisions such as path selection, recharging decision, and waiting decision for the drone during the delivery process. The MDP is defined as a 5-tuple $\langle S , A , D P , r , \gamma \rangle . \ S = \{ s _ { 1 } , s _ { 2 } , \ldots , s _ { n } \}$ is a finite set of states. $s _ { i } = \langle t , r e , c n \rangle$ is the combination of current time, remaining battery power of the drone, and current node. $A = \{ a _ { 1 } , a _ { 2 } , \ldots , a _ { n } \}$ is a finite set of actions, including flying to a certain adjacent station, recharge, and wait. The action space is variable, and the actions the drone can take depend on the current state of the drone. Assume that $A _ { i } \in A$ is the set of optional actions of the drone in state $s _ { i }$ and $N _ { i }$ represents the set of adjacent nodes of node $s _ { i } . c n$ . In the drone delivery scenario, the state transition function $D P ( s ^ { \prime } | s , a )$ is deterministic. Thus, let $T ( s , a )$ represent the new state arrived after state s performs action $a . r ( s , a )$ is the reward function. The reward obtained by the drone flying to adjacent nodes is set to a negative value for energy consumption. The reward for recharging or waiting is set to a fixed value. Î³ is the discount factor, which takes a value in the range of [0, 1]. The policy is denoted by $\pi , \pi ( a | s )$ represents the probability that the policy performs action a at state s. $V ^ { \pi } ( s )$ denotes the state-value function based on the policy Ï in the MDP.

The dynamic phase encompasses the period from the drone take off to the completion of package delivery, i.e., the service implementation phase. Due to wind uncertainty, the policy learned based on predicted wind conditions in the static phase may be ineffective. For example, the changes in wind cause the energy consumption of the selected DaaS service to increase or exceed the remaining battery power of the drone. We address this challenge by conducting dynamic detection during the delivery process. When changes in wind conditions influence the policyâs effectiveness, a near optimal adjacent node is selected based on $V ^ { \pi } ( s )$

The pseudo-code of PIDC is presented in Algorithm 2. The static and dynamic phases are executed at the cloud server and edge server, respectively. In the static phase, the cloud server obtains the policy $\pi ( s )$ and state-value function $V ( s )$ based on the predicted wind conditions by the policy iteration path planning algorithm (Algorithm 3) (Line 1). In the dynamic phase, the edge server first obtains current wind conditions and droneâs states. Then, it composes the near optimal drone service based on the output of the static phase. The specific process is as follows. Whenever the drone arrives at a station, the edge server selects a near optimal action $a _ { i }$ (Line 4) from the optional actions by $\pi ( s )$ . If action $a _ { i }$ is to recharge or wait, the algorithm executes the action and updates the droneâs state (Lines 5-6). If action $a _ { i }$ is to fly to an adjacent node, calculate the actual energy consumption of the drone according to current wind conditions (Line 8). Then, the edge server determines whether action $a _ { i }$ satisfies the flight conditions (Lines 9-10). If the flight conditions are satisfied, the selected service is added to the DaaS composition plan. Then, it executes action $a _ { i }$ and update droneâs state (Lines 11-12). If changes in wind conditions cause the policy to fail, the edge server initiates dynamic processing (Lines 14-20). First, a near optimal adjacent node is selected based on the current wind conditions and the $V ( s )$ (Line 14). Then, it is determined whether the energy required to fly to the near optimal node exceeds the remaining battery power of the drone. If the conditions are satisfied, the drone flies to the node (Lines 16-17). Otherwise, the drone recharges at the current node (Line 19). The process continues until the package reaches its destination. Finally, the algorithm returns a DaaS composition plan from the specified source to the destination (Line 24). The time complexity of PIDC depends on the policy iteration path planning algorithm of the static phase.

Fig. 6 shows the delivery time of the four methods. Our method exhibits the lowest delivery time, reduced by 13.3%, 12.6%, and 7.2% compared with other algorithms, respectively. The experimental results indicate that our method has a superior performance in terms of energy consumption and time.

To evaluate the robustness of PIDC, we investigate the impact of the accuracy of the predicted wind conditions on the effectiveness of the method. In our experiments, we generate several sets of predicted wind conditions by randomly modifying some of the actual wind conditions. The prediction accuracy represents the proportion of the predicted wind conditions that match the actual wind conditions. Fig. 7 shows the delivery energy consumption of the PIDC and SP-TAG where the X-axis represents the prediction accuracy and the Y-axis is the delivery energy consumption. Each final result is the average of the experimental results from 5 sets of predicted wind conditions. The delivery energy consumption of Shortest and $\mathbf { A } ^ { * }$ remains unaffected by the predicted wind conditions. Therefore, the smaller value of the two algorithms is represented by the red dashed line in the figure. As illustrated in the figure, the delivery energy consumption of PIDC and SP-TAG gradually increases when the accuracy decreases. Our method is consistently better than SP-TAG. When the accuracy drops below or equals 0.5, the delivery energy consumption of SP-TAG surpasses the optimal value of Shortest and $\mathbf { A } ^ { * }$ . However, even at an accuracy of

0.25, our method still yields comparable results to the other two algorithms. This is because the dynamic processing of PIDC can reduce the impact of changes in wind conditions on the energy consumption of drones.

## VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

In this paper, we investigated the multi-package drone delivery problem in an edge-based last-mile drone delivery system, with a special focus on the impact of dynamic wind conditions on the energy consumption of drones. In order to develop efficient service provisioning plans, we first established a spatio-temporal DaaS (Drone-as-a-Service) model based on service sharing. Then, we proposed a wind-aware DaaS service provisioning strategy for multi-package delivery (named MW-DSP) to minimize the delivery energy consumption. Meanwhile, we proposed an improved genetic algorithm (named ADS-GA) for service sharing and a policy iteration based DaaS composition method (named PIDC) to compose near optimal DaaS services. Comprehensive experimental results demonstrated that our strategy can effectively reduce the energy consumption of drones, and outperformed many other representative strategies. In our future work, we will explore the impact of robustness under uncertain wind conditions and availability of recharging equipment to relax the current assumptions. Meanwhile, we will investigate delivery service selection methods in resource-constrained environments and develop lightweight learning-based models for online service selection.
